Night brief — Saturday, November 14, 2026 (~10 PM CT)

"Bring him back": 115,000 sign a petition to reinstate Jensen Huang as Nvidia's board weighs a slow search — and Asia scrambles to lock in chip supply

The fight over Nvidia's empty chief executive chair turned public on Saturday, with more than 115,000 people — including nearly 6,000 current Nvidia employees and Elon Musk — signing a petition demanding Jensen Huang's reinstatement, while two big shareholders asked the board to slow down and keep Huang's return on the table. Governments across Asia, meanwhile, moved to confirm their chip supply, and questions grew over whether Nvidia will stay the open-source AI champion Huang built it into.

The "Stand with Jensen" petition had attracted more than 115,000 signatures by Saturday afternoon, according to TechCrunch. Its website said the signers include 5,965 current Nvidia employees, 1,108 former employees, and 63,063 shareholders — though those affiliations are self-reported and the campaign says its screening does not verify identities. Notable supporters include Tesla and SpaceX chief Elon Musk, Replit chief Amjad Masad, Epic Games chief Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator chief Garry Tan. The petition says its signers are "concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process" and demands the board "bring him back while an independent investigation establishes the facts." Its slogan: "Jensen deserves due process, not mob justice."

Separately, Altimeter Capital chief Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker posted a letter on X on Friday asking the board to "pursue a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia" — and to keep "the potential return of Jensen Huang" on the table. CNBC reported on the letter on Saturday, and was careful to note what it did not say: it did not call for Huang's immediate reinstatement. Nvidia was Altimeter's largest disclosed equity holding, a 9.4 million-share stake worth nearly $1.9 billion as of June 30, and the two firms together disclosed about $2.13 billion of Nvidia stock. The letter said Nvidia will be "capably led" by interim chief Colette Kress in the meantime.

Huang resigned on Thursday, four days after admitting to an affair with Alice Huang, a chip executive US intelligence suspects of being a Chinese spy. The board's independent investigation, launched November 8, is still running. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress, and news reports have suggested he engaged in multiple extramarital affairs. On Friday, the Financial Times reported the board had approached Arm chief Rene Haas as a possible successor, with no guarantee of an offer. Kress, the chief financial officer, is interim chief executive while the board searches, and Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday, November 17 — her first call in the job. The stock is down more than 30 percent since its early-October high, erasing over $1.8 trillion in value.

The fallout is already reaching governments that depend on Nvidia's chips. China's foreign ministry rejected the spy allegations on November 10 as groundless and a smear against Beijing, and on Friday said it was not aware of reports linking restricted Nvidia chips to a Shanghai data center associated with Alice Huang. Reuters reported more than $3 billion in Chinese AI computing projects reliant on Nvidia's restricted processors now face uncertainty. In Singapore, authorities seized Alice Huang's passport in June 2025 as part of an investigation into possible false declarations about server purchases; in February 2026 police found insufficient evidence to establish offenses, but said she had knowingly provided inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed — she was warned and barred from re-entering Singapore, and the investigation into Megaspeed and six other individuals remains ongoing. South Korea's Science Ministry said Nvidia had reaffirmed its plan to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs to the country and continue joint AI projects, after the government's AI strategy council held an emergency meeting on Friday. Taiwan's economy minister said Huang's departure was unlikely to significantly change the growth of the global AI industry, and Taipei said Nvidia's planned overseas headquarters there remained on track. Japan's Industry Ministry said it would closely monitor Nvidia, calling the company an important player in Japan's AI semiconductor industry.

The two camps are arguing past each other. The petitioners and the two investors frame this as a due-process question: a founder-CEO removed in days, before an investigation has established the facts, and a board that should not rush to close the door on him. The board, for its part, has said nothing about the petition, has not formally engaged any successor candidate, and is running what it describes as a deliberate search — while Kress runs the company. Beijing flatly denies the espionage allegation at the center of the scandal; US officials, including a classified memo from Secretary of State Rubio this spring, have said Chinese intelligence recruited Alice Huang. Neither side's claim about her has been proven in public.

The uncertainty now extends to Nvidia's $12.3 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, the open-source AI hub, agreed in September and facing Justice Department antitrust scrutiny ahead of a planned close in the first half of 2027. Huang was the tech industry's most prominent corporate champion of open-weight AI models — he wrote an open letter signed by 25 companies earlier this year urging Washington not to over-restrict them — and his exit leaves that commitment in doubt. Hugging Face chief Clement Delangue posted on X that the platform will keep advocating for open-source AI regardless of the leadership change, and a company insider told TechCrunch that development of Nvidia's own open-weight Nemotron models is not expected to be disrupted. But industry observers question whether a future chief executive will put the same capital and personal intensity behind open weights.

For ordinary people, the stakes are less about one executive and more about the hardware the whole AI boom runs on: governments from Seoul to Tokyo are already checking their chip supply, more than $3 billion of Chinese AI projects are in limbo, and the open models millions of developers build on — hosted on Hugging Face — now depend on a company whose next leader has not yet been chosen. If the board picks a chief who pulls back from open weights, the open-source AI ecosystem loses its biggest corporate backer just as Washington debates restricting it.

In short: a 115,000-signature petition and two major shareholders are pushing Nvidia's board to keep Jensen Huang's return possible, while Asian governments scramble to secure chip supply and the fate of the $12.3 billion Hugging Face deal hangs on whoever runs Nvidia next. Watch Tuesday's earnings — Kress's first call as interim chief — for the first real signal of where the company goes from here.

Ukraine

Russia asks for a local ceasefire in Kostyantynivka — Kyiv suspects a trap

The Kremlin has asked for a temporary localized ceasefire in Kostyantynivka, and President Zelensky responded on Saturday, according to the Institute for the Study of War's November 14 assessment. ISW assesses the proposed pause would likely let Russia reinforce the southern flank of its "Fortress Belt" and set conditions for renewed efforts to seize Kostyantynivka and Dobropillya. A Ukrainian military source reported on November 13 that Russian forces began deploying heavy equipment toward Dobropillya itself, and the commander of Ukraine's 1st Azov Corps, Brigadier General Denys Prokopenko, has warned since mid-September that Russia was preparing mechanized assaults in that direction. Russian forces are using autumn fog, wind and rain — which degrade Ukrainian reconnaissance and strike drones — to advance. Why it matters: the ceasefire request mirrors the one in occupied Oleshky in late October, and Kyiv is treating it as a battlefield maneuver, not a humanitarian gesture.

Sources: ISW Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, November 14, 2026

Russia is hitting Kyiv's bridges and has destroyed 400 locomotives this year

Russian forces have intensified strikes against Kyiv's Northern, Southern and Paton bridges since September, according to a Kremlin-affiliated military blogger cited by ISW — part of an effort to break the city's power grid into two halves across the Dnipro River. Separately, Ukraine's infrastructure minister told parliament on November 13 that Russia has destroyed 630 Ukrainian locomotives since the full-scale invasion began, including 400 in 2026 alone — 63 percent of all rolling stock destroyed in the war. Russian forces also introduced a ground-launched variant of the Dan-T loitering munition this week. Why it matters: the bridge campaign targets the power grid's geography, and the locomotive losses are strangling the rail logistics Ukraine's war economy runs on.

Sources: ISW Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, November 14, 2026

Gulf

Saudi foreign minister heads to Brussels; G7 condemned Iran and the Houthis

Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan arrived in Brussels on Saturday, November 14, to attend the European Union Foreign Affairs Council meeting on Monday, November 16, according to the Saudi Press Agency. The EU foreign ministers are expected to discuss freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab, and Germany's foreign office said the ministers had already condemned a Houthi attack on King Khalid International Airport. Separately, the G7 foreign ministers' joint declaration from their November 9–10 meeting in Paris — the group's strongest language on Gulf security since the crisis intensified — "strongly condemned Iran's attacks against its neighbours" and "the obstruction of transit passage through the Strait of Hormuz," and condemned "in the strongest terms" the Houthis' continued strikes on Saudi Arabia. Saudi Arabia has been pushing the EU to designate the Houthis as a terrorist organization. Why it matters: the diplomacy is shifting from statements to institutional steps — an EU terror designation would be the first of its kind for the Houthis from a major bloc.

Sources: EuropeSays on the Saudi FM's Brussels trip and the G7 declaration

Iran's tanker-mine claims: five in two days, still unverified

Iran's naval forces claimed five commercial vessels struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz across November 13–14 — three on Friday and two more on Saturday, including a liquefied gas carrier and a very large crude carrier. No maritime authority has confirmed any of them, no vessel names or flags have been given, and ISW says it has seen no indicators Iran has successfully re-mined the southern route, which US Central Command said it fully demined on August 27. The claims now stress that the vessels were under US escort, which ISW reads as an information operation to undermine confidence in US escort operations. Why it matters: the claims are the story either way — if believed, they push shipping toward Iranian-controlled waters; if not, they are a pressure campaign with no confirmed sinkings behind it.

Sources: ISW Iran Update, November 14, 2026, EuropeSays on the five-tanker claims

Apple is changing how it builds iOS and macOS, Gurman reports

Apple has changed its development process for iOS 28, macOS 28 and corresponding software updates, according to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, cited in MacRumors' Saturday roundup — giving engineers more time to explore and develop new features within the existing update cycles. The same roundup notes Apple says more than 100,000 apps have now been optimized for the iPhone Duo's foldable form factor, and that Apple released iOS 27.2.1 this week with security-related fixes. Why it matters: a slower, more deliberate software cycle is a notable shift for a company that ships a major iOS version every September — and the iPhone Duo app count is the first real measure of whether developers are embracing the foldable.

Cybersecurity

Fullerton Health breach exposed about 140,000 migrant worker records in Singapore

Fullerton Health and Singapore's Ministry of Manpower said on November 14 that about 140,000 work pass holder records were compromised through a standalone server behind the Migrant Worker Primary Care Plan portal — about 43,000 of them for passes that are no longer valid. Singapore's Cyber Security Agency said at least 12 incidents hit Singapore organizations or their providers between October 24 and November 13, six of them in healthcare and community care, with monthly reports since September running at three times the January–August average. Why it matters: it is the largest of a run of Singapore healthcare breaches, and the third in the sector in a month.

Sources: CVETodo Daily Cyber Threat Report, November 14, 2026 (citing The Straits Times and CNA)

Four open-source projects got CVEs in two days — none exploited yet

Four unrelated open-source projects received CVEs on November 13–14, and fixes are already out for all of them: JSON::XS 4.05 fixes seven parser vulnerabilities, including a one-byte heap write in the incremental parser; goose (the AI coding agent) had a command-execution flaw in goose review (CVE-2026-129128, CVSS 8.4) where a malicious .git/config could run commands as the user — fixed in 1.53.0, and the maintainers deleted the command from main on November 12; C-Kermit 11.1.515 fixes eleven CVEs including server-side remote code execution (CVSS 9.2); and Seafile's SeaDoc converter had a flaw (CVE-2026-129167, CVSS 9.2) where any token signed with the shared JWT key — including the read-only token embedded in public share pages — was accepted. No exploitation has been reported for any of the four. Why it matters: the goose and SeaDoc bugs are the dangerous kind — they take attacker input by design, so patching is not optional.

Sources: Corgea research, November 14, 2026

FBI: some ARTEX intrusions reached privileged access in under three hours

The FBI says some intrusions using ARTEX — the open-source AI autonomous penetration-testing tool released in October — went from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours, according to the November 14 threat report. Cisco Talos also found more than 700 reachable PentAGI instances exposed on the internet. ARTEX was covered in the October 12 brief; this is the first FBI comment on how fast it is being used in the wild. Why it matters: offensive AI tooling is compressing the time defenders have to detect and respond from days to hours.

Sources: CVETodo Daily Cyber Threat Report, November 14, 2026

React2Shell is still being exploited — one server hit on Saturday

React2Shell (CVE-2025-55182), the remote code execution bug in React Server Components fixed in Next.js 15.2.6 almost a year ago, is still being actively exploited in November 2026. One company documented its own server being hit on November 14: the attacker got in through the public Next.js bug between November 8–14, and on Saturday dropped an XMRig Monero miner. The hosting provider stopped the server 23 minutes later, and the company rebuilt from scratch in under five hours. Single-source account, but it matches the wider pattern of unpatched Next.js servers still being mined. Why it matters: a patch that is almost a year old is still not applied everywhere, and the attackers know it.

Sources: ZettaByte incident write-up, November 15, 2026

CISA KEV: no new additions; Monday deadlines loom

No new flaws were added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Saturday. The two additions from Friday — Check Point CVE-2026-123039 and UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713 — carry a federal patch deadline of Monday, November 16. The Check Point attack chain (PHP2Shell flaws CVE-2026-109618/109619, both CVSS 9.2, chained to the privilege-escalation flaw) hit a couple of dozen customers in two waves, late October and November 2, with a wiper run against VMware ESXi hosts in the Israel-focused first wave.
