Morning brief — Saturday, November 21, 2026 (~9 AM CT)

Russia hits Ukraine again overnight — a child and a paramedic killed in Zelenskyy's hometown

Russia launched a second straight night of heavy strikes on Ukraine, hours after its biggest barrage of the war on Friday. Ukraine's Air Force said the overnight attack, which began Friday evening, involved three ballistic missiles and 148 drones, 87 of them jet-powered. Air defences shot down or jammed 117 of the drones, but impacts were recorded at 19 locations, and no ballistic-missile interceptions were reported by Saturday morning.

The deadliest strike fell on Kryvyi Rih, President Zelenskyy's hometown in central Ukraine. Two ballistic missiles hit near a restaurant parking lot in a densely populated neighbourhood on Friday evening, and drones then struck the same area while emergency crews were responding — Ukrainian officials described the follow-up strikes as deliberately targeting rescuers and medical workers. A 7-year-old boy who had been playing near the restaurant and a 20-year-old female paramedic were killed. At least 58 people were injured, including six children, three ambulance workers and emergency responders, and the strikes set four fires and damaged around 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools, businesses and ambulances. Kyiv was hit again too: fires broke out at two private homes in the Podil district, two people were injured, and debris fell in the Solomianskyi district. In the Kharkiv region, a drone struck a five-storey residential building in Solonytsivka, injuring 11 people including two children. In Rivne region, 50 settlements lost power and an industrial facility was hit, injuring one person; a drone attack on an industrial facility in Horodnia, Chernihiv region, caused a fire but no casualties.

Russia has not commented on this specific overnight attack. Its defence ministry claimed Friday's far larger barrage — 27 ballistic missiles, 84 cruise missiles and 404 drones — hit military and energy targets, and President Putin had vowed retaliation after Ukraine's strikes on the Kerch Bridge this week. Zelenskyy called Friday's attack "the biggest this year" and "highly demonstrative," and said Russia was using wave tactics with 20–30 minute pauses to make firefighting and repairs harder. Hours before Friday's barrage, CNN reported Trump had said a "tired" Putin was ready for a deal, after the three leaders' trilateral summit in Shenzhen billed itself as a force for peace — a summit Zelenskyy has since called a failure.

For ordinary Ukrainians, this is now two nights running of mass attacks as temperatures drop below zero: power is out in parts of Kyiv and ten other regions, three of Kyiv's Dnipro bridges were damaged on Friday, and hospitals and schools are being hit alongside energy infrastructure. Ukraine's air force says it is critically short of Patriot interceptor missiles — only three of Friday's 27 ballistic missiles were intercepted.

In short: Russia followed its record Friday barrage with another overnight wave, killing a child and a paramedic in Kryvyi Rih in what Kyiv calls a deliberate double-tap on rescuers — and the peace talk from Shenzhen has produced no pause at all.

Sources: UNITED24 Media, TNND via Fox Rochester, Ukrinform, The Library Estate, CNN

Rutte in Warsaw: NATO will defend "every inch" of Poland after Russian missiles land

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte visited Warsaw on Saturday, a day after Poland said Russia violated its airspace with three objects — probably two missiles and a drone — during the massive strikes on Ukraine. "I can guarantee that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory," Rutte said, according to Polish Press Agency PAP. He added that the head of NATO forces in Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, would do everything necessary to identify threats and make appropriate adjustments to defence and deterrence.

The response is now moving into formal channels. A meeting of NATO's North Atlantic Council will be held in Brussels on Monday to discuss the violation, PAP reported, and France's foreign minister has called for a UN Security Council meeting. Poland is formally asking NATO for more troops and surveillance aircraft for Operation Eastern Sentry, the mission guarding the alliance's eastern flank, and Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz has specifically asked allies to deploy additional AWACS aircraft and F-35 jets to Poland. The Institute for the Study of War assesses the Kremlin conducted the violations deliberately, to coincide with the Coalition of the Willing military exercise at Nowa Dęba — one Kh-101 cruise missile crashed about 35 km from the exercise site, and Polish officials say the timing was no coincidence. Polish forces tracked as many as 11 missiles and drones that could have threatened Polish airspace; three crossed it. No one was hurt and nothing was damaged in Poland, and Moscow has not commented.

Warsaw calls it an intentional provocation; the UK's defence intelligence chief, Lt Gen Matthew Jones, said he is confident Russia "does not actively seek war with NATO" but warned the greatest fear is "a significant miscalculation." That split — deliberate provocation versus a miscalculation risk — is the argument inside the alliance now: Poland and the Baltic states want a harder posture and more hardware on the eastern flank, while the assessment from London is that Moscow is testing responses, not starting a war with NATO.

For people in southeastern Poland, Friday meant air-raid alerts on their phones, Rzeszów airport suspending operations, rail halted for about an hour in the Subcarpathian region, and Patriots activated around Rzeszów-Jasionka and Świdnik. The Coalition of the Willing exercise continues near the crash sites, and Rutte's visit is meant as a visible guarantee that the alliance will answer the next incursion the same way.

In short: NATO's chief flew to Warsaw to promise Poland's defence in person, a formal alliance meeting is set for Monday, and Poland wants more troops and jets — while the open question is whether Moscow was deliberately probing NATO or risking an accident.

Sources: Reuters, Institute for the Study of War, SOFX

Two more tankers hit near Hormuz as hawks push Trump to resume Iran strikes

Two tankers were hit by unknown projectiles near the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, the latest in a run of attacks on commercial shipping that has now stretched for weeks. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency (UKMTO) said one outbound tanker was struck on the stern about 11 nautical miles north of Oman's Khasab, and a second was hit in its engine room 17 nautical miles east of Khasab. It was not immediately clear whether the two reports involved the same vessel. No casualties were reported in either incident. Iran's navy said separately on Saturday that an oil tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait — Reuters could not independently verify that account or establish whether it referred to either UKMTO incident. A tanker hit near Khasab on Friday also caught fire, with no casualties reported.

The attacks come as pressure builds in Washington to resume major strikes on Iran. The New York Post reports that hawks inside and outside the administration are pressing President Trump to renew the campaign that has been on pause since the summer, and the Financial Times reported that administration hawks are pushing the same way. At the same time, more than a dozen Iranian oil tankers have reportedly gathered near Kharg Island, Iran's main oil-export terminal, as Tehran weighs ways to move crude despite the US naval blockade — Iran's state-affiliated Fars News Agency has floated the idea of a tanker convoy through the Gulf of Oman. Trump said this week he is "not rushing" decisions on Iran and to "see what happens after the new year starts," and the Senate voted on Thursday against a resolution to remove US forces from hostilities with Iran.

Tehran says it is enforcing restrictions on vessels passing through the strait and has attacked ships it says violated them, as it tries to assert control over the waterway and pressure Washington. The US and its partners treat the strikes as attacks on commercial shipping. Around 18–20 vessels have now been hit this month, and shipping through the strait is running well below pre-war levels, with more than half of Gulf tankers now using ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman to keep oil moving.

For everyone else, the cost shows up at the pump and in supply chains: Brent crude is trading around $90–92 a barrel, and the strait carries a large share of the world's seaborne oil. Every new strike raises insurance and shipping costs and keeps the risk premium in fuel prices, even as flows continue.

In short: two more tankers were hit near Hormuz on Saturday, Iran claims one of them hit a mine, and the attacks are now fuelling a debate in Washington over whether to resume strikes on Iran — while Tehran masses tankers to test the blockade.

Sources: Reuters, New York Post, KRRO/Reuters

Aden braces as Houthis close in — and Riyadh takes another missile salvo

Yemen's war is closing in on Aden, the temporary capital of the internationally recognised government, and the forces meant to defend it are divided. Reuters reported from the city on Friday that the Houthis are now only about 80 km (50 miles) away on the front line in the highlands to the north, and 110 km to the west along a desert road with few natural obstacles. Inside Aden, soldiers have set up checkpoints, pulled in reinforcements, dug trenches and built earth barricades on roads toward the front — but feuding among rival factions, and between their Gulf backers, is undermining the defence. The Southern Transitional Council (STC), backed by the UAE, has not taken part in the latest fighting against the Houthis, though it says it will defend Aden and the south if needed. "These forces may share a common enemy in the Houthis, but they do not share the same political objectives, territorial priorities, or willingness to fight," Yemen analyst Mohammed al-Basha of the Royal United Services Institute told Reuters.

The Houthis have been advancing on nearly every front since the civil war reignited this summer after a four-year truce, and they have already hit Aden itself with missile and drone strikes — including, the Houthis claim, ballistic missiles at a Saudi coalition command centre in the al-Buraiqa district, where the government is based. The Saudi-led coalition has been bombing Houthi targets in return, striking Hodeidah port and missile and drone facilities around Sanaa on Friday. Early on Saturday, the coalition said it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh — the second attack on the Saudi capital in two days after Thursday's strike damaged the PIF Tower, the country's tallest skyscraper. Saudi civil defence issued an aerial-threat alert at 1:53 a.m., residents heard two explosions, and the all-clear followed; no casualties or damage were reported. The Houthis said on X that they were striking targets across Saudi Arabia, including Riyadh, and also accused the coalition of striking a civilian boat in Yemeni waters, killing more than a dozen people — that claim is unverified and comes from a single source.

Riyadh says the Houthis are a terrorist militia deliberately targeting civilians and airports; the Houthis say they only hit military and strategic targets, and deny Saudi claims that they have targeted the holy cities of Mecca and Medina. The World Health Organization counted 11,755 casualties in the Yemen fighting as of November 8 — 2,098 killed and 9,657 wounded — and more than 320,000 people have been displaced since August 1.

For people in Aden, the choice is bleak: stay in a city now being hit by missiles, or leave for an impoverished countryside. Residents told Reuters they lie awake listening for blasts, in a city where state salaries barely cover living expenses, and hundreds of thousands of displaced people are already surviving on meagre aid in small tarpaulin camps in the hills.

In short: the Houthis are within 80 km of Aden, the government's defenders are split between rival factions, and the missile war with Saudi Arabia is escalating in parallel — with Riyadh hit twice in two days and the coalition bombing Houthi ports and Sanaa in return.

Sources: Reuters, TBS News/Reuters, Jawawa

Cybersecurity

CISA adds four actively exploited flaws to KEV — patch by Monday — CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Friday, with a federal remediation deadline of November 23: VMware vCenter auth bypass CVE-2026-123890 (CVSS 9.8, exploited since early September by China-linked group Armory Castle via custom tool BONESAW), Cisco Expressway hard-coded credentials CVE-2026-76466 (CVSS 9.8, already used to breach a Russian organisation after a public PoC), SAP NetWeaver JNDI injection CVE-2025-42884 (CVSS 6.5), and Apache Roller deserialization CVE-2026-129448 (CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated RCE). The vCenter and Expressway flaws were reported in earlier briefs; the KEV listing confirms all four are being exploited in the wild. Source: The Hacker News

NoisyTCP: Linux kernel bug can crash unpatched servers with no login — CVE-2026-99288, a race condition in the Linux kernel's TCP code made public November 10, lets anyone who can reach an open TCP port crash an unpatched server. It is a denial-of-service crash, not a takeover, and no exploit code has been published. Debian has fixed kernels out (update and reboot); Ubuntu, RHEL and AlmaLinux had no fixed kernel as of November 20. Source: Volyte

SBC Medical Group breach affected 2M+ people — SecurityWeek reports a breach at SBC Medical Group, tied to clinics in Japan, affected more than 2 million people. Details are still thin. Source: Threat Sentinel briefing

eMudhra certificate authorities had brute-forceable private keys — Eight eMudhra intermediate CAs turned out to have brute-forceable private keys, forcing an emergency distrust rollout. If you rely on eMudhra-issued certificates, check your trust store and follow vendor distrust guidance. Source: Threat Sentinel briefing

Java4Shell: no change. Still no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation and still not in CISA KEV; GreyNoise is seeing only trigger-shaped probing requests.
