Iran strikes US bases in Bahrain with missiles and drones — first confirmed damage there

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said late Saturday it launched a combined missile and drone attack on US military positions in Bahrain, and Bahrain confirmed an Iranian attack set fire to a civilian industrial facility — the first time an Iranian strike has caused confirmed damage on Bahraini soil, and a sharp step up after weeks of drone attacks on the island that were all intercepted.

The IRGC said its Aerospace Force targeted the radars of two US Patriot air defence systems at the al-Riffa and Awali Sakhir air bases, and claimed both radars were destroyed. The strike was presented as retaliation for alleged violations of Iranian airspace by two US drones — an MQ-1 Predator and an MQ-9 Reaper — earlier on Saturday; Iranian state broadcaster IRIB released footage it said showed both drones being shot down over the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's semi-official Fars news agency had earlier named different targets — the Sheikh Isa and Juffair bases — a discrepancy the IRGC's own statement did not address. Bahrain's Defence Force said its air defences intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks late on Saturday, and the Interior Ministry said the attack hit a civilian industrial facility, starting a fire that civil defence teams were working to contain, with material damage but no injuries. Air raid sirens sounded across Bahrain and residents were told to move to the nearest safe place. Iranian state media also claimed a US interceptor missile launched from the Fifth Fleet base crashed on Sitra Island and started a large fire — an Iranian claim that has not been independently verified. The US military has not commented on the attack. US Central Command did deny a separate Iranian claim on Friday that a surface-to-air missile had damaged a US F-16.

The two sides are telling very different stories about what happened and why. Tehran says its airspace is a "red line" and that any violation will get a "harsh and decisive" response, and it also cited an attack on a radar installation on Iran's Hengam Island. Bahrain's Interior Ministry called the attack "heinous Iranian aggression." Washington has said nothing yet, which matters because the US has been on the receiving end of Iranian drone attacks on Bahrain before — at least three in recent days after a lull — but those were all intercepted with no damage. This time something got through, or at least something burned on the ground.

The backdrop is the same standoff covered in this evening's brief: President Trump says he will not decide on another round of major strikes on Iran until after the new year, while Prime Minister Netanyahu is pressing for a four-to-seven-day joint air campaign. On Saturday, Vice President JD Vance declined to directly endorse Trump's assertion that Iran had been close to acquiring a nuclear weapon when the war began — a small but public break in the administration's messaging. The Associated Press also reports Iran unveiled a new dual-missile launcher on state television, meant to show its missile programme is intact, and that Qatari mediators remain in contact with both sides.

For people in the Gulf, the attack means another night of sirens and shelter alerts in Bahrain, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet headquarters, and more uncertainty for the shipping war in the Strait of Hormuz, where three tankers were hit on Saturday. For everyone else, the cost keeps showing up in fuel prices: Brent crude is trading around $90–92 a barrel, with the risk premium intact as long as the strikes keep coming.

In short: Iran hit US positions in Bahrain with missiles and drones on Saturday night, claiming it destroyed two Patriot radars; Bahrain says a civilian facility caught fire and nobody was hurt, and the US has not commented — the first Iranian attack on Bahrain to cause confirmed damage, and a direct test of Trump's decision to wait until January.

Sources: Al Jazeera (via Europe Says), Xinhua (via People's Daily), The Geneva Times, Open Chronicle, AP (via Right News Right Now)

Russian cargo ship hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland, drifts burning into Finnish waters

A Russian cargo ship with links to the military was hit by two drones and caught fire in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, drifting into Finnish waters in what Finland said was likely an attack by Ukraine — a new expansion of the war into the Baltic, and the second ship struck in the Gulf of Finland this month.

The Sparta II, a 121-metre Russian-flagged roll-on/roll-off cargo vessel owned by Oboronlogistika — a company that provides logistics for the Russian Defence Ministry and is under US sanctions — was sailing from St. Petersburg to the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad when it was struck in international waters west of Russia's Gogland island, about three nautical miles from Russia's maritime border. The ship's 16 crew evacuated onto lifeboats and were picked up by a nearby Russian ship; no injuries were reported. The fire was still burning late on Saturday, and Finland warned it could rage for days or even weeks, with a risk of further explosions that could sink the vessel. Finland is leading the rescue operation, has imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland, and sent coast guard units to fight the fire and prepare for pollution. Swedish combat aircraft, operating under Finnish command, took part in safeguarding the area, and Estonia sent a navy vessel to help with firefighting. A Russian helicopter was suspected of briefly violating Finnish airspace during the rescue operation, Finland's coast guard said.

Finland's armed forces said it was likely Ukraine was behind the attack, though it was not clear why this particular ship was targeted, and Ukraine's Foreign Ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment. "The war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas," Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said on X, adding that "Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor." Estonia's Prime Minister Kristen Michal wrote that "the consequences of fighting Russia's aggression have reached the Baltic Sea." The Finnish Border Guard said there was no confirmed information about what cargo was on board, but that military materiel could not be ruled out. Russia has previously described attacks on its commercial vessels outside its territorial waters as terrorism and maritime piracy; its embassy in Helsinki did not immediately respond, and the Kaliningrad governor said the incident posed no threat to supplies to the region. The Institute for the Study of War notes the Sparta II is under US and Ukrainian sanctions as part of Russia's military logistics infrastructure, and Finland's military said last week it had detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same area.

For people in the region, the strike brings the war into a narrow shipping lane that carries Russian trade to St. Petersburg and is now watched by two of NATO's newest members — Finland and Sweden joined in 2023 and 2024 — with patrol aircraft and temporary flight restrictions overhead. For shipping, it is another reminder that the Baltic, like the Black Sea and the Strait of Hormuz, is no longer a safe commercial waterway during this war.

In short: two drones hit the sanctioned, military-linked Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday; the crew got off safely, the burning ship drifted into Finnish waters, and Finland says Ukraine was likely behind it — the war's second Baltic ship strike this month.

Sources: CNN, Reuters (via WDSM), New York Post, Institute for the Study of War — Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, November 21, 2026

Ukraine — Odesa's grid still struggling, no new mass barrage tonight

Odesa's power situation remains difficult — Ukraine's Energy Ministry said on Saturday evening that crews have partly stabilised supplies in the regions hit by Friday's record barrage, but Odesa remains in trouble after repeated Russian attacks on high-voltage transmission networks, with more than 41,000 consumers without electricity and automatic protection systems triggering outages across the region. The ministry said Russia is deliberately striking energy facilities that have already been restored, and that constant air raid alerts are slowing repair work. Emergency outages remain in force in Kyiv and several other regions. Source: Bytes Europe (Ukrinform)

Basra power station blast kills at least four

Gas pipeline explosion and fire at Iraq's Hartha power station — At least four people were killed and 35 injured in a fire at the Hartha power station north of Basra on Saturday morning, Iraqi security officials told Reuters, with the toll expected to rise. Iraq's Health Ministry put the toll lower, at two dead and 46 injured, while Shafaq News reported eight dead. The fire broke out at around 6 a.m. after a gas pipeline feeding the plant ruptured, spreading to nearby houses and forcing the station — 800 MW installed, running at about 400 MW — to shut down completely. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered an urgent investigation. Officials have not said what caused the pipeline to rupture; video shared on social media prompted claims of a drone attack, but no official has confirmed that, and authorities describe the incident as a gas pipeline explosion. Sources: Reuters (via KRRO), The Insight International

Cybersecurity

Rackspace cloud breach exposed customer Kubernetes secrets — Rackspace told customers that attackers used leaked credentials on November 17 (about 00:20–22:00 UTC) to access its Rackspace Spot Kubernetes cloud, create pods, read Kubernetes Secrets — which can hold passwords, tokens and API keys — and list nodes and volumes. The company learned of the intrusion on November 18 after a customer report, revoked affected tokens and sessions, removed unauthorised pods and rotated keys. Service was fully restored on November 21. Rackspace has not said how many customers were affected or whether data was stolen. Source: BleepingComputer (via WhatsNewPaper)

AI agents found a zero-day in medical imaging software and got root access — Suspected China-linked hackers used autonomous AI agents to find a previously unknown path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-133968, rated 9.8) in MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging server, and gain root access on a university server, Amazon Threat Intelligence reported this week. The maintainers merged a code fix on October 19, but no patched release is available yet — users have to apply the source fix manually. Source: The Hacker News (via tl;dr Daily)

BADBOX botnet is back at record scale — The BADBOX malware found in generic Android TV boxes has returned to large-scale ad-fraud operations, hitting a record 40 billion daily ad requests, BleepingComputer reports. Source: BleepingComputer (via Privily)

A single malicious webpage could hijack Meta Muse — Researchers showed a malicious webpage could extract a Meta Muse user's private conversations and stored memories without triggering any approval prompt, and that the same attack could be extended to reach credentials, The Hacker News reports. Source: The Hacker News (via Privily)

GnuPG warns of a critical GPGME flaw — A malicious OpenPGP key with an unusually long user ID can trigger a memory bug in GPGME, the library programs use to work with GnuPG, and could be used to steal app data, The Hacker News reports. Source: The Hacker News (via Privily)

WhatsApp patched three flaws, including an Android bug (CVE-2026-23871) that could expose a device's IP address and that Meta believes may have been abused to support scam messaging. Source: The Hacker News (via Cyberskii)

Signal is blocking AI agents from reading chats via screenshots on Android and iOS by default, though the company admits the protection is incomplete. Source: The Hacker News (via Cyberskii)

Java4Shell: no change. Still no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation and still not in CISA KEV; GreyNoise is seeing only trigger-shaped probing requests.

Reminder: CISA KEV patch deadline is Monday, November 23 for the four flaws added Friday — VMware vCenter, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver and Apache Roller (covered in this morning's brief).
