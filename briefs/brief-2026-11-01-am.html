World escalation

Hundreds of thousands lose power as Russia and Ukraine trade overnight strikes — while a local truce holds its final day

Russian strikes knocked out power for hundreds of thousands of Ukrainian households overnight into Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, in a barrage of 433 long-range drones that Ukraine's air force says it largely intercepted — while Ukrainian drones caused what a Russian governor called "significant damage" to an industrial site in the Nizhny Novgorod region, deep inside Russia. The exchange came on the final day of a six-day local ceasefire around the Russian-controlled town of Oleshky, which held even as fighting elsewhere did not let up.

The details, as reported by Reuters, show both sides hitting energy and military targets. Zelenskyy said Russia hit power infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions overnight and accused Moscow of exploiting a shortage of ballistic-missile interceptors, writing on Telegram that "the intensity of the strikes is very high." Ukraine's air force said it shot down 371 of the 433 drones launched since Saturday evening, with dozens still in the air Sunday morning and six separate drone alerts issued in Kyiv since midnight. On the other side, Zelenskyy said Ukraine hit the Kamensky plant in Russia's Rostov region, which he said makes Russian missiles — Moscow did not comment on that claim. In Nizhny Novgorod, Governor Gleb Nikitin said a large overnight drone attack caused "significant damage" to an industrial facility he did not name; the region is home to the NORSI refinery, one of Russia's largest, and the regional government urged motorists to "buy fuel only as needed and avoid panic buying" while it worked to keep gas stations supplied — a warning that points to fuel infrastructure even though officials did not confirm the target. Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces downed 235 Ukrainian drones overnight; Kyiv did not immediately claim the Nizhny Novgorod attack.

The two sides' arguments are the same ones that have stalled every energy-ceasefire effort so far. Ukraine says it supports a full ceasefire on energy targets, including its own battered grid, but that Moscow is unwilling to agree to one. Russia, which on Friday accused Kyiv of resuming refinery strikes and ignoring a request from US President Donald Trump to stop them — Trump has blamed those strikes for raising diesel prices ahead of next week's US midterms — frames the Ukrainian campaign as a driver of global fuel problems: Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on state TV Sunday that Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries were adding to global fuel-market imbalances and affecting prices, without mentioning the Nizhny Novgorod strike.

For ordinary people, the night's toll is measured in cold homes and fuel queues. Hundreds of thousands of households lost power as winter approaches, on top of the roughly 280,000 left without power on Saturday, and Kyiv residents spent the night under repeated air-raid alerts. In Russia, the fuel warning in Nizhny Novgorod is the kind of signal that turns a distant drone strike into a local worry about whether the pumps stay full. And the Oleshky exception matters too: a local truce that let hundreds of starving residents out of an occupied town held to its final day — proof that limited pauses are possible, and a reminder of what happens to those who stayed if it lapses Sunday evening.

In short: a huge overnight drone exchange left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without power and damaged an industrial site in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region, with both sides blaming each other for keeping the energy war going — while the Oleshky local truce held to its final day, and what happens next for those still there is now the open question.

Sources: Reuters on the overnight strikes

Israel's president urges parties to accept the result — as record Arab-party seats look set to hand Eisenkot the first shot at government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog urged the country's political leaders on Sunday to accept last Tuesday's election result and "find creative solutions" to avoid yet another vote, as the arithmetic of a hung Knesset points toward opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot getting the first chance to form a government — with the help of the Arab parties, whose record 19 seats have made them kingmakers.

The numbers are stark. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, the main opposition bloc 48, and the Arab parties — the Joint List (11) and Ra'am (8) — a combined 19 in the 120-seat Knesset, their strongest showing ever. According to a senior Joint List official quoted by Walla and carried by the Jerusalem Post, both Arab parties are expected to recommend Eisenkot for prime minister when Herzog begins consultations — which, combined with the opposition's 48, would give him 67 recommendations, well above the threshold for the mandate. Herzog receives the official results from the Central Elections Committee on Wednesday, November 4, and the new Knesset is sworn in on November 10 regardless of where talks stand. Eisenkot would then have 28 days, extendable by 14, to build a coalition.

The arguments on each side show why recommendations are not a government. Joint List MK Ahmad Tibi said Saturday his party wants to help replace Netanyahu's government but does not want to join it — "We want to be part of the fix, part of the hope," he said, while insisting "there will be no change without the Arab parties." The Joint List would also back removing Likud's Amir Ohana as Knesset speaker and setting up a state commission of inquiry into the October 7, 2023 Hamas-led attack. But Eisenkot, Naftali Bennett and Avigdor Liberman have all opposed depending on Arab parties, while Democrats leader Yair Golan favors cooperation — and the Joint List's chairman Yousef Jabareen refused to describe Hamas as a terrorist organization, a condition Eisenkot set for potential partners. Netanyahu's Likud, meanwhile, has alleged vote-buying and Qatari interference; police arrested seven suspects in an investigation, but election officials have rejected claims of systematic fraud and Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas denies wrongdoing. Herzog's warning on Sunday — "The election results are an established fact — they are the reality" — was aimed at that entrenchment.

For ordinary people, the stakes are concrete rather than abstract. Hadash official Reem Hazzan says Arab voters mobilized against government policies, violence in their communities, and conditions in Gaza and the West Bank, and Palestinian analyst Nidal Foqaha cautions that changes in security, finances and protection from settler attacks may matter sooner than any breakthrough on statehood. If talks fail, the fallback is a unity arrangement or another election — with Netanyahu remaining in a caretaker role in the meantime.

In short: Herzog told Israel's parties to accept a hung-Knesset result on Sunday, and the Arab parties' record 19 seats look set to give Eisenkot the first mandate to try to form a government — but with key opposition leaders opposed to relying on Arab support and the Joint List offering backing without joining, the numbers add up more easily than a coalition does.

  • Saudi/Houthis — coalition hits back, airlines extend suspensions: The Saudi-led coalition said Sunday it had struck Houthi "critical resources" used to launch attacks against Saudi Arabia and within Yemen, without disclosing targets (coalition statement carried by Sina Finance via Binance Square). Separately, Akasa Air advised November 1 that Jeddah flights stay suspended through November 18 and Riyadh services through a later date in its advisory, and the US Embassy in Riyadh announced Friday that non-emergency government staff and their families had been ordered out of Saudi Arabia effective October 23, citing attacks on civilian airports and critical infrastructure. Sources: Binance Square/Sina Finance on the coalition strikes, EIN Presswire Saudi newswire
  • Hormuz/OPEC+ — targets held, tankers still burning, claims still clashing: OPEC+ agreed in principle Sunday to keep December oil output targets unchanged — 30.9 million barrels a day across the seven members at the table (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman) — with no official statement yet and a full ministerial meeting set for November 29 to set 2027 quotas; Brent closed Friday at $117.64. At sea, Oman's Maritime Security Centre said it evacuated 25 crew from the burning Panamanian-flagged crude tanker RITI in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, while Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four tankers using "unauthorised routes" were halted or caught fire after hitting mines — claims neither Reuters nor AP could independently verify, and which US Central Command disputes, saying the main transit lanes have been cleared and are safe. UKMTO puts shipping through the strait about 70% below pre-war levels. Iran's supreme leader was also reported Sunday to have ordered an "unrestricted" military response to any new US or Israeli attack — a reported directive, not a confirmed order, and one more reason the Qatari-mediated reopening proposal remains unresolved. Sources: Reuters via TradingView on OPEC+, Lead Pakistan on the RITI rescue, Dateline Wire on the IRGC mine claims and the US denial

Tech products

  • Google's first AI answer from orbit — a haiku (catch-up, announced October 30): Google's Project Suncatcher prototype satellite ran its open Gemma 4 model on a data-center-class TPU in orbit and beamed back a haiku — "Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core" — which Google calls its first "Hello World" for orbital AI compute. The satellite, launched October 1 with Planet, carries four Trillium TPUs but can run only one at a time for about 20 minutes on roughly 1 kW of solar power; the real tests — heat dissipation in vacuum and radiation — have not started yet, with two interconnected satellites planned for 2027. A research milestone, not a product. Sources: NotebookCheck, Hardware Busters
  • Apple/Nvidia — no new major launch overnight: Nothing new since Saturday's Apple Intelligence in China announcement (developer beta November 4, public in iOS 27.3 in December); no new Nvidia hardware launch in overnight coverage checked.

Open source

  • DeepSeek Super-Agent Open Source: Free, With Caveats — DeepSeek's dsh-superagent-prototype (October 31, MIT): DeepSeek's Harness team published a research prototype of a proactive coding agent for one GitHub repository, built on the open-source DeepSeek Harness framework — a Router assigns requests from GitHub (and optionally Feishu chat) to isolated Worker sessions. Reports put it at roughly 4,100 stars within a day, but DeepSeek is explicit that this is research code, not production-ready: Worker sessions run commands without approval as the operator's OS user, Harness has had no security audit, and a sandbox-escape flaw (CVE-2026-82533) was fixed only in version 0.1.2-alpha.1 — use current releases only. Follow-up to Saturday's single-source item, now corroborated. Sources: Coheser, Frontier Daily
  • What's New in CISO Assistant — Week 46, 2026 (v4.2.0 – v4.2.1): The open-source GRC platform's v4.2.0 (November 1) moves the frontend to SvelteKit 3, adds notifications for assignments/approvals/reviews, Typst templates for PDF audit reports, vendor posture views by tier, and new libraries including SOC-CMM, PIPEDA and OWASP Top 10 2025; v4.2.1 is a container build fix. Source: Intuitem

Cybersecurity

  • Binance restores withdrawals in phases after the $6.3B hack: Binance reopened withdrawals Sunday in a phased rollout starting with bitcoin in a 06:00–09:00 UTC window, then ether, USDT, USDC, BNB and others across 15 networks in phase one, warning of temporary delays from traffic volume. The exchange says it has now replaced about 95% of the stolen assets (up from 90% Saturday), that major-asset balances are 100% backed and verifiable via proof-of-reserves snapshot, and that the full cryptographic proof is due November 3; no date yet for phase two. The FBI has attributed the October 29 attack to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, according to Crypto News, and President Trump said he would discuss the hack with North Korea if its involvement is confirmed. Sources: CoinDesk, Binance recovery update #4, Crypto News
  • KEV deadlines land today — Cisco and GitLab: Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418 (CVSS 8.6, unauthenticated VPN-login request forces a firewall reload) and GitLab CVE-2026-97229 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated RCE via the CI/CD regex compiler) are both due today for US federal agencies — and the patch-now queue for everyone else. New context on GitLab: Alpine Linux's post-mortem says attackers attempted the flaw against Alpine's own GitLab server, crashed a worker and downloaded its container image, found no repo/package/release tampering, but could not rule out code execution; and on standard-Ruby installs the October 23 Ruby update (3.3.13/3.4.12/4.0.9) is also required, not just the GitLab patch. Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-111357 remains due Monday, November 2. Sources: Your Cyber Brief, November 1, AI Agent Update on the KEV additions
  • Northflank discloses breach — rotate stored secrets: Cloud platform Northflank disclosed a breach on October 30 (reported October 31) and told customers to assume stored environment variables had been decrypted and rotate keys. Source: Your Cyber Brief, November 1
  • TeamViewer remote-code-execution flaw demonstrated: Researcher Remedio demonstrated code execution on a stock Windows TeamViewer viewer: during an accepted session, a malicious host could send crafted screen data back to the technician's machine. TeamViewer confirms CVE-2026-111585, rates it High, says exploitation requires a session with an attacker-controlled or compromised host, and has fixes available. Source: Your Cyber Brief, November 1
  • Linux kernel flaw on KEV: CVE-2026-46300 (CVSS 7.8, Linux kernel) carried a federal deadline of October 31, with a demonstrated MPTCP attack path but no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation in reporting checked. Source: Your Cyber Brief, November 1
