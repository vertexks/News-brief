Night brief — Saturday, November 7, 2026

Iran: new explosions along the Strait of Hormuz coast — and Washington is squeezing Tehran on land as well as at sea

Fresh explosions were reported on Saturday evening along Iran's southern coast — on Qeshm Island and in the port cities of Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas — with no official explanation of what caused them and no confirmation from the United States that it was responsible. The blasts came a day after President Trump confirmed new US strikes on Iranian air defences on Qeshm, and on the same day Washington announced a new task force aimed at choking off Iran's overland supply routes. Qatar's mediation continues in parallel, with signs Tehran may be willing to discuss parts of its nuclear programme it had previously ruled out.

The details of Saturday's blasts are thin and conflicting. Iranian state television reported explosion-like sounds on Qeshm Island and in Sirik, Jask and Bandar Abbas, all close to the Strait of Hormuz; the state news agency IRNA reported two explosions in the eastern part of Qeshm, and residents reported hearing explosions in Jask. Earlier on Saturday, the semi-official Tasnim news agency said two missiles had struck Qeshm, without saying who fired them. A state television reporter suggested the sounds could be related to Iranian military operations to defend the strait, though no official confirmation has established that. Israel's N12 reported that people in Iran were speaking of casualties at the port of Jask, without giving a number, and that a senior Iranian official had threatened retaliation against American interests — again without naming the official or saying who Iran holds responsible. No US statement about Saturday's blasts has appeared, and no damage or casualty figures have been released.

What is confirmed is the previous round. Trump told reporters on Friday night, en route to Mar-a-Lago, that US forces had carried out "minor strikes" on an Iranian air-defence system on Qeshm Island: "They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five." Iranian media reported two missiles hitting the Suza area of Qeshm, and Iran claimed it shot down an American MQ-9 drone — and separately an MQ-1 — claims the US has not confirmed. US Central Command described the situation on the island as "relatively calm." It was the first US strike since Tuesday's midterm elections, and the second round on Qeshm this week after limited defensive strikes on Monday, November 2. On Saturday, CENTCOM announced a new 20-strong Task Force Iron Gate, under Adm. Brad Cooper, to stop Iran using ground routes to move advanced weapons and dual-use materials, coordinating with unnamed US government agencies across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea. CENTCOM said the naval blockade had bottled up $25 billion in Iranian crude oil.

The two sides are describing the same moment in opposite terms. Trump's argument, made to reporters on Friday night, is that the war was necessary and that Iran is now eager to deal: "We're dealing with them now. They want to make a deal very badly," he said, adding that "if we didn't hit Iran, you wouldn't have Israel today." His administration is split on what comes next — Axios reported earlier that Vice President Vance and envoy Steve Witkoff favour a deal, Defence Secretary Hegseth and Israel's Netanyahu favour renewed major combat, and Treasury Secretary Bessent and CIA Director Ratcliffe favour keeping the blockade and pressure without war or a deal. Tehran's position, repeated by its officials, is that it will not negotiate on its nuclear programme or reopen the strait until the US meets its seven conditions, including ending the blockade, lifting sanctions, unfreezing assets and paying war compensation — and its army spokesman warned on Saturday that retaliation against US bases would have "no geographical limitations" if Iran is attacked. But Bloomberg reported on Saturday, citing a diplomat familiar with the talks, that Iran is showing a willingness to discuss aspects of its nuclear programme that had previously been off the table. Under the latest Qatari proposal, the US would lift the blockade and release some frozen Iranian assets while Iran would allow IAEA inspectors back into its bombed nuclear sites. Qatar's prime minister said Saturday that mediation remained active and Washington was still willing to find a solution; Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said he had spoken with 12 foreign ministers in 48 hours.

For ordinary people, the stakes are measured in fuel and shipping. At least 11 ships were attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz over the past week, according to UK Maritime Trade Operations, and three of them sank. Oil settled lower on Friday — Brent at $98.32 — on hopes of a deal, but fresh explosions near the strait could reverse that quickly, and US diesel reserves are already below 100 million barrels for the first time in more than 20 years. There is still no ceasefire in force, the US naval blockade continues, and Monday, November 9 is a new moon — the dark conditions commanders prefer for air operations — which is why it remains the date to watch.

In short: explosions were reported Saturday evening on Qeshm Island and in Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas, with the cause unconfirmed and no US statement — a day after Trump confirmed Friday strikes on Qeshm air defences and CENTCOM announced a new task force to cut Iran's overland supply routes. Qatar's mediation continues, with Bloomberg reporting Iran may now discuss nuclear issues it had ruled out, but Tehran's seven conditions still stand and there is no ceasefire.

Israel: Herzog picks Eisenkot to try to form a government — but the maths to a majority is brutal

Israeli President Isaac Herzog announced on Saturday evening that he will task Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief who leads the Yashar party, with forming Israel's next government, handing Benjamin Netanyahu a political setback after the October 27 election left parliament without a clear majority. The formal handover of the mandate is scheduled for Sunday at 5 p.m. at the President's Residence in Jerusalem, starting a 28-day clock — extendable by up to 14 days — for Eisenkot to build a coalition.

Caption: Gadi Eisenkot (left) with President Isaac Herzog in November 2026. Herzog announced on Saturday evening that Eisenkot would get the first chance to form a government after 67 Knesset members recommended him, against 46 for Netanyahu. (Arnon Bossani / The Media Line)

The decision followed several days of consultations with the parties in the 120-seat Knesset. Factions representing 67 lawmakers recommended Eisenkot, while 46 backed Netanyahu and a faction holding seven seats declined to recommend either. Herzog had said on Thursday, at the end of the formal consultations, that he would keep talking to political figures over the weekend before deciding; those talks included separate meetings with Netanyahu and Eisenkot. But the recommendation tally is not the same as a coalition. In the election, Netanyahu's right-wing and religious bloc won 53 seats and Eisenkot's four-party alliance won 48 — neither reaches the 61 needed for a majority. Eisenkot's 67 recommendations include 19 from the two Arab-led parties, the Joint List (11) and Ra'am (8), and he has ruled out forming a government with them as formal coalition partners. Avigdor Lieberman, leader of Yisrael Beiteinu in Eisenkot's own alliance, has said he would oppose a government dependent on Arab-led parties. Herzog himself warned that the numbers were not the whole story: "The results of the consultations may appear clear, but the authority vested in me by law is to determine who has the ability to form a government, and that is not entirely clear, given the mutual vetoes between the blocs and within the blocs themselves."

The two camps are pitching very different governments. Eisenkot said on Friday that he intends to form a national emergency government, and his supporters present him as the candidate who can end a long period of political deadlock. Netanyahu's camp argues that the election result — his bloc won more seats than Eisenkot's — means he remains the more natural prime minister, and Netanyahu has seized on a vote-tampering investigation to cast doubt on the process, though the Central Election Committee said the integrity of the vote was not compromised. If Eisenkot cannot form a government within the allotted period, Herzog may nominate another candidate, and if no candidate succeeds, Israel will hold a new election. Netanyahu remains prime minister in a caretaker role while negotiations continue, and the new Knesset is due to be sworn in on Tuesday, November 10.

For Israelis, the practical consequence is weeks of coalition bargaining before any new government can take office — and possibly another election if the maths does not work. A caretaker government cannot take major decisions, and the deadlock comes while Israel is still managing the aftermath of the war in Gaza and the wider regional confrontation with Iran. Eisenkot's initial mandate expires on December 6, with a possible extension to December 20.

In short: President Herzog has chosen Gadi Eisenkot to try first to form Israel's next government, with the formal mandate handed over on Sunday and 28 days to build a coalition. Eisenkot has 67 recommendations to Netanyahu's 46, but his bloc holds only 48 seats and needs 61 — and the 19 Arab-party recommendations that got him the mandate come from parties he has ruled out as coalition partners, so the path to a majority is far from clear.

Ukraine: Komyshuvakha toll rises to 55; Ukraine hits missile-parts plant in Russia

The death toll from the Russian bombing of the humanitarian aid point at Komyshuvakha has risen to 55, according to a war briefing summarising Saturday's reporting — up from 53 on Friday. The aid point was hit by a 3-ton glide bomb earlier in the week, with a second strike hitting a shelter. Separately, Ukraine struck the Proton plant in Oryol, which makes components for the Iskander ballistic missiles fired at Kyiv hours earlier, and Russian media reported a Ukrainian hit on the Saratov refinery (unconfirmed). Chernihiv was reported fully blacked out after Friday night's barrage, and DTEK imposed emergency outages across Kyiv region. A Naftogaz oil and gas facility in central Ukraine was struck three times, sparking a large fire. Sources · Security Signal

Poland: three bodies found at Krakow gunman's home; suspect in critical condition

Police found the bodies of three people — two women and a man — during a search of the gunman's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 120 miles north of Krakow. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there are "strong indications" they are members of the suspect's immediate family. The suspect, a 24-year-old Polish citizen, underwent multiple surgeries and is in a "stable but very critical condition," a regional police spokesperson told Reuters. Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz denied reports the man was a soldier: "The man was not and is not a soldier in the Polish Armed Forces and has never had anything to do with the military." The toll in Krakow stands at six killed and 15 injured (some outlets report 16 injured, counting the suspect). National mourning runs November 8–9. Sources · Reuters via 98.5 The River

Yemen: toll from al-Shariya strikes rises to 27 killed, 36 wounded

The Houthi-run health ministry now says at least 27 people were killed and 36 wounded in Saturday's airstrikes on al-Shariya village, east of Sanaa — up from the 25 killed reported in the evening brief. The ministry said the strikes hit homes in the Bani Hushaysh district and that most of those killed were women and children; a hospital director said many victims remained trapped under the rubble, including first responders. The Houthis blame Saudi Arabia; Riyadh has not commented, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the account. The ministry's figures changed several times during the day (11, then 22, then 25, then 27 dead), so treat the toll as still fluid. Sources · Zark News

Tech: Google Cloud opens Vera Rubin AI instances

Google Cloud made its A5X accelerator instances generally available on November 6 — Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 systems on its AI Hypercomputer platform, each bare-metal instance carrying four Rubin GPUs with 288 GB of HBM4 apiece (1,152 GB of GPU memory per machine) and up to 7,200 Gbps of aggregate networking. General availability does not mean instant rental: access requires contacting Google to arrange a future capacity reservation, and on-demand, Spot and Flex-start purchasing are not supported. Ineffable Intelligence, founded by AlphaGo researcher David Silver, was named as an early customer. Source

Cyber: WP Data Access auth bypass disclosed; no new KEV additions

WP Data Access plugin for WordPress (through 5.5.89) has an incorrect-authorization flaw, CVE-2026-118267 (CVSS 8.1), letting an unauthenticated attacker write to arbitrary database rows through its app endpoints — including overwriting the administrator's password to take over a site — but only if an administrator has published a Data App that allows anonymous writes; on default installs an attacker needs a Subscriber-level account. Published November 7; no known exploitation, not in CISA KEV. Source

No new CISA KEV additions today beyond the three already covered in the evening brief (WordPress CVE-2026-119977, Apple CVE-2024-27834, SonicWall CVE-2026-19868). The SonicWall SSL-VPN MFA-bypass flaw is confirmed exploited in the wild; federal patch deadline is November 20. Sources · CVETodo
