1. World escalation

  • Energy ceasefire: Russia says it hit no Ukrainian energy targets overnight — first clean night since Trump's Sunday announcement. Moscow's daily strike summary mentioned transport and logistics but no energy facilities. Zelensky says Ukraine has held off refinery strikes for two days and Russia failed to reciprocate; Kremlin spokesman Peskov would only say "contacts are ongoing." Trump acknowledged Monday there have been "violations on both sides."

Source: AnewZ (Reuters wire), NY Post

  • FT and CNN report the State Department and Pentagon warned Ukrainian and European officials that US assistance would stop if Kyiv doesn't rein in long-range strikes. This is the follow-up to the previously unverified PURL/intel-cutoff claim; the White House still hasn't commented. Developing.

Source: NY Post

  • Hormuz is now the worst tanker war in two weeks — 24 ships struck in that window, six on Sunday alone, the heaviest single day since the war began Feb. 28. Oil flows through the strait are under half pre-war levels. ISW warns Iran is getting better at targeting ships and may be improving its anti-ship missile capabilities.

Source: NY Post

  • IRGC claims it struck a supertanker in the Strait of Hormuz that had its transponder off (reported Oct 13, incident allegedly a day earlier). Unconfirmed — vessel identity and damage unknown. Single-source state-media-adjacent reporting; developing.

Source: Dainik Jagran roundup

  • WSJ: Iran is fortifying its nuclear sites and rallying the public for a possible return to fighting. Tehran has warned Arab officials it could strike regional targets including Saudi oil facilities; a third US carrier strike group and another Marine expeditionary unit are steaming toward the region; Trump has publicly mulled hitting Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site.

Source: WSJ

  • Trump warned the Houthis on Tuesday and said he "might" ask Turkey's Erdogan to help hit them. Comes after the weekend's airport attacks; the US has held off striking since the May 2025 ceasefire on US vessels.

Source: NY Post

  • Poland's first assassination-plot suspect is now identified as Krzysztof N., 66 — charged with nine offenses including plotting to forcibly overthrow state authorities using stockpiled explosives found in his apartment. Prosecutors say he built a group of several hundred followers via online recordings; he faces 2–15 years. A court rules Wednesday on keeping him in custody.

Source: Reuters

4. Cybersecurity

  • CORRECTION — the morning brief called October Patch Tuesday "unusually quiet"; it was actually Microsoft's largest ever. ~1,413 CVEs per the MSRC, including three zero-days: CVE-2026-96951 (CVSS 8.8, ACTIVELY EXPLOITED Windows Shell Mark-of-the-Web bypass via command injection), CVE-2026-108398 (.NET Framework, found by Google Project Zero, no known exploitation), and CVE-2026-92707 (MessagePack-CSharp, publicly disclosed). Microsoft also debuted "risk cohorts" — provisional CVE groupings for shared assessment. Patch Windows first.

Source: DeafNews

  • Ivanti Connect Secure zero-day actively exploited: CVE-2026-104935 (CVSS 8.6), an authentication-bypass flaw allowing unauthenticated remote command execution. Ivanti's Oct 13 advisory says exploitation happened before disclosure as a true zero-day against a "limited number of customers"; CISA added it to KEV the same day. Worse: the attacker chained it with a second, still-unpatched privilege escalation flaw. Volexity published a full analysis — if you run ICS, patch now and don't trust the Integrity Checker alone.

Source: IT Security News, Cybersecurity News, SecurityOnline

  • Sharecare: ~318k email addresses surfaced on Have I Been Pwned, tied to ShinyHunters' August extortion campaign. 73% were already in HIBP from earlier breaches, so the exposure is mostly recycled — but it's another reminder that ShinyHunters kept what they stole.

Source: Undercode News

  • France supermarket fridge-hack: investigators are hunting the attackers; still no claim of responsibility. French security firm Onyphe says outdated, vulnerable Danfoss refrigeration controllers remain widely exposed online; some impacted stores are back up.

Source: DevDiscourse (syndicated), CNN

  • Ahold Delhaize's Tuesday update: a controlled restart of systems is underway, Albert Heijn deliveries are resuming, but some stores still have fresh-food shortages. The company says there's no evidence of customer or employee data theft; the Dutch NCSC says it sees no link to the French supermarket attack.

Source: LinksGraph, Reuters via KELO-FM, SecurityWeek
