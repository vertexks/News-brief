World escalation

US strikes Qeshm after Iran hits Bahrain — and the war spreads to Kuwait

The United States confirmed on Tuesday that its fighter jets struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island, after Iran targeted a US radar in Bahrain for the third time in five days — and the day's fighting spread further, with Kuwait saying it faced hostile drone attacks and Bahrain intercepting more Iranian drones overnight. It is the sharpest single-day escalation in the Gulf war in weeks, and it came even as Qatari mediators kept meeting Iranian officials.

The details, as reported by Reuters, AP, CBS and Iranian state media: a US military official, speaking anonymously, said US fighter jets fired on a Revolutionary Guards missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, calling the strike "self-defense" and "in response to IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets." Hours earlier, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted a US radar system at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base; Bahrain and US Central Command said the drones were intercepted, with no casualties or damage. It was the third attempted Iranian strike on a US military installation in five days, and all three were intercepted, US officials said. Iranian media reported five or six explosions on Qeshm on Tuesday afternoon, and Hormozgan deputy governor Ahmad Nafisi said the strikes caused no damage to residential, commercial or infrastructure areas. AP notes the strikes broke a lull of about two months, though limited US strikes on Qeshm have continued periodically. Overnight, Bahrain's defence force said it had intercepted more Iranian aerial attacks on Wednesday, and Kuwait's military said on Tuesday evening it was hitting back at "hostile drone attacks," with air defences intercepting them — no one has claimed responsibility for the Kuwait attack.

Iran, meanwhile, made a string of claims that have not been independently verified: its Revolutionary Guards said they shot down a US MQ-1 Predator south of Qeshm at about 1:45 p.m., calling it the 18th MQ-1 they claim to have downed, and the army separately said it downed a US-made LUCAS drone over the strait. Iran's navy said a supertanker and an LPG carrier caught fire after striking mines, one reportedly carrying two million barrels of crude, and a third tanker was later reported on fire. The US has not confirmed either drone loss — Washington rarely confirms or denies individual drone losses — and Reuters could not independently verify the tanker claims. What is confirmed independently is that UK Maritime Trade Operations said the crew of a tanker attacked north of the Omani port of Khasab was unaccounted for, and shipping monitors counted 17 confirmed incidents in the strait this month. Iran's navy warned shipping firms against using the southern route, calling it "an iron and fiery wall of mines," while the US military said in late October it had cleared the strait's main transit routes.

The two sides are also fighting a war of words over something darker. CNN reported, citing US intelligence assessments, that Iran could be developing plans to target administration officials with a dirty bomb or chemical weapon in Washington — the reporting stressed it was "an effort to give itself options rather than an indicator of an imminent attack." Trump said the US takes it very seriously and that Iran would "pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before" if it set off a dirty bomb in Washington. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, called the reports "RUBBISH" and invoked the false Iraq WMD claims of 2003: "Spare us the Iraq War-style propaganda." The New York Times separately reported that Iran's network of dams might be cover for underground missile development. On diplomacy, Qatar's foreign ministry said mediation continues and a meeting with the Iranian side was held on Tuesday, warning that escalation poses an "extremely grave danger" to reaching an agreement; Araghchi also met Oman's foreign minister and Sultan Haitham in Muscat, where the two sides discussed safeguarding maritime corridors.

For ordinary people, the cost is showing up in fuel prices and in the risks borne by seafarers. Brent crude settled at $93.14 a barrel on Tuesday, up 0.76%, and WTI at $86.01 — well below April's wartime peak of $126.41, but the market swung all day on each new incident, and Brent eased to about $92.98 on Wednesday morning after US inventory data showed a 5.28-million-barrel build. Iraq resumed pumping oil to Turkey through its pipeline at 120,000 barrels a day, rising toward 500,000, specifically to avoid the strait. Inside Iran, Amnesty International said on Tuesday that the authorities have used the cover of war to intensify repression after the January crackdown, with its lead researcher saying "there is no doubt that thousands were massacred in less than 48 hours" — the exact toll still cannot be quantified after the January 8–9 internet blackout.

In short: the US confirmed it hit an IRGC missile site on Qeshm in self-defense after Iran's third attack on Bahrain in five days, Iran claims two US drones downed and three more tankers hit by mines — none of it verified — and the fighting spread to Kuwait, with Bahrain intercepting more drones overnight. Watch whether Washington confirms or denies the drone losses, whether the tanker attacks continue, and Thursday's Shenzhen trilateral, where Trump says he will raise Iran.

Another night of strikes on the grids — and the Kerch arch is in the sea

Russia hit Ukraine's power grid again overnight, Ukraine struck back at Russian energy sites, and the full scale of Ukraine's Kerch Bridge attack is now clear: Reuters-verified video shows the bridge's main road arch has crumbled into the sea. Britain, meanwhile, announced more than 1,000 new air-defence missiles for Ukraine's winter, and a Russian drone flew over Moldova's capital.

The details, as reported by Al Jazeera and Reuters: Zelensky said Russia's overnight attacks damaged power generation facilities and substations in nine regions, leaving emergency outages in many cities, with hours-long power and water outages across Kyiv and surrounding regions. Russia's Defence Ministry said it hit two power stations in the Kyiv and Zhytomyr regions, and Reuters, citing Ukraine's emergency service, reported strikes on Poltava, Dnipro, Kirovohrad and Rivne as well. On the other side, Ukrainian drone strikes killed three people in Russia's Belgorod region, according to local Russian authorities, and Russia's Energy Ministry said Ukrainian strikes hit energy infrastructure overnight, with temporary outage schedules in force and parts of Russian-occupied Crimea also losing power. Russia claimed it downed 709 Ukrainian drones across 14 regions, Crimea and the Black and Azov seas — a figure that, like all such tallies, has not been independently verified.

On the Kerch Bridge, the picture sharpened on Tuesday. Videos and images posted by Zelensky and verified by Reuters showed the bridge's main road arch had crumbled into the sea, leaving a yawning gap after two strikes in as many days. Crimea's Moscow-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, said two people were killed and road traffic was suspended, with rail traffic partially resumed. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov promised a repair plan that would make the bridge "even better than before," but Russian military bloggers and other experts said repairs would be hard given Ukraine's capacity to strike again, and Finnish military analyst Emil Kastehelmi posted: "The road bridge won't be repaired during this war." Zelensky framed the operation as payback — "Russia strikes our infrastructure every day, including bridges... we are reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began" — and said Ukraine had repeatedly offered de-escalation steps that Russia rejected. In Moldova, President Maia Sandu said a Russian drone flew over the capital Chisinau, a rare occurrence, and pledged to press for further sanctions; Russia rejected the accusation, saying no proof had been provided.

The allies' answer to the winter campaign came at the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in Brussels. Britain announced a £240 million package — more than 1,000 additional short-range air-defence missiles and launchers, plus more than a dozen air-defence radars — with deliveries starting in mid-December, on top of 400 surface-to-air missiles already supplied ahead of winter. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the systems, built by Thales in Belfast, have proven effective against the Iranian-designed jet-powered Shahed drones Russia is using against Ukrainian cities. The package is part of a £500 million pledge from last month's Budget, and Prime Minister Andy Burnham is expected to announce the remaining support at a "coalition of the willing" meeting on Thursday. Germany and Britain also rejected Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby's criticism of Ukraine's deep strikes at the Brussels meeting: Pistorius called the nightly Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy, water and heating infrastructure "kind of terrorism," and Streeting said Ukraine's strikes "helped to degrade Putin's war machine, buying time and saving lives."

For ordinary people, it is the power grid that decides the winter. About 700,000 households in the Dnipropetrovsk region were without electricity on Tuesday morning, and the new overnight strikes hit nine more regions' generation and substations. In Kyiv, the two struck bridges are still only partly reopened, and some districts remain on backup heating. The human toll keeps rising from earlier strikes too: Ukraine's national police said the death toll from the November 2 Russian strike on a humanitarian aid centre in the frontline village of Komyshuvakha has risen to 72, with four people still missing.

In short: both sides spent another night hitting each other's energy infrastructure, the Kerch Bridge's main road arch is now in the sea after two strikes, and Britain pledged 1,000+ missiles and radars for the winter — while a Russian drone over Chisinau rattled Moldova. Watch Thursday's Shenzhen trilateral and whether the overnight grid attacks keep escalating.

Cybersecurity

  • Critical Java Vulnerability Requires Immediate Patching — "Java4Shell" (CVE-2026-114938): a critical memory-corruption flaw in OpenJDK's Base64 decoder can let an unauthenticated attacker crash the JVM or run code remotely. Security firm Calif, which found the flaw with help from an AI coding tool, rates it 9.2 under CVSS 4.0 (Oracle rates it 8.1); researchers demonstrated pre-authentication remote code execution on default installations of Keycloak, Atlassian Jira, Atlassian Confluence, JFrog Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate, and authenticated RCE on Kafka, Solr, Elasticsearch, Gerrit and Spinnaker. It affects unpatched OpenJDK builds from 17.0.6 onward (branches 17, 21, 25, 27) running on x86-64 processors with AVX-512 VBMI — Intel Xeon from Ice Lake through Granite Rapids and AMD EPYC from Zen 4 onward; ARM64 is not affected. Oracle released fixed builds on November 17 (17.0.21.1, 21.0.13.1, 25.0.5.1, 27.0.1.1), and a single JVM flag closes the exposure where patching has to wait. CISA issued an alert on November 17 urging immediate patching, internet-facing systems first, and warning that bundled runtimes in containers and appliances must be found. No active exploitation has been reported as of November 17. (CISA · Qualys · Sophos)
  • Norway's Equinor detects ongoing cyberattack, oil and gas output unaffected: Europe's largest gas supplier said on Tuesday it had detected an ongoing cyberattack against some of its IT systems, with its internal email system down and disruptions to IT applications. The company said it has taken steps to neutralise the attack, that there is no indication data was stolen, and that operations and production are unaffected. Norway's National Security Authority said it is aware of the incident; Norway raised its cyber-risk level to 4 out of 5 in late October. (Reuters via KWSN · Cryptopolitan)
  • Optimizely supply-chain attack hit 178 organizations' websites — "OPTIMJACK": Optimizely confirmed that an attacker exploited a flaw in its Web Experimentation account signup flow to inject malicious JavaScript into customer projects, which was then served from Optimizely's own CDN to visitors of at least 178 organizations — including Zoom, the BBC, CNN, Salesforce, GitLab, IKEA, HSBC, Equifax, Air France-KLM, Ledger and Peacock. The payload swapped cryptocurrency wallet addresses, skimmed payment cards, stole cookies and screenshots, and delivered iOS exploit kits to iPhone and iPad visitors; about $7,000 in crypto reached the attacker's wallets. Security firm Report URI found the code in 188 of 1,611 snippets it checked, starting with the BBC's snippet at 04:21 UTC on November 16; most of it was cleaned up within a day. Optimizely says it has fixed the flaw, removed the injected code and secured affected accounts; Salesforce confirmed it was impacted and disabled Optimizely scripts, and Ledger confirmed it was affected. No CVE has been assigned yet, and how the attacker got write access to so many unrelated projects is still unexplained. (Computerworld · Breach News · Report URI via matt.security)
  • Attackers target MSP ScreenConnect servers (CVE-2026-84869): more than 40 MSP ScreenConnect servers received over 688,000 guest-run file requests exploiting the flaw to push and execute malicious files during remote sessions. Single-source report — treat as reported, not confirmed. (SitRep)
  • N-able N-central zero-day tied to Apache Axis deserialization: a report ties the end-of-life Apache Axis 1.x deserialization flaw (CVE-2026-90436, CVE-2026-129280) to active N-central zero-day exploitation, with 21 servers compromised. Single-source report — treat as reported, not confirmed. (CVETodo)
  • Cisco details Node.js and Brotli vulnerabilities: flaws in the Brotli decoder, DNS resolution and TLS hostname validation (CVE-2026-111497, CVE-2026-105482, CVE-2026-105483, CVE-2026-106069) could allow remote code execution or traffic interception. Fixes are available in Node.js 22.x, 24.x and 26.x. (SitRep / Cisco)
  • KEV watch: no new additions since this morning's Puppet Enterprise CVE-2026-115731; the catalog stands at 1,782 entries. The Apache HTTP/2 flaw CVE-2026-119618 is still not in KEV despite Singapore's CSA saying it is reportedly being exploited.
