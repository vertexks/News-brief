Morning brief — Tuesday, November 10, 2026

Russia pounds Ukraine's grid as Abu Dhabi talks open — and end day one still split

Russia hit Ukraine's power grid again overnight, killing two people and knocking out substations across the country, on the very morning Russian, Ukrainian and American negotiators sat down in Abu Dhabi for their first three-way talks since February. By the end of day one, the positions were exactly where they started: Moscow wants the shape of a future European security order agreed before any ceasefire, Kyiv wants the shooting to stop first, and the land question hasn't moved at all.

Russian forces struck 10 Ukrainian regions overnight, hitting five major electrical substations that move power between regions — in Poltava, Dnipropetrovsk, Vinnytsia and Kirovohrad — according to Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi. Two people were killed in the Vinnytsia region. State grid operator Ukrenergo said energy infrastructure was hit in eight regions overnight and into the morning, leaving homes without power in six, including Kyiv, Kharkiv and Dnipropetrovsk. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile overnight. The Guardian's live blog, citing the Kyiv Independent, reported at least nine people killed and 39 injured across Ukraine over the past day. Russia's defence ministry said its forces shot down 155 Ukrainian drones overnight over eight Russian regions and Crimea, and said it struck cargo vessels and a tanker in the Black Sea, including a ship east of Odesa.

The talks themselves opened in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday with the US envoy Jared Kushner telling reporters that almost daily contacts with both sides had "narrowed the gaps" and that the Americans had "some good ideas on how to bridge it" — but he stopped short of promising a settlement. The Russian delegation is led by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky, Ukraine's by foreign intelligence chief Rustem Umerov, with presidential office chief Kyrylo Budanov also taking part. Ukrainian negotiators spent roughly two months preparing, Umerov said, and Kyiv's mandate covers "all the issues", with security guarantees the priority.

What each side argues is now on the record, and the two positions pull in opposite directions. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said after the day's session that Russia would not agree to an immediate ceasefire without knowing what would happen next — in Moscow's telling, the outlines of a future European security architecture should be agreed first, and only then should the fighting stop. Kyiv and its European backers want the reverse: Ukraine is seeking binding assurances that Russia will not invade again and wants the fighting to stop, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on Monday that any meaningful discussion of Europe's security architecture can only come once a ceasefire is achieved. On territory, Moscow wants Ukraine to withdraw from the parts of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson that it claims to have annexed; Kyiv refuses to cede territory and has proposed freezing the conflict along the current front line. US officials have said both sides showed "a little bit of flexibility" in private, but neither government has said anything similar in public, and President Trump has publicly suggested Ukraine will end up with "a little bit different shape" — a remark Kyiv has not welcomed.

Europe's place at the table was contested all week. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov insisted the format is strictly three-way, and Kushner described the Europeans as being kept informed on the sidelines, with a wider format only after progress is made. Even so, European officials from the UK, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission met the US and Ukrainian teams in Abu Dhabi, and a joint statement said any settlement must respect Ukraine's sovereignty, territorial integrity and independent decision-making, and that a strong NATO will continue to be central to collective Euro-Atlantic security. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Europe does not need to establish a new security architecture.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the overnight strikes are the point, not the talks. The grid attacks are part of a renewed air campaign as winter approaches, and Moscow has expanded its targeting in recent months to businesses, consumer logistics and other economic targets — a home improvement store in Kirovohrad was hit overnight — in a bid to weaken the war effort. Homes across six regions woke up without power, and Ukraine has struggled to intercept the fast-moving ballistic missiles and jet-powered drones. One small humanitarian step stands out against that: a six-day local ceasefire around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky at the end of October and beginning of November allowed civilians to leave and aid to get in, and the US says it will keep working on prisoner exchanges and other humanitarian issues.

In short: the first three-way talks in nine months opened in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and ended day one with the same split — Moscow wants a security order agreed before a ceasefire, Kyiv wants the ceasefire first, and neither side has budged on territory — while Russia kept hitting Ukraine's power grid overnight, killing two people and cutting power in six regions. The second session is Wednesday, and Washington has signalled it may follow Abu Dhabi with separate meetings with Putin and Zelensky.

Houthis/Saudi — overnight update: drone downed over Eastern Province, Saudi jets hit Sanaa

The overnight exchange covered in the night brief — three Houthi missiles intercepted, two aimed at Riyadh and one at the south — had two additions on Tuesday morning. The coalition later said it also intercepted and destroyed a drone over Saudi Arabia's oil-rich Eastern Province, and the Houthi-run Saba news agency reported Saudi airstrikes on Sanaa, hitting the al-Mufazir gas station in the Jadar district north of the capital and a building belonging to the state oil company in southern Sanaa. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the Sanaa strikes, and the Houthis did not immediately claim the overnight launches. No casualties were reported from the interceptions. (Reuters via RadioCMS)

Iran — Canada reimposes sanctions; US answer still due today

Canada reimposed sanctions on 11 Iranian entities on Tuesday for contributing to Iran's nuclear activities and ballistic missile programme, Foreign Minister Anita Anand said. The list includes Iran's Nuclear Fuel Production and Procurement Company, the Nuclear Reactors Fuel Company, the National Iranian Oil Company and the National Iranian Tanker Company. The US answer to Tehran's two-day Hormuz proposal is still expected today, with Qatari officials due in Tehran the same day. Iran has not responded to the single-source report of a phased US offer (partial blockade lift first, then nuclear talks), and Tehran's own officials remain split — Pezeshkian talking negotiation, security chief Rezaei calling talk of a deal "fake news". (Reuters)

Israel — new Knesset sworn in today; opposition picks Kahana for speaker

Israel's 26th Knesset is sworn in this afternoon, with 46 of its 120 members serving for the first time. Hours before the ceremony, the opposition bloc chose Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party as its candidate for Knesset speaker, in a bid to take control of parliament from Amir Ohana of Netanyahu's Likud. At least 61 lawmakers must sign a request or vote to replace the speaker. Eisenkot, who received the mandate to form a government on Sunday, has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. Netanyahu remains prime minister of a caretaker government until a new coalition is sworn in. (The Insight International · 10News)

Khor Mor — still shut, no restart date; US envoy condemns the attack

Production at the Khor Mor gas field in Iraqi Kurdistan remained suspended on Tuesday, with operator Dana Gas still assessing damage from Sunday night's drone strike and giving no restart date. The strike cut 2,500–2,800 MW — roughly 70–80% of the region's power — and Baghdad has ordered household gas supplies to the region until the field restarts. A US presidential envoy condemned the attack on Tuesday. No group has claimed responsibility. (Silent Dossier · JBizNews)

Tech

Nvidia unveils GR00T 2, a robot model that learns by watching humans

Nvidia introduced GR00T 2 at the Conference on Robot Learning in Austin on Monday, November 9 — an open world-action model for robots, built on its Cosmos platform, that learns new manipulation skills largely from watching videos of humans do the job. The model was trained on robot data plus 100,000 hours of open-source egocentric human video, which Nvidia released as the Human-1M-100K dataset under a Creative Commons licence. Nvidia reports the model completed 40 of 50 evaluated tasks at least once with no task-specific demonstrations, averaged 71% success with one demonstration per task and 95% with five — its own figures, for 50 tasks it has not named. The model code is due in December and no weights release date or licence has been announced, so "open" is for now more of a promise than a download. Alongside it, Isaac Lab 3.0 adds a Newton physics option and reinforcement post-training, with up to 2.5× training throughput on a dexterous-manipulation benchmark. (NVIDIA Developer Forums · TechTimes · Poyo AI Daily)

Anthropic retracted its IPO pricing hours before the roadshow

Anthropic sent banks a securities filing late Monday showing about 866.7 million Class A shares at $70 to $80 each — implying up to $69.3 billion in proceeds at the top of the range, plus a separate $26 billion private placement from Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC — then emailed back saying the pricing details were "inaccurate" and that it would update the filing later. Reuters, which saw the emails, could not determine what was wrong, and Anthropic declined to comment. The roadshow is still expected to start today, Tuesday, with pricing the following week. The $69.3 billion figure is a draft number the company disowned, not a confirmed IPO size. (Reuters via The Hindu BusinessLine · Unite.AI)

Nvidia — Monday's sell-off wiped about $250 billion off its value

Nvidia shares fell on Monday after CEO Jensen Huang's admission of an affair with a woman US officials allege was a Chinese intelligence asset, wiping roughly $250 billion from the company's market value, according to AI-news roundups this morning. The board's independent investigation continues, House chairs want an answer by Wednesday, November 11, on a closed-door briefing November 18, and Nvidia reports Q3 earnings November 17. Catch-up: the Financial Times reported on November 3 that Nvidia has verbally told its global cloud partners to sell at least 51% of their services to non-Chinese customers — a cap on the overseas-rental route Chinese firms use to reach advanced chips — applying to new contracts only, with Nvidia neither confirming nor denying the 51% figure. (Kill The AI · Clash Report)

Cyber

Xen Orchestra critical auth bypass is being actively exploited — patch now

Vates published a critical advisory on Tuesday for Xen Orchestra (VSA-2026-062): an empty-object login token in REST cookie handling lets an attacker bypass authentication entirely and act with admin privilege, and from there take over managed XCP-ng pools and nodes as root. Vates says the flaw is actively exploited by a threat actor operating from 91.211.251.112:8001 and a Tor onion address, and that exploitation by other actors can't be ruled out. Affected: xo-server before 5.213.1, and 5.213.2 in Xen Orchestra before 6.9.1 (stable) and 6.10.0 (latest). Fixed in Xen Orchestra 6.9.1 and 6.10.0, released today. (Vates advisory · Your Cyber Brief)

Barracuda confirms exploitation hitting a small number of organisations

Barracuda's advisory, updated November 9, says it has identified exploitation affecting a small number of organisations on specified appliances with vulnerable firmware and an attacker-accessible admin interface. Patches are out for the named appliances; Barracuda has not named the victims or described the evidence in detail. (Your Cyber Brief)

Plex2Shell — catch-up: QNAP warned last week that unpatched Plex servers are being hit

QNAP warned on November 6 that attackers are actively exploiting the Plex2Shell chain — four chained flaws (CVE-2026-96652, CVE-2026-96654, CVE-2026-96655, CVE-2026-96656) in Plex Media Server — against internet-exposed NAS devices, gaining unauthenticated remote code execution via port 32400. CISA added all four to its KEV catalog on October 20. Over 36,000 internet-exposed Plex servers were identified as unpatched, and QNAP says patching alone won't remove backdoors already installed — affected users should treat devices and stored data as fully compromised and do a clean reinstall. Fix: Plex 1.43.3.10861 or later. (All About Cookies · Aviatrix)

No new CISA KEV additions since the four added Monday (GPUStack, FreeSWITCH, OpenSSH "SSHole", IBM Aspera Faspex) — federal remediation deadline for those four is Thursday, November 12.

Courts

Farwell jury goes back to deliberate today — judge expected to give an Allen charge

The federal jury in former Stoughton detective Matthew Farwell's murder trial — accused of killing Sandra Birchmore, 23 and pregnant, in 2021 and staging it as a suicide — resumes deliberating today after telling the judge on Monday it was deadlocked after about 10½ hours and did not expect that to change. Judge Denise Casper sent the panel home Monday afternoon; former Massachusetts AG Martha Coakley said the judge will most likely give an "Allen charge" today, instructing holdout jurors to listen to the other side. A conviction on either count carries a mandatory life sentence. (Particle · Vinnie Politan)

Krakow: no fresh developments; post-mortems on the nine victims began Monday and were expected to take about two days.
