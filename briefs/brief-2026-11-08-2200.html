Night brief — Sunday, November 8, 2026

Nvidia's board opens an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang over an alleged Chinese spy link

Nvidia's board of directors announced on Sunday that it is opening an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation — hours after Huang told employees in an email that he had been unfaithful to his wife. Huang, 63, remains CEO while the inquiry runs, and the board says it has no reason to believe company secrets or US national security were compromised. It is the biggest corporate-governance story in tech this year, landing the night before Huang's GTC keynote and nine days before Nvidia's quarterly earnings.

The woman at the centre of the reports is Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le — not related to Jensen Huang — a former executive at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based data-centre company and major Nvidia customer in Southeast Asia. According to the New York Times, US intelligence agencies concluded last year that she was sharing information with the Chinese government while working with Nvidia to build data centres in Asia, and a highly classified spring 2025 memo warned top Trump administration officials that she had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to learn about US-made AI chips and possibly push Nvidia to make its most advanced chips available in China. The Financial Times later reported that a US intelligence memo alleged a romantic relationship between the two Huangs. Megaspeed has bought more than $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia chips since June 2024, according to the Times, and officials from the Commerce and Justice departments have been investigating Megaspeed and its potential ties to the Chinese government for more than a year. Megaspeed was spun off from a Chinese company in 2023.

Huang's email to staff, reported by CNBC and others, said he had been unfaithful to his wife, that his actions had hurt her and their children, and that he took full responsibility. He apologised to colleagues and shareholders, said he remained committed to leading Nvidia, and asked for privacy for his family. "Regarding the national security concerns raised in news reports," he wrote, "Nvidia's Board of Directors has initiated an independent investigation." The board said a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent lawyers, will oversee the review, and that Huang supports it and has committed to cooperating. It gave no timetable. The Wall Street Journal reports that Huang will remain CEO while the inquiry proceeds, and Bloomberg reported the same.

The two sides of this story disagree sharply on what the relationship meant. US officials cited by the Journal and the Times treat Alice Huang as a recruited intelligence asset who used the relationship to get close to the head of the world's most important AI chipmaker. China's Foreign Ministry has called the allegations "unsubstantiated" and described them as smears. Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said its officials had not claimed Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset and had not investigated her personally — though it confirmed an investigation into Megaspeed is ongoing. Nvidia previously described Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang as "not unusual or unexpected" given their business ties, and Huang told Bloomberg the company had examined Megaspeed's GPU deployments and found no evidence its chips had been illegally diverted to China. An affair admission does not establish that Huang knowingly assisted an intelligence operation or disclosed sensitive information — that is exactly what the board inquiry is meant to examine.

Congress is moving in parallel. Three Republican House committee chairs — Brian Mast (Foreign Affairs), John Moolenaar (China Select) and Rick Crawford (Intelligence) — have asked Huang to sit for a closed-door briefing on November 18 about his interactions with Alice Huang and Nvidia's protections against foreign espionage, and to accept the date or propose an alternative by November 11. Senator Elizabeth Warren cited reporting that Megaspeed had bought at least $4.8 billion of advanced Nvidia chips.

For ordinary people the stakes are less about the affair than about the chips. Nvidia sits at the centre of the US-China AI race, and its hardware powers much of the AI boom — the same boom that has already pushed up memory prices and reshaped tech spending. Markets have not yet had a chance to react: the announcement came on a Sunday, so Monday's open is the first test, and Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings on November 17. Huang is still scheduled to deliver the GTC keynote on Monday at 9 a.m. CET, which now lands hours after the board's announcement.

In short: Nvidia's board is independently investigating whether its CEO was targeted by a Chinese intelligence operation, after Huang admitted to an affair with a Megaspeed executive US intelligence agencies suspect of passing information to Beijing. The board says it has no evidence secrets were compromised, Huang stays in charge, and Congress wants him briefed by November 18 — with markets, the GTC keynote and earnings all coming in the next nine days.

Iran talks stall after the midterms — Araghchi warns of "hell" if the US chooses war

Hours after President Trump told CNN on Sunday that fresh US strikes on Iran were "highly unlikely" if talks keep progressing, Iran's foreign minister said the talks themselves have gone quiet — and warned Washington to accept Tehran's offer or face military retaliation. Both sides are now publicly saying the diplomacy has stalled, and blaming each other for it.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi posted on X on Sunday: "American voters have made their choice. Time for the White House to make its own." He said Iran's two-day proposal still stands — under it, the US would end the war on all fronts, unfreeze Iranian assets, lift oil sanctions and end its naval blockade of Iranian ports, and Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days. "Or choose war, and see our alert and battle-hardened Warriors rain down hell," he added. Speaking to reporters in Tehran, he said mediators had made no progress since the "shock" of the US midterm elections, in which Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress. "The main reason is the US election results, which have obviously come as a shock to everyone," Araghchi said, according to Al Jazeera. "It will take some time for things to settle down and for the atmosphere to become conducive to the mediators resuming their visits. We have to wait."

Washington's answer, delivered Sunday by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, was equally blunt. Speaking to reporters at Homestead Air Reserve Base in Florida before flying to Paris for the G7 foreign ministers' meeting, Rubio said diplomatic efforts had achieved no breakthrough and called Tehran's demands "unacceptable," "outrageous" and "ridiculous," according to ANI, citing Al Jazeera. He acknowledged that multiple third-party nations are mediating, but dismissed suggestions a deal is imminent. His assessment lines up with Araghchi's on one point — nothing is moving — and clashes with Qatar's statement on Saturday that "significant progress" had been made.

Behind the public statements, the backchannel is still alive. EA WorldView reports that Vice President J.D. Vance has been speaking almost daily with mediators, including Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani and Pakistan's military chief Field Marshal Asim Munir, and that the two sides have discussed a working-level technical meeting in the coming days. Iranian negotiators are reviewing proposals to limit Tehran's nuclear programme, according to a US official and a diplomat cited by EA WorldView, and Qatari officials are expected to continue discussions in Tehran on Tuesday. Vance has said the Iran war was a leading cause of the Democrats' midterm victories.

The US is also tightening the economic screws. Iraqi Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein told Al Jazeera that the US Treasury has asked Baghdad to shut almost all of its commercial border crossings with Iran, leaving only two operational — one of them in the autonomous Kurdistan region. Hussein said the Hormuz blockade has already cost Iraq $60 billion, and that backchannel agreements had previously let Iraqi crude move through the strait via a designated southerly corridor. Fighting has largely subsided, AFP reports, but Iran is still trying to blockade the strait while the US Navy maintains its counter-blockade of Iranian ports.

The two sides remain far apart on sequencing, and that is the core of the stall. Washington insists nuclear issues must feature in the first talks; Tehran says it will not negotiate on its atomic programme until its core conditions are addressed. Trump has said he is willing to relieve sanctions and release frozen funds in exchange for progress on nuclear issues, but on Sunday he also repeated that he would strike again "if necessary" — and named 38 bridges the US could knock down in an hour.

For the world economy, the shipping war is the part that bites: flows through Hormuz are running at under 60% of capacity, and Brent settled around $98 on Friday after a roughly 12% weekly drop. For Iraq, a country that depends on oil exports, the blockade is already a $60 billion wound. And for the region, the next watch points are concrete: whether mediator travel resumes, whether Qatari officials' Tuesday talks in Tehran produce anything, and whether the new moon on Monday — a dark night favourable for military operations — brings any movement. The US carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt is not expected in the region before mid-November.

In short: a day after Trump took new strikes off the table, Iran says mediation has frozen since the midterms and warns of "hell" if Washington chooses war, while Rubio calls Tehran's demands ridiculous and says there is no breakthrough. The backchannel through Vance and the Qataris is still open, with talks in Tehran on Tuesday, but both sides are now publicly dug in — and the US is squeezing Iraq's border trade with Iran in the meantime.

Ukraine — updates

Russian drone hit rescue crews at the Komyshuvakha aid-centre site

A Russian drone struck emergency workers on Sunday at the site of the November 2 bombing of a humanitarian aid centre in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, damaging specialised rescue equipment. No one was injured in the strike, according to Ukraine's State Emergency Service, but it came as crews were still searching for victims of an attack that has now killed at least 55 people, with around 45–50 still listed as missing by Ukraine's military intelligence agency, HUR. Ukraine has accused Russia of a "double tap" — a second strike aimed at the rescuers responding to the first. President Zelenskyy condemned the strike as an example of "extreme brutality." The toll of 55 was already reported; the strike on the rescue crews is new. (Bytes Europe/AP · EuropeSays)

Krakow — updates

Main Market Square reopens; post-mortems start Monday

Krakow's Main Market Square reopened to residents and tourists on Sunday evening, a day after the shooting that killed six people and wounded 15. Post-mortem examinations on all nine victims — the six killed in Krakow and the suspect's parents and sister in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski — begin on Monday morning and are likely to continue into Tuesday, prosecutor Eryk Stasielak told the Polish Press Agency. The 24-year-old suspect, Michał O., remains in critical condition, and prosecutors will decide on Monday what steps to take involving him if he survives. The investigation into the six Krakow killings and the three deaths at the family home is being handled as one case, classified as an act of a terrorist nature. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the suspect acted alone and held radical, ultra-nationalist views; Le Monde reports that disinformation about the attacker's political affiliations spread from Saturday night into Sunday. Of the two wounded police officers, one is stable and the other is serious but stable. (Energy Radio · Le Monde)

Tech

Apple is pushing to make Siri truly agentic — as early as next year

Apple CEO John Ternus has pressed software chief Craig Federighi to move past Siri tweaks, with an autonomous, agentic Siri targeted as early as next year, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports. Gurman says the closed-door meeting happened in recent weeks, that Apple's internal comparison set is ChatGPT Dots, Meta's Muse and Grok, and that Apple is reworking how it develops iOS, iPadOS and macOS 28 to give engineers more room before features lock. Fine-grained control of Apple's own apps — rotating and cropping photos in Photos, organising Safari tabs — is expected with iOS 27.3. Gurman reports Apple has no illusions about keeping pace with its rivals. Single-source (Gurman/Bloomberg), so treat the timeline as reported, not confirmed. (Daily AI Thread · FourWeekMBA)

Anthropic's IPO filing: $895 billion in compute commitments (catch-up, Nov 2)

Anthropic's amended IPO filing (S-1/A, November 2) discloses about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements, nearly all of it for computing power and data centres — more than double the $430 billion in its original October filing. About $69.8 billion falls due in the next 12 months, and $447.5 billion by the end of 2030. The filing also adds Q3 results: about $17 billion in revenue (up 12x year over year) and a $567 million operating loss. The biggest suppliers: Broadcom (~$161 billion in equipment leases), Google (~$121 billion), SpaceX's AI unit (up to $120.5 billion) and Amazon (~$110 billion). The roadshow can start November 10 at the earliest. Why it matters: it puts a hard number on what frontier AI actually costs to run — and how much of that bill comes due before the revenue to pay for it. (Unite.AI · AI Intent)

Cybersecurity

Japanese retailer Iris Ohyama: data of up to 7.7 million people may be exposed in a cloud breach

Japanese manufacturer Iris Ohyama, which runs the Iris Plaza online store, disclosed that personal information belonging to about 7.7 million people may have been exposed after unauthorised access to a third-party cloud service it uses. The company found a suspicious file in the external cloud service on October 28, suspended Iris Plaza and related systems, and disclosed the incident on October 29. The potentially exposed data covers about 5.8 million registered and former members plus 1.9 million non-members — names, addresses, phone numbers and birthdates; some passwords were encrypted, and credit card information was not stored in the affected service. The company says it has not confirmed how many records were actually accessed, has received no reports of misuse as of November 5, and has not identified the attackers or received any direct communication from them. Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission and police were notified October 29. (BleepingComputer · Neowin)

"sshole" SSH flaw: exploit attempts are ramping up fast

Opportunistic scanning for the new SSH flaw known as "sshole" (CVE-2026-123158) is ramping up quickly. SANS Internet Storm Center honeypots saw long-password exploit attempts rise from 23 on November 1 to 713 on November 6, with the first real exploit attempts on November 5. If you run an internet-facing SSH server, this is one to patch and watch. (CVETodo daily threat report)

Chainalysis update: most products back online, two still down

Chainalysis has restored Reactor, KYT (interface and API) and Address Screening as of its November 7 update, with tighter controls — customers must reset passwords, rotate API keys and register trusted IPs for allowlisting. Data Solutions and Chain (Beta) were still offline. The breach itself — names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users — was already reported in the November 7 night brief. (Tech Insider)

Courts

Eisenkot's coalition puzzle: two rival plans take shape

With the Knesset sworn in on Tuesday, November 10, the two rival coalition plans are now clear. Yisrael Beiteinu chairman Avigdor Liberman says he is willing to compromise on conscription legislation and wants a government of 61 MKs without the Arab parties, bringing in all of United Torah Judaism and Shas. Eisenkot's alternative is a minority government of his 48-seat bloc, supported externally by the Arab parties, potentially with United Torah Judaism abstaining. Negotiating teams from Yisrael Beiteinu and Yashar met minutes after Liberman's announcement, and Eisenkot and Liberman are expected to meet despite their rivalry. Eisenkot wants to move fast; Liberman expects a slower process. (Israel RealTime · Votendi)
