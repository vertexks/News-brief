Ukraine: Trump meets Putin's envoy, says a "big agreement" is coming — as Russia lines up new strikes on the power grid

President Donald Trump met privately at the White House on Thursday with Kirill Dmitriev, Russian President Vladimir Putin's envoy, and came out saying a deal to end the war in Ukraine could be close — and bigger than Ukraine alone. "There's going to be an agreement, it's going to be a big agreement and it's going to involve more than Ukraine," Trump told reporters in the Oval Office, adding it might include all of Europe. He declined to say what he discussed with Dmitriev. The meeting happened the same day his son-in-law Jared Kushner and other US envoys held talks with Ukrainian and European officials at NATO headquarters in Brussels, which Kushner called productive and focused on security guarantees for Ukraine and a broader European security structure. The US is holding three-way talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 with Ukraine and Russia — the first trilateral peace discussions since February.

The White House meeting grew out of an unusual event hosted by First Lady Melania Trump on Thursday, bringing Russian and Ukrainian officials together to mark a six-day local ceasefire she helped broker around Oleshky, a besieged city in Russian-occupied Kherson. The truce ran October 27 to November 1, allowed food and medical supplies in, and let nearly 800 people evacuate. Dmitriev thanked her for her "distinguished humanitarian role" and said Putin personally gave instructions for the operation; Ukraine's representatives — Davyd Arakhamia, who leads Zelenskyy's party in parliament, and human rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets — presented her with the Order of Princess Olga, First Class. The White House called it the first time a first lady had brought representatives of two nations at war together for diplomacy. Notably, Trump said he did not meet the Ukrainian delegation, and the White House gave no reason why.

On the ground, the war is not waiting for the diplomacy. Russia struck energy infrastructure in Ukraine's Kyiv and Kirovohrad regions on Friday, injuring three people in the Boryspil district near Kyiv, according to regional authorities cited by Le Monde. Russia's Defence Ministry said it hit an energy storage battery facility in the Kyiv region and a cargo vessel in the Black Sea port of Odesa, and bombed a power station near Sumy with a FAB-3000 aerial bomb. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned in his Friday evening address that intelligence shows Russia is preparing a new wave of strikes — against energy, bridges across the Dnipro River and other civilian infrastructure — and renewed his call for more Patriot air-defence interceptors. Zelenskyy and Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko also traded fresh accusations on Friday over who failed to prepare the capital for the strikes that have plunged the city into unprecedented power and water outages. And Ukraine's refinery campaign keeps biting: Bloomberg reports Ukraine has now hit six Russian oil plants in a week, including Afipsky, and the large Yanos refinery that supplies Moscow had to halt all crude processing after being hit three days ago. Ukrainian strikes also killed three people in Russia's Belgorod region, local authorities announced Wednesday.

Each side is reading the moment differently. Trump and his envoys are presenting the Dmitriev meeting and the Oleshky truce as proof a deal is within reach, with the Abu Dhabi talks as the next step. Kyiv's argument is that the nightly barrages on power plants and the planned strikes on Dnipro bridges show Moscow is trying to freeze Ukraine into submission before winter, and Zelenskyy is pressing for more air defence rather than concessions. Moscow has not commented on the new strikes in the reporting so far, and has previously framed its energy campaign as retaliation for Ukrainian refinery strikes.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the immediate reality is blackouts, not summits: Kyiv is entering another winter of emergency power cuts, with the city of more than 3 million facing long outages and a shortage of interceptors to stop fast-moving missiles and drones. For Russians, the refinery campaign is squeezing fuel supply and the export earnings that fund the war. The next real test comes Tuesday and Wednesday in Abu Dhabi, when the three sides sit down for the first time since February.

Houthis claim six attacks across Saudi Arabia — and Syria is now sending military advisers to Riyadh

The Houthis claimed six attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities on Thursday, according to a statement from their military spokesman Yahya Saree — targeting the Ras Tanura oil refinery with a ballistic missile, an Aramco facility in Abqaiq, King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh with multiple missiles, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam with a drone, the Rabigh oil refinery with a ballistic missile, and a Saudi military headquarters in the Najran region. There was no independent corroboration of the group's claims. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh on Thursday; Saudi Civil Defence said shrapnel from the interception wounded one Saudi citizen and damaged a house and a vehicle. The Arab League condemned an attempted Houthi missile attack targeting the Madinah region on Friday, calling it a serious escalation.

The attacks came despite a rare meeting earlier this week in Tehran between senior Saudi and Iranian officials, after which both sides agreed to resolve regional differences through dialogue. Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan all sent high-level representatives to the Tehran meeting, hosted by Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei and attended by Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman. Iran's press release said participants agreed to continue discussions on peace and security; Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry stressed opening negotiation channels to prevent a return to escalation. The Houthis kept firing anyway.

Riyadh is meanwhile widening its circle of helpers. Reuters reported Friday that Syria is aiding Saudi Arabia with military advice and training, and has sent a limited number of military personnel to the kingdom, according to two Syrian military sources and a senior US official. Syrian advisers and training teams are travelling to Saudi Arabia to help train Saudi and Yemeni units, and joint training exercises are under way in both countries. The move follows a Saudi-Syrian defence cooperation deal signed Wednesday during Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa's visit to Riyadh, and Turkey's ratification on Thursday of the Mecca defence pact. A senior Syrian diplomat cautioned that "any suggestion of military deployments at this stage is premature," and Washington has urged Damascus to move cautiously, warning that any Syrian deployment to help in the Yemen war risks embroiling the country in the wider regional war. Saudi authorities also warned Friday of a possible danger in the Yanbu oil hub as Houthi attacks persist.

The Houthis say their operations target Saudi oil facilities and military bases only, and deny targeting holy sites — the coalition has repeatedly accused them of launching drones toward Mecca and Medina. Riyadh's position is that the strikes are an assault on civilian infrastructure and the kingdom's sovereignty, and it is leaning on the new Mecca pact partners and now Syria for support. Inside Yemen, the internationally recognised government has called on the UN Security Council to take "deterrent and punitive measures" to stop Houthi attacks on Aden International Airport, and fighting and the siege around Taiz continue.

For ordinary people, the pattern is now daily: air-raid warnings in Riyadh and Najran, shrapnel falling on homes, airports under threat, and a war inside Yemen that keeps getting worse — the Rasd Organization for Rights and Freedoms warns about 1,110 kidney-failure patients in besieged Taiz face dialysis shortages. The diplomacy in Tehran produced words about dialogue; the missiles kept flying the same day.

In short: the Houthis claimed six attacks across Saudi Arabia on Thursday — refineries, airports and a military HQ — with no independent confirmation, while the coalition says it intercepted missiles over Riyadh and the Arab League condemned an attempt on the Madinah region. Syria is now sending military advisers to help Riyadh, and the Tehran dialogue produced no pause in the firing.

Cyber: OpenSSH "sshole" exploited within hours — and a new SonicWall KEV entry

OpenSSH "sshole" — CVE-2026-123158, exploited in the wild within 9 hours of disclosure. The flaw lets remote attackers log into passwordless accounts on Alpine Linux and other musl-based systems by submitting an oversized password (257–1,024 bytes): musl's crypt() returns '*' for the overlong password, which matches accounts whose stored password field is also '*' — the standard marker for "no password login." Threat intel firm Previdian reported Friday that its sensors saw the first attempt less than nine hours after the CVE was published, and confirmed successful root access on a honeypot running real Alpine Linux and OpenSSH — eight attempts total, all from one Japan-based IP (89.116.89[.]18). No production compromises have been reported. The fix is OpenSSH 10.6p2, released November 5, with Alpine pushing patched packages for every branch the same day. If you run Alpine or other musl systems, update or set PasswordAuthentication no now. Sources · The Hacker News

New CISA KEV addition today: SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN, CVE-2026-19868 (CVSS 7.5). CISA added the flaw Friday based on evidence of active exploitation. A weakness in SonicOS SSLVPN may let a user with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML authentication controls — including SAML-enforced MFA. Affected: SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and older, and 8.2.2-8015 and older. Federal remediation deadline: November 20. CISA has not named an actor or the scale of exploitation. The catalog now stands at 1,769 entries. Sources · EventMonitor

US agencies warned Friday to harden cold-chain systems. Five US agencies (CISA, FBI, HHS, USDA, DOT) told operators on November 5 to harden refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems, following the remotely triggered defrost attacks on European supermarkets and an attempt on three French plasma stores (EFS, hit November 2). Take controllers off the internet, isolate them on their own VLAN, and alert on setpoints outside safe ranges. Source

Zyxel GS1900 zero-day — the patch that didn't patch. Researchers at Binarly found a second, previously unpatched flaw (CVE-2026-107441) in Zyxel GS1900 switches after the June fix for CVE-2026-7273 failed to close the hole. ThaiCERT issued an alert November 6. Install Patch 3 firmware and restrict web management to an admin VLAN. Source

WordPress CVE-2026-119977: the federal patch deadline was today, November 6 — covered in the morning brief (240,000+ exploit attempts blocked). No new figures surfaced in this window.
