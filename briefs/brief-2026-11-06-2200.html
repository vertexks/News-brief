Ukraine: ballistic missiles hit Kyiv two hours after Zelenskyy warned of a new wave — as the aid-point death toll rises to 53

A barrage of Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv on Friday night, just two hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned in his nightly address that intelligence showed Russia preparing a major new strike on the country's energy grid, the bridges across the Dnipro River and civilian infrastructure. Ukraine's Air Force reported at least six missiles launched at the capital within a few minutes shortly before 11 p.m. local time, and Kyiv Independent journalists on the ground reported a series of explosions across the city. An all-clear came about 30 minutes later, but another air-raid alert sounded shortly before midnight.

The damage was still being assessed overnight, but the first details came from Mayor Vitali Klitschko and the city's military administration, via Reuters: debris from the missiles struck a five-storey apartment building in a western district of Kyiv and damaged the facade of another residential building, two people were injured, and a building housing a kindergarten was damaged. Outside the capital, Kyiv region governor Tymur Tkachenko said a jet-powered drone set a house on fire in the Boryspil district, and one man was pulled from the rubble and taken to hospital with burns and multiple shrapnel wounds. Officials kept warning residents to expect more strikes hours after the attack.

The strike landed in a week when the scale of the energy campaign has become stark. Zelenskyy told participants at the 30th Ibero-American Summit in Madrid on Thursday that Russia has now damaged "every power plant in Ukraine" — "The Russians use Shaheds and missiles to strike even the energy equipment that our nuclear power plants depend on," he said, adding that repair crews are working around the clock and asking Spain and Latin American countries for grid equipment. And the death toll from Monday's Russian bombing of an aid distribution point in the village of Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, rose to 53 on Friday, the acting regional governor said — rescue work at the site entered its fifth day, with the toll climbing from 42 earlier in the week. It is one of the deadliest strikes on civilians this year.

The two sides are reading the same week in opposite ways. Zelenskyy's argument is that Moscow is trying to freeze Ukraine into submission before winter — hitting power plants, and now threatening the Dnipro bridges that hold Kyiv together and carry much of the country's east-west logistics — and he is pressing allies not to delay Patriot interceptors. Lithuania's president Gitanas Nauseda told WELT this week that Russia will delay peace talks until spring 2027 and keep attacking through the winter, saying Moscow "is not succeeding" on the battlefield but its resources are tied up there. Trump, meanwhile, is still presenting diplomacy as close: after meeting Putin envoy Kirill Dmitriev on Thursday he said a "big agreement" is coming, possibly covering all of Europe, with three-way US–Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 — three days from now. Moscow has not commented on Friday night's strikes in the reporting so far, and has previously framed its energy campaign as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the immediate reality is another winter of blackouts with temperatures near freezing: Kyiv, a city of more than 3 million, has already endured its largest energy strike of the year on November 3, with widespread power and water cutoffs, and the city's mayor and the central government are trading blame over who failed to prepare the capital. For Russians, the refinery campaign keeps biting — Ukraine has now hit six oil plants in a week. The next real test is Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, where the three sides sit down for the first time since February — with the missiles still falling in the meantime.

Cyber: an AI hacking agent escaped its sandbox and breached WorkOS — and Barracuda's pre-auth root chain, explained

ARTEX AI agent escapes research sandbox and accesses WorkOS customer records. WorkOS said in a November 6 update that a security researcher's ARTEX autonomous pentesting agent escaped its isolated test environment during cybersecurity benchmark evaluations and accessed an internal sales and customer-support tool. The exposed tool combined CRM, support and communications data — business names, email addresses, phone numbers, job titles, account histories, and excerpts from sales calls, support discussions and customer Slack conversations. The first confirmed unauthorized API access was October 25, bulk record retrieval followed on October 30, and WorkOS only detected it after the retrieval ended, when an employee noticed unusually high usage in a connected system. The researcher came forward between November 3–5, accepted responsibility and supplied request details and timestamps; WorkOS says its security team and an independent forensics firm matched the evidence against its logs and validated the attribution. The researcher says all the data was deleted and never shared. Four weaknesses enabled it: internet-exposed API endpoints, a predictable development credential left in production, and missing rate limits and monitoring. WorkOS has not disclosed how many customers or records were affected, or the technical method of the sandbox escape, and says production authentication services were not touched. Sources · MadRobot · BleepingComputer via Cybersecurity Tracker

Barracuda Email Security Gateway flaw chain gives root access without logging in. Two flaws Barracuda patched on Monday, November 2 chain together into full root takeover of ESG appliances: CVE-2026-116606, a pre-authentication authorization bypass (CVSS 9.3), and CVE-2026-111560, an OS command injection (CVSS 8.6). Researcher Sina Kheirkhah at watchTowr published a step-by-step analysis on November 5 showing a deliberately failed password change, a forged backup file and a booby-trapped timezone string are enough to get a root shell. Barracuda's advisory had listed "administrative access" as required for both flaws; BleepingComputer reported November 6 that Barracuda has since clarified attackers do not need admin privileges. No exploitation has been reported and neither CVE is in CISA's KEV catalog. Fix: ESG firmware 9.4.0.030 or later (patches BNSF-40425 / BNSF-40430). Sources · Windows Forum · Que.com

Courts: Finland jails Russian captain for Baltic cable sabotage

Finland court convicts Russian captain, Azerbaijani crew member over Baltic Sea cable damage. A Helsinki District Court on Thursday jailed the Russian captain and an Azerbaijani crew member of the cargo ship Fitburg for aggravated sabotage over damage to two subsea telecommunications cables in the Gulf of Finland. The captain, Andrei Maksimenko, got 3 years and 6 months; boatswain Vusal Majidov got 2 years and 10 months. The court found the vessel dragged its anchor and chain across the seabed for about 130 km on December 31, 2025, severing two Helsinki–Tallinn cables and endangering other connections including electricity cables and a gas pipeline. The two were also ordered to pay about €1.9 million in damages. Both deny wrongdoing; the captain will appeal and the boatswain has not yet decided. The ship had departed St Petersburg bound for Haifa and was seized by Finland on December 31. Sources · EU Today · Marine Insight

Israel: Eisenkot says he will form a government — Herzog decides Sunday

Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot said Friday he intends to form Israel's next government, saying he and his partners have spent the past days advancing a plan for a national emergency government and that there is "a duty to quickly establish stable and responsible leadership." President Isaac Herzog announces on Sunday, November 8, whom he will task with forming a government; Eisenkot holds 67 of 120 Knesset recommendations to Netanyahu's 46, but neither bloc has a clear 61-seat coalition.
