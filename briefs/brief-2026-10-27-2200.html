World escalation

Nobody won Israel's election — and both sides claimed the mandate anyway

Hours after the polls closed, Israel's election produced no winner, only two rival claims to lead the country. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called for a "strong, right-wing national government," while challenger Gadi Eisenkot said voters had rejected Netanyahu and called on him to retire from politics. Neither claimed outright victory — because, on every major exit poll, neither bloc has the 61 seats needed to govern.

The numbers barely moved from the evening's exit polls, then tightened further. Reuters reports that revised exit polls from Kan 11 and Channel 13 put the blocs tied at 51–51, while Channel 12 gave Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc a narrow 52–49 edge. With actual votes trickling in, one early-count report put Likud at 25.3% of the vote and Eisenkot's Yashar at 16.6% with about 14% of polling stations counted — early and partial, not a result. Turnout was 77.7%, according to Israel's Central Elections Committee, the highest in more than two decades and about 7 points above 2022. Final results are expected Thursday or Friday.

The two camps read the same deadlock very differently. Netanyahu, speaking after 3 a.m. at Likud headquarters in Jerusalem, framed the moment as a security one: he said Israel faces "a singular historical opportunity, together with our partner President Trump, to remove the existential threat of Iran once and for all," and warned that "ulterior motives must not divert us from this sacred mission." Eisenkot shot back that Netanyahu is "a caretaker prime minister, without a mandate or the people's trust," warned him not to use Israel's security for political purposes, and called on the security establishment to "stand firm" against putting political interests ahead of the country's security. He has also opposed another election, given the high turnout.

The arithmetic is what makes this hard for both men. Netanyahu's nationalist-religious coalition, which at its height held 68 seats, would fall well short of 61, and the far-right religious parties of Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich are projected at a combined 13 seats — no major gains for either. Eisenkot's path is blocked from the other side: the Arab parties outside both blocs, projected at 17–19 seats, could hold the balance of power, but Eisenkot has all but ruled out forming a government with them. That leaves him needing support from parties that will not join him, and Netanyahu needing seats he does not have. As Yohanan Plesner of the Israel Democracy Institute put it, the exit polls are a major defeat for Netanyahu's coalition — but Eisenkot has not emerged as a clear winner, and much could change as votes are counted.

For ordinary Israelis, the practical consequence is that Netanyahu stays on as caretaker prime minister while coalition talks drag on — talks that after past elections have run for weeks or months — and if neither bloc can form a government, another vote may be needed. That leaves the unresolved confrontation with Iran, the Gaza aftermath, and Netanyahu's ongoing corruption trial in the hands of a government without a fresh mandate. The Arab parties' surge, driven by record turnout among Palestinian citizens of Israel, is the night's other lasting fact: at the Joint List headquarters in Nazareth, candidates spoke of blocking Netanyahu's return, with candidate Faten Gattas saying, "We refuse the occupation and racism, and we will remain here."

In short: a record-turnout vote, a deadlocked count, and two men claiming a mandate neither holds yet. The next act is not a victory speech but coalition math — and the final results, due Thursday or Friday, could still shift the picture.

Strikes on Kyiv, a rare call to Putin, and talks booked for Abu Dhabi

While Israel counted votes, the war in Ukraine produced a strange pairing on Tuesday: another night of missiles aimed at Kyiv's bridges and logistics, and a rare diplomatic opening — the leaders of Finland and Norway phoning Vladimir Putin to push US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi in early November.

The strikes came first. Reuters reports Russia hit Kyiv's Southern Bridge with ballistic missiles overnight into Tuesday, part of a barrage of up to 16 Iskander-M or S-400 missiles and more than 280 drones, with Kyiv the main target. Mayor Klitschko confirmed the bridge was hit and closed, suspending road and metro traffic between Osokorky and Chervonyi Khutir; published images showed a large hole in the deck. Ukraine's State Emergency Service said one person was killed and 15 injured in the capital. Zelensky called it "a serious escalation in Russia's campaign targeting our logistics." The same day's toll spread wider: Russian attacks also caused deaths and injuries in Kharkiv and Pavlohrad, a drone strike on a bus in Moscow-held Zaporizhzhia killed one person and injured 30, and a drone attack on the Russian city of Bryansk killed one and wounded six. Russia's Defence Ministry said it had struck transport infrastructure in Kyiv, a logistics hub in the Kharkiv region, and industrial sites and cargo vessels it said served military purposes. Russia and Ukraine both deny deliberately targeting civilians; Reuters said it could not independently verify the reports.

The diplomacy ran in parallel. Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre called Putin from Helsinki on Tuesday to convey messages on future negotiations ahead of the Abu Dhabi meetings, according to a statement from both governments — meetings Kyiv and Moscow agreed on Friday to hold as US-mediated technical talks in early November. Zelensky, who spoke to the pair afterwards, said on X that they had told Putin real steps were needed to de-escalate attacks on Ukraine's energy facilities and infrastructure. Stubb said the war's outcome directly affects the security of Finland and Norway, both of which border Russia, and of Europe more widely: "Ending the war requires direct discussions." The two leaders stressed they acted in their national capacities, not for Europe as a whole, and reaffirmed continued support for Ukraine. The Kremlin's line, as relayed in coverage of the call, is unchanged in substance: any settlement must account for Russia's "legitimate interests," and resuming talks does not restrict ongoing combat operations — a position the strikes on Kyiv the same night did nothing to soften. Some European partners have called the Nordic outreach unilateral, and analysts warn the talks could give Moscow diplomatic cover while military pressure continues.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the two tracks point in opposite directions at once. The bridge closure cuts one of Kyiv's most important Dnipro crossings — road and metro both — with repairs promised but not quick, and the overnight barrage is part of a two-week pattern of concentrated missile strikes on the capital. The Abu Dhabi talks, if they happen, are technical-level meetings, not a peace deal, and the first such US-mediated discussions since February. Meanwhile around Russian-occupied Oleshky, where the local humanitarian ceasefire took effect at 3 a.m. Tuesday, no aid arrival or evacuation had been independently confirmed by Tuesday evening — the wider aid operation is still planned for October 29.

In short: missiles on Kyiv's logistics by night, a phone call to Putin by day, and technical talks booked for early November that neither side has yet allowed to slow the fighting. Watch whether the Oleshky aid operation actually moves on Thursday, and whether Abu Dhabi produces anything beyond a meeting.

Sources: Reuters via Antigua Tribune, October 27; Particle News, October 28; EuropeTimes, October 27

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on Tuesday: a real-time world model that generates ambient audio natively — wind, waves, and characters that talk and react — with scenes editable by plain text and Gemini guiding generation as an agent. Cybersecurity

  • CISA adds the Brotli flaw to its exploited list. CVE-2026-111497 — the out-of-bounds write in Google's Brotli compression decoder, CVSS 9.2 — joined CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 27, with a federal remediation deadline of November 10. This is a step up from its status as of October 26, when it was flagged as a serious flaw with public proof-of-concept code but not yet listed as exploited. Fixes are in Brotli 1.0.10, 1.1.1 and 1.2.1. It joins the Adobe Commerce/Magento flaw (CVE-2026-48356, also due November 10) added the same day and reported in the evening brief. Source: SecurityOnline, October 27
  • PolyShell loader reported on 953 stores. Sansec researchers, via the Carthage October 27 threat brief, report a loader campaign sitting on 953 online stores, mostly Magento, tied to the Adobe Commerce/Magento file-upload flaw now on CISA's exploited list — a sign that stores which never installed Adobe's July fix are still exposed. Single-source report; treat the store count as researchers' finding, not an official tally. Source: Carthage Electronics, October 27
  • Standing deadline: PHP2Shell's federal patch deadline is Thursday, October 29 (CVE-2026-109618/109619, fixes in PHP 8.2.35/8.3.36/8.4.27/8.5.12) — already reported, no rehash needed.
