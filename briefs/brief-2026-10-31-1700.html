World escalation

Ukraine hit Russia's rocket-fuel factory overnight — while Russian strikes left 280,000 households without power

Ukraine's long-range campaign moved from refineries to the munitions chain itself overnight. According to Ukraine's General Staff, reported by the Kyiv Independent via Europe Says, Ukrainian attack drones and Neptune cruise missiles hit the Kamensky Combine in Kamensk-Shakhtinsky, in Russia's Rostov region, overnight on October 31. The General Staff says a finished-goods warehouse caught fire and four production workshops were damaged. The plant makes solid rocket propellant for the Uragan, Smerch and Tornado-S multiple-launch rocket systems, as well as other missile and aircraft weapons, and it has been sanctioned by the EU, UK and US for its role in the war. The same night, Ukraine says it also hit four Russian military supply depots in occupied Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia and Luhansk regions.

Russia has not commented on the Kamensky claim itself — Rostov Governor Yuri Slyusar acknowledged an overnight air attack on Kamensk-Shakhtinsky and four other districts but said nothing about the plant — and the Kyiv Independent notes it could not independently verify the military's claims. That caveat matters, but the plant's role is not in dispute: OSINT project Varta, cited by Militarnyi, describes it as one of the largest chemical plants in southern Russia, producing gunpowder, explosives and ammunition components, and it was already hit by a drone strike on August 24. The General Staff's argument is straightforward: hitting solid rocket propellant production reduces Russia's ability to shell Ukrainian towns with multiple-launch rocket systems. Moscow's side of that argument, repeated throughout the war, is that Ukrainian strikes on its industry are attacks on its war economy — and its own overnight claims ran in the other direction, with the Defence Ministry saying it intercepted 433 Ukrainian drones over 19 regions and struck Kyiv's CHP-6 plant, Kharkiv industrial workshops and a cargo ship off Odesa.

The other half of the night was the toll on Ukrainian civilians. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian strikes left around 200,000 households in the Dnipro region and more than 80,000 in the Kyiv region without electricity — about 280,000 in total — after hits on a combined heat and power plant in Kyiv, four electrical substations and a gas facility in Dnipropetrovsk, a power plant in Shostka, and railway infrastructure in Dnipro and Kharkiv. Kyiv's mayor had already confirmed three drone strikes on CHP-6 on Friday night, and on Saturday evening a 12-year-old girl was injured in Kyiv's Obolon district when debris from a projectile fell on a shopping centre, according to the Kyiv City Military Administration. Ukraine's General Staff counted 173 engagements on Saturday, heaviest around Kostyantynivka and Pokrovsk. This is the third night in a row of the resumed deep-strike campaign — Novokuibyshevsk on Thursday night, the Bashneft-Novoil refinery in Ufa on Friday night, Kamensky now — and it comes as winter approaches, when power outages stop being an inconvenience and start being a survival issue.

In short: Ukraine says it damaged a key rocket-propellant plant and four supply depots overnight, Russia claims a huge drone interception and its own strikes on Kyiv's power plant, and the confirmed civilian cost is 280,000 households without power plus a child injured in Kyiv — the energy war and the munitions war now running in parallel.

Sources: Europe Says / Kyiv Independent on the Kamensky strike, OSINT Baltic security monitor, Particle news roundup, Kyiv24 on the Obolon injury, Defense and Tech on Russia's overnight claims

Iran says Hormuz could reopen in two days — but only on its terms, and Washington hasn't agreed to them

The most consequential diplomatic development of the day is also the most conditional. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Al Jazeera on Saturday that talks with Qatari mediators have produced a proposal cutting the implementation time for Iran's plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to two days — down from a Qatari suggestion of five. But the plan comes with Tehran's full list of conditions attached: lifting sanctions imposed after the Islamabad memorandum, reducing US forces in the region, restoring oil waivers, releasing all of Iran's frozen funds including money held in Qatar, and recognition of the shipping routes Iran discussed with Oman, with other routes closed. Araghchi called a letter from US Vice President JD Vance "a positive step" toward dialogue, then immediately added that Iran sees "no need to meet with the Americans at this stage" and dismissed reports of a meeting as "media speculation."

That directly tempers the other Saturday reporting. The Qatari newspaper Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, cited by The Media Line, reported that an American delegation is expected in Doha after more than 12 hours of Qatari-Iranian talks in Tehran produced preliminary understandings on Iran's nuclear programme, Hormuz navigation and financial incentives, with China and Russia expected as guarantors. A separate draft framework reported by Al Arabiya English goes further, suggesting Iran's highly enriched uranium stockpile could be transferred to a third country or reduced in enrichment, with Hormuz reopening within seven days of an initial understanding. Neither government has publicly confirmed any of this, and an Axios correspondent cited by Iran International said Tehran and Washington have not agreed to resume direct talks. An Iranian delegation is in Doha, but according to the Revolutionary Guards-linked Tasnim agency, it is there to advance talks with Qatar, not to negotiate with US officials.

Meanwhile the strait itself stayed dangerous. UKMTO reported a tanker struck near Iran's Lavan Island on Saturday; Iran's Revolutionary Guards said it hit a mine after using an "unauthorised route." On Friday, UKMTO reported a small boat attaching limpet mines to a tanker, causing an engine-room fire — described by Reuters via CNBC as the first known successful limpet-mine strike in the waterway since the war began in February. And the market has already priced in the risk: Brent's December contract settled Friday at $117.64 a barrel, up 2.4%, its highest since late April, with the January contract at $113.43; WTI settled at $104.12. Brent finished October up 13.6% and WTI up 15.2%. US average regular gas was about $4.18 a gallon on Saturday, according to AAA, cited by US Brief.

For ordinary people, the gap between the two stories is the whole story. A two-day reopening on Iran's terms would ease fuel prices fast; a failed mediation, three days before the US midterms on November 3, leaves the post-election military option that both sides have been warning about squarely on the table. OPEC+ meets online on Sunday, which may add its own signal before markets reopen.

Caption: A file photo of Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi meeting his Qatari counterpart in Tehran in 2024 — Qatar is again the mediator, this time shuttling between Tehran and Washington over Hormuz and Iran's nuclear programme, and Araghchi's Saturday interview is the clearest public statement yet of what Tehran wants in return for reopening the strait.

In short: Iran says Hormuz could reopen in two days if the US lifts sanctions, pulls back forces and frees frozen funds — but says there are no plans to meet US officials yet, reports of a Doha breakthrough are unconfirmed by either government, and oil just closed October at its highest since April.

The US ordered its non-emergency staff out of Saudi Arabia as Houthi drones kept coming — four intercepted in a day

The US Mission to Saudi Arabia disclosed on Friday that it has ordered the departure of non-emergency government employees and their families from Saudi Arabia, an order that took effect on October 23, citing safety risks from Houthi attacks on civilian airports and critical infrastructure that have caused casualties and major travel disruptions. US government staff must now get special authorisation to use Saudi airports. The embassy noted Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport has partially reopened, while Jeddah and Dammam remain operational with some delays and cancellations. The order is the clearest public US signal yet of how seriously Washington takes the Houthi campaign — even as the US has held off its own strikes since the May 2025 ceasefire.

The attacks behind it continued through Saturday. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted four Houthi drones over Medina, Riyadh, and southern and eastern Saudi Arabia on October 30–31, according to coalition spokesperson Turki al Maliki, reported by ISW. The Houthis denied on October 30 that they targeted Medina and have not claimed responsibility for these drone attacks. On the ground in Yemen, government-aligned forces repelled Houthi advances along the Taiz–Aden road in Lahij on October 30–31 with Saudi close air support, and recaptured high ground overlooking al Hazm in Jawf. A separate investigation by Geneva firm AURORAH Geosignal Intelligence, reported by Europe Says, claims Russian satellites were observed over Saudi military and energy targets before and after Houthi strikes — a single-source claim worth noting, not yet corroborated, and it does not change the immediate picture for travellers: airports disrupted, an evacuation order in force, and no sign the drone campaign is slowing.

In short: Washington has formally pulled its non-essential people out of Saudi Arabia, the coalition says it downed four Houthi drones in a day, and fighting inside Yemen is heating up along the Taiz–Aden road — while a claim of Russian satellite help for Houthi targeting remains unverified.

Sources: Cumhuriyet Daily on the US departure order, ISW Iran Update, October 31, Europe Says on the AURORAH satellite claim

Open source

  • AiSOC — open-source SOC with AI agents, v20.4.0: AiSOC is a self-hosted Security Operations Center under the MIT licence: it collects logs, correlates them into incidents and hands them to AI agents that record every prompt and tool call. Started May 2026, it counts 5,489 stars and 561 forks as of October 31, with v20.4.0 out that day. Worth a look for a home lab or small SOC; note there is no independent security audit on record, and the version number has raced from 8.1.0 in September to 20.4.0, which says more about release pace than maturity. Source: Pasquale Pillitteri
  • MiniCPM-OCR — open document-parsing models: ModelBest and OpenBMB open-sourced the MiniCPM-OCR series on October 31: MiniCPM-OCR 1.0 (1.3B) and MiniCPM-OCR Jev (2B) for document parsing and parsing-quality assessment, on GitHub, Hugging Face and ModelScope, with vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and Ollama support plus fine-tuning code. Useful if you scan or parse documents locally instead of sending them to a cloud API. Source: PR Newswire via Morningstar
  • jakubkrehel/skills — 14 agent skills for interface review: A design engineer turned his interface-review method into 14 Markdown agent skills (9,398 stars, created July 2026, latest commit October 25) that plug into a coding agent — the standout in the October 31 Open Source Radar roundup, alongside the already-covered Mika Android agent. Source: DEV Open Source Radar, Oct 31

Cybersecurity

  • Binance says 90% of the $6.3B theft is replaced; withdrawals targeted for Sunday: Binance says it has replenished 90% of the cryptocurrency stolen on Thursday (up from ~80%), with its top 10 user assets — BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and ZEC — now fully backed at 100%, funded from corporate treasury, its futures insurance fund and a partial SAFU deployment expected within 24 hours. Withdrawals, paused since the hack, are on track to reopen in phases on Sunday, November 1, subject to security checks. A new proof-of-reserves snapshot (balances at midnight UTC October 31) shows 100%+ coverage for those tokens, but the full cryptographic proof users can independently verify is not due until November 3 — so Sunday's reopening comes before outside verification. Sources: CoinDesk, CryptoBriefing
  • Oracle E-Business Suite flaw now seeing exploitation attempts: CVE-2026-101767 (CVSS 9.8) lets someone with no login run SQL as the main APPS database user and then run commands on the server. Oracle fixed it in its October 20 Critical Patch Update; a week later, decoy servers started catching attackers trying it, and Shadowserver counts more than 670 internet-facing EBS instances. If you run EBS, this is the patch — it is not yet in CISA's KEV catalog as of October 31. Source: Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, Oct 31
  • China-linked Vapor Panda used an open-source AI pentest tool against 200+ organisations: CrowdStrike reports that the state-backed group Vapor Panda used the ARTEX agentic AI penetration-testing framework (paired mainly with DeepSeek and a second commercial model) to target more than 200 organisations across at least 14 countries since October 17, with about 140 showing compromise or moderate-confidence intrusion evidence in 11 days. Victims include foreign ministries, a European presidency and prime minister's office, defence/intelligence agencies, 19 US organisations in pharma, semiconductors, defence, nuclear and finance, and telecoms in the US, Belgium and the Philippines. The campaign is still active — and it is a case study in why an open-source offensive tool cuts both ways. Source: Cyber Recaps, Oct 31
  • Backdoored Claude Code extension reached up to 5,000 developers: A malicious version of Anthropic's Claude Code extension (versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313) appeared in Cursor's extension marketplace for roughly 11 hours on October 29 and was downloaded by at most 5,000 users across Windows, macOS and Linux. The tampered package was not built or published by Anthropic — an attacker gained access to publish it under the legitimate extension identity. If you installed or updated Claude Code in Cursor around October 29, verify your extension version. Source: Cyber Recaps, Oct 31
  • TeamViewer flaw lets the supported computer attack the technician + Android "SMS of Death" PoC: TeamViewer CVE-2026-111585 (CVSS 8.8, fixed in 15.83) reverses the usual direction — the computer being supported can attack the technician who connects to it. Separately, a public proof of concept for an Android "SMS of Death" (CVE-2026-58882) that crashes unpatched phones went public on October 31. Patch both. Source: Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, Oct 31
  • Deadlines landing now: No new KEV additions on October 31. Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418 and GitLab CVE-2026-97229 are due Sunday, November 1; Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-111357 is due Monday, November 2. Source: Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, Oct 31
