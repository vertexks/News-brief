Morning brief — Thursday, November 12, 2026

Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia — resigned as CEO after 33 years

Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive and a director of Nvidia, effective immediately, the company announced on Thursday morning. The board accepted his resignation on Wednesday, according to a regulatory filing, ending a five-day crisis that began when Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman US officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence. Chief financial officer Colette Kress takes over as interim CEO while keeping her finance job, and Harvey Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive who has sat on Nvidia's board since 1993, becomes the company's first-ever board chair.

The resignation came hours after Nvidia's board met late on Wednesday night to decide Huang's future. Reuters reported the meeting citing a source familiar with the matter, and CBS News, citing two sources, reported the board was preparing to remove him. The Wall Street Journal first reported on Wednesday that the board was preparing to oust him, hours after The New York Times reported a second affair, with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports to Huang. The first affair, with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed whom US officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence, was admitted by Huang in a staff memo on Sunday. Bloomberg reported the board has been working with an outside firm to identify potential replacements.

The company's announcement gave no reason for the departure, instead thanking Huang for his contributions over 33 years. "Jensen's contributions to NVIDIA are profound. NVIDIA is one of the most consequential technology companies in the world. The Board and the company thank Jensen for his immeasurable contributions to the company and the industry," the company said. The board said operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue without interruption, and the fiscal third-quarter earnings report stays on Tuesday, November 17.

What each side is saying has split along market-versus-company lines. Investors have made up their minds: Nvidia shares were down nearly 12% in early trading on Thursday, and have fallen more than 19% since Friday, wiping out more than $855 billion in market value, according to TechCrunch. CNBC recorded a 10.4% premarket drop. "The market is starting to price Nvidia without its founder as the likely outcome, not a tail risk," portfolio manager Armina Rosenberg of Minotaur Capital told Reuters on Wednesday. "Losing Jensen would be a real blow." Prediction market Polymarket had moved to an 88% chance Huang resigns or is removed by the end of the year, with odds for a successor favouring Ian Buck, who runs the data-centre business. The fallout spread to the companies Nvidia bankrolls: CoreWeave fell about 9% on Thursday, Nebius 16% and IREN 6.6%, while rivals AMD and Broadcom rose after hours on Wednesday.

The deeper worry is succession, not just scandal. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and was its only CEO, running a famously flat organisation with around 60 direct reports and no heir apparent or succession plan — though two of his children work in executive roles. He owns about 3.5% of the company and, unlike most Silicon Valley founders, holds no supervoting rights, so the board could vote him out at any time. TechCrunch notes the stakes go beyond one man: as of the end of the second quarter Nvidia reported $99 billion in investments plus $25 billion in commitments, $36 billion in cloud service commitments, and up to $105 billion in credit guarantees for an OpenAI data centre campus in Ohio — a web of financing for the AI buildout that now has no clear steward. Congress is still in the picture too: the House chairs' request for Huang to appear at a closed-door briefing on November 18 is now in limbo, and Axios reported Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick stalled President Trump's crackdown on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China.

For ordinary people the impact is still mostly market, not product: no interruption to GeForce drivers, product shipments or developer programs has been announced, and demand for Nvidia's chips is not expected to drop in the near term. But analysts warned the wider universe of companies Nvidia finances — largely customers building data centres that will use its chips — could suffer if the turmoil drags on. "In these types of booms or bubbles, sometimes all it takes is a slowdown to shift it into the bust phase," Nick Ferres of Vantage Point Asset Management told Reuters. The next hard dates are earnings on Tuesday, November 17 and the requested Capitol Hill briefing on November 18.

In short: Huang resigned Thursday after the board met Wednesday night; Kress is interim CEO and Jones is the first board chair. The company gave no reason, but the market read it as an ouster — shares down nearly 12% in early trading and more than $855 billion in value gone since Friday.

Russia hit Kyiv overnight as US envoys met Putin in Moscow — no outcome announced yet

Russia launched a large combined overnight attack on Ukraine starting at 18:00 on Wednesday, striking Kyiv and multiple regions with ballistic missiles, anti-ship missiles and 123 strike drones, hours before US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff sat down with Vladimir Putin in Moscow. In Kyiv's Solomyanskyi district a Russian drone hit a nine-storey residential building, setting floors three through six ablaze and killing a 44-year-old woman; five people were injured, Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said. Ukrainian air defences reported 94 targets neutralised by 08:00, with damage recorded across eight regions. Russia said it downed five Ukrainian drones heading for Moscow and 71 across Russia overnight.

The details show a deliberate escalation on the night the diplomacy resumed. Russia's Defence Ministry said it had hit a rocket factory in Kyiv and a drone assembly workshop and missile fuel factory in the wider Kyiv region, as well as electricity substations in the western Zhytomyr region, and local officials reported damage to energy and civilian infrastructure in the north-western Rivne region. Ukrainian reporting, via h.ua, says the attack used Iskander-M, KN-23 and S-400 ballistic missiles, Tsirkon anti-ship missiles and 123 strike drones, including 73 jet-powered Shaheds. Moscow's claims about which military facilities were destroyed could not be independently verified. The timing matters: the strikes landed the same night the Abu Dhabi talks ended with only a prisoner-swap agreement, and hours before the envoys' Moscow meeting.

In Moscow, Kushner and Witkoff arrived on Thursday for what Reuters called the latest push for a peace deal, the 10th time Witkoff has met Putin in person. According to RIA Novosti, Witkoff told Putin that Donald Trump was seeking to end the war as quickly as possible, and described the Abu Dhabi discussions as having taken place in a constructive spirit. Kushner discussed preliminary conversations with European countries about the continent's postwar security arrangements, and Witkoff said he hoped to announce "shortly, hopefully maybe even at the end of this meeting, the next trilateral meeting that will include the Europeans." Putin responded that Moscow had never objected to dialogue with European governments, and said he wanted a peace that would be "durable and lasting… eternal, for good." Putin also proposed a humanitarian initiative involving Kostyantynivka, a frontline city in eastern Ukraine. Axios reports the US is pushing a new Europe-Russia security framework as part of the peace push, with the envoys expected to ask Russia to submit a proposal for a security agreement with Europe and to allow European representatives into the next round of talks.

What each side is saying has not moved. Kyiv has strongly rejected Putin's demands to surrender the whole of the eastern Donbas region in return for peace, and with talks deadlocked on land, the prisoner swap — 1,000-for-1,000, possibly 1,500, the biggest of the war — remains the only concrete outcome of the Abu Dhabi round, with numbers to be finalised in Moscow. Medinsky, Russia's chief negotiator, said the sides had "moved closer to peace" but that Moscow's baseline demands were unchanged. No outcome statement from the Moscow meeting had been published by the time of this brief; the envoys are due in Dublin on Thursday night to meet Zelenskyy, who is attending the sidelines of an EU leaders' summit there.

For ordinary people in Ukraine the night meant more of the same grinding toll: another residential building hit, another death in the capital, and more energy infrastructure damaged as winter approaches, with power outages already running into the hundreds of thousands in the east after the grid campaign of the past two weeks. For everyone else the stakes run through energy and shipping: the war's escalation and the Hormuz campaign together have kept oil volatile, with Brent around $92–93 this week.

In short: Russia hit Kyiv with missiles and drones overnight, killing one woman in an apartment block, on the same day US envoys met Putin to push a Europe-Russia security framework and a next trilateral round including Europeans — but no outcome from the Moscow meeting had been announced yet, and the envoys head to Dublin tonight to brief Zelenskyy.

Hormuz — IRGC claims two supertankers exploded Wednesday night; no names, unverified

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Thursday that two supertankers carrying more than 4 million barrels of oil combined were disabled by massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday night after ignoring warnings against using an "unauthorised" southern shipping route. Iranian state media later said the tankers had exploded after hitting naval mines, though the Guards' own statement did not specify the cause. No vessel names, flags, owners or crew conditions have been given, and UKMTO has issued no matching confirmation. The Guards warned the same fate awaits any vessel that turns off its navigation systems and cooperates with the US military on the southern route. Treat the claim as unverified — it rests entirely on an Iranian military statement. (Reuters via MarineLink · PressTV · Vocemundi)

Houthi/Saudi — debris from intercepted missiles fell in the Mecca region

Saudi Arabia said debris from two ballistic missiles intercepted by its air defences fell in the Mecca region on Wednesday, as the kingdom and the Houthis continued trading attacks. Saudi state television identified the locations as Sabuhah village, about 35 km northeast of the Grand Mosque in Mecca, and Wadi al-Maghmas, about 21 km east of the mosque. The Houthis have repeatedly denied Saudi accusations that they target Mecca and Medina, saying their military operations are directed at military and strategic targets elsewhere in Saudi Arabia. This is follow-up detail to Wednesday's intercepts already covered in the evening brief (three cruise missiles toward Riyadh, two drones toward Mecca and the south, two missiles west — all intercepted, no casualties). No new Houthi attacks were reported overnight. (Reuters via FroggyWeb)

KEV watch — the November 9 four are due today; no new additions

No new CISA KEV additions were published overnight. The federal patch deadline for the four flaws added November 9 falls today, November 12: SSHole (CVE-2026-123158, OpenSSH), GPUStack auth bypass (CVE-2026-102895, CVSS 9.8 — GPUStack's advisory says it was exploited against a 2.1.1 deployment, where attackers created backends running reverse shells), FreeSWITCH code injection (CVE-2026-116779) and IBM Aspera Faspex (CVE-2026-123218). The Barracuda pair is due tomorrow, November 13; the six added November 10 (including the Chrome zero-day CVE-2026-125350 and Outlook CVE-2026-124194) are due November 24. The KEV catalog now lists 1,779 entries. (Pandromeda · ImmunitySec KEV)

Courts

Netanyahu trial — judges order the Attorney General to appear next Wednesday

The panel hearing Benjamin Netanyahu's corruption trial ordered Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara on Thursday to appear at the Jerusalem District Court on Wednesday, November 18, at 9:30 a.m. to discuss how the proceedings are being managed and the options for moving them forward. The decision follows her refusal to attend a proposed discussion, conveyed to the court by prosecutors a day earlier. This follows the defence's request to summon her after former Attorney General Avichai Mandelblit's testimony about the authorisation of investigative actions in Cases 1000, 2000 and 4000; prosecutor Yehudit Tirosh has pushed back against the summons while signalling openness to a negotiated resolution. (JBizNews · Haaretz notification)

FTC v. Amazon — unredacted ads complaint: prices raised up to 50%, 250% on Prime Day

The FTC and 22 states filed an updated, unredacted version of their complaint against Amazon over its online search advertising auction, alleging Amazon secretly raises advertising prices by as much as 50% above auction results on ordinary days and by as much as 250% on major shopping days like Prime Day or Black Friday, misleading advertisers about the true cause. The suit was filed in August in the Western District of Washington. Separately, the economics hearing in the FTC's antitrust case continued, with Judge Chun pressing the FTC on consumer harm; the FTC conceded it cannot quantify a but-for world. The Prime "dark patterns" jury trial continues in Seattle. (FTC · Typefully — economics hearing)

No fresh developments in the Murdaugh retrial (next hearing Friday, November 13).
