World escalation

Israel votes — and nobody wins: exit polls point to deadlock

Israelis voted Tuesday in their first election since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attacks, and the exit polls say the country is split almost exactly down the middle. Neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor challenger Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc is projected to reach the 61 seats needed to govern, setting up days or weeks of coalition wrangling — and possibly another election.

Turnout was the story of the day before any seat count: USA Today reports at least 80% of eligible voters cast ballots, a record high, with 74.3% already counted by 8 p.m. local time, two hours before polls closed. The exit polls published after voting ended were strikingly consistent across the major broadcasters. Channel 12 put Netanyahu's bloc at 50 seats and Eisenkot's bloc at 52; the Kan public broadcaster had it 50–53. Likud remains the largest single party at 24–25 seats, ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar at 21–23 — but a bloc, not a party, forms a government. The big mover was the Arab-led parties, projected at 17 seats, up sharply, making them potentially decisive in coalition talks. One outlier broke the pattern: Channel 14, a network generally supportive of Netanyahu, projected his bloc at 60 seats and Eisenkot's at 43, with Likud at 30 — a reminder that exit polls are projections, not results, and the picture can shift as votes are counted overnight.

The two camps read the same numbers very differently. Eisenkot said in a statement: "If these are the final results, they would mark a victory." Fellow opposition leader Yair Lapid went further, calling it a "total collapse" for Netanyahu's bloc and saying "the public told them to go home." Netanyahu, who voted in Jerusalem with his wife Sara, spent the day warning supporters that the election was being stolen, pointing to the arrests of three men in the Negev suspected of trying to buy votes for the Islamist Ra'am party, with investigators finding envelopes of tens of thousands of shekels and ballot slips. He also appealed urgently as reports emerged of unusually high turnout among Arab voters. Eisenkot's camp, by contrast, framed the vote as a verdict on the government of October 7 and the failure to establish an independent commission of inquiry.

For ordinary Israelis, the immediate consequence is uncertainty, not a new government. If neither bloc reaches 61, leaders must court smaller parties outside their coalitions — including the Arab parties — or Netanyahu could remain as caretaker prime minister while talks drag on. Preliminary results are due as early as Wednesday, and the coalition math, not the vote count alone, will decide who governs through the unresolved confrontation with Iran, the Gaza aftermath, and Netanyahu's ongoing corruption trial.

In short: a record-turnout vote that changed the balance — Netanyahu's bloc lost its majority, Eisenkot's bloc edged ahead — but gave nobody a government. The next act is coalition negotiation, with the Arab parties holding unusual leverage.

Sources: USA Today, October 27; Algemeiner, October 27; New York Post, October 27; The Sun, October 27

Houthis claim a missile hit Saudi Arabia's biggest refinery — as Tehran talks and oil markets pull in opposite directions

Yemen's Houthis said Tuesday they hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery — the kingdom's largest — with a ballistic missile, while diplomats from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan were in Tehran trying to de-escalate the wider regional war. Oil fell on the diplomacy, but stayed near six-month highs on the attacks.

Reuters reports Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the strike caused a fire at the Ras Tanura complex, which includes a shipping terminal on the Gulf and refines 550,000 barrels a day. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the strike. Bloomberg News, cited by Reuters, reported a person familiar with the matter as saying Saudi Arabia had contained a fire near Ras Tanura's terminal in the early hours. The Houthis also claimed attacks on two military transport jets at Najran Airport and an air base in Khamis Mushait — neither confirmed by Riyadh. Separately, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched toward Riyadh, and civil defence authorities briefly issued an air raid alert in the capital Tuesday afternoon. On Monday, the Mecca defence pact countries — Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan — met Iranian officials in Tehran to try to de-escalate the overall conflict, and on Tuesday Iranian Security Council chief Mohsen Rezaei met Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei; Iranian state media said the four countries agreed to continue talks on peace and security.

The two sides' accounts diverge in the usual way: the Houthis present the refinery strike as a direct hit with a fire; Riyadh has not confirmed it, and the Bloomberg-sourced account describes a contained fire near the terminal rather than a refinery outage. What is not in dispute is the pattern — Houthi strikes on Saudi airports, ports and oil installations have intensified since September, and nearly all Saudi oil exports flow through Ras Tanura and the Red Sea port of Yanbu, both previously attacked. Market analysts at Gelber & Associates put it plainly: "Continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and the expanding military presence in the Gulf are keeping supply risks in place, even as diplomatic headlines encourage some of the recent premium to unwind."

For ordinary people, the price signal is the one that reaches the pump. Reuters reports Brent crude settled down $1.93, or 1.67%, at $113.88 a barrel on Tuesday, with US West Texas Intermediate down $1.86 at $99.90 — a pullback on the Tehran talks, but still near six-month highs. Swiss bank UBS now expects Brent to stay elevated near $110 through year-end, up from a previous $100 forecast. October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways since a Washington think tank began tracking maritime incidents in 2017.

In short: a claimed strike on the kingdom's biggest refinery, an unconfirmed but contained fire, missiles intercepted over Riyadh — and, in parallel, the first real diplomatic channel in weeks. Oil eased on the talks but is not coming down while the attacks continue.

Sources: Reuters via WNCY, October 27; Reuters via Devdiscourse, October 27; Reuters via Euronext, October 27

Tech products

  • Oracle expands Nvidia deal to "multi-gigawatt scale" — and starts offering Google's TPUs. Reuters reports Oracle said Tuesday it will expand its cloud AI chip deal with Nvidia's newest Vera Rubin servers to multi-gigawatt scale (no value disclosed; Nvidia's Jensen Huang has previously said Vera Rubin systems can generate up to $40 billion per gigawatt for Nvidia). The notable twist: Oracle will skip some Nvidia networking chips in favor of its own in-house "Acceleron" chips, and will begin offering Google's tensor processing units for the first time, alongside inference chips from Cerebras and Intel-backed SambaNova. Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman called it "a massive expansion of our customer base of tier-one customers." The read: even Nvidia's biggest cloud customers are diversifying, especially for inference, where Nvidia's grip is looser than in training. Source: Reuters via WTBX, October 27
  • Apple's touchscreen Mac event is Wednesday — what to expect. Apple's "Pro and tell" event in Los Angeles starts 10 a.m. Pacific Wednesday. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pros with touch-enabled OLED displays — a first for any Mac — plus a Dynamic Island replacing the notch, lighter builds, and the existing M5 Pro and M5 Max chips from March rather than new silicon. Pricing could run up to $500 above current models ($2,499 for 14-inch, $2,999 for 16-inch). macOS Golden Gate is said to add a "dynamic interface" that adapts controls based on whether you're touching the screen or using the trackpad. The M6 14-inch MacBook Pro ($1,999) and iMac ($1,499) from Tuesday remain the conventional options, on sale November 6. Sources: MacRumors, October 25; Digital Trends, October 27

  • Debloat your Android TV over Wi-Fi. A restore script undoes every step, with tested lists for Xiaomi, TCL, Sony and more. Up 2,616 stars today on GitHub trending. Source: GhTrends, October 27

Up 1,879 stars today; which drives keep working varies as the sites update. Source: GhTrends, October 27

  • TradingAgents: a simulated trading firm of LLM agents. TauricResearch/TradingAgents — a multi-agent LLM financial-trading framework that simulates a real trading firm, with specialized agents assessing market conditions and guiding decisions. Up 1,395 stars today, now at 116.2k. Source: GitDiscover, October 27

Cybersecurity

  • CISA adds Adobe Commerce/Magento flaw to KEV. CVE-2026-48356 — an unrestricted upload of a file with dangerous type in Adobe Commerce and Magento, CVSS 9.3, added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog October 27 with a federal remediation deadline of November 10. It can lead to arbitrary code execution, but exploitation requires user interaction (a victim visiting a maliciously crafted URL). Affected: Adobe Commerce up to 2.4.9 / 2.4.8-p5 / 2.4.7-p10 and matching lines, Magento Open Source up to 2.4.9; fixed in the 2026-jul releases. Source: mAlice.ai, October 27
  • GitLab CVSS 10.0 — unauthenticated RCE, no exploitation stated yet. CVE-2026-110264 in GitLab CE/EE, rated 10.0, allows unauthenticated remote code execution. Update internet-facing servers first and assess for compromise. Source: SecureResearch, October 27
  • Also on CISA's exploited list per today's brief: Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-prem) SQL injection that can compromise the orchestrator host, and the Collabora Online built-in CODE server (Nextcloud) OS command injection leading to RCE — both listed as exploited; apply vendor mitigations. BeyondTrust Remote Support exploitation also flagged in the same brief. Source: SecureResearch, October 27
  • Standing patch priorities unchanged: PHP2Shell (federal deadline October 29 — CISA confirmed active exploitation Monday), Node.js Brotli CVE-2026-111497, and the HPE Aruba AOS flaws from last week.
