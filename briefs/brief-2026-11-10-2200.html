Abu Dhabi talks end day one with warm words and no deal

The first three-way talks between Ukraine, Russia, and the United States in nine months wrapped up their first day in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday with both sides talking past each other in public: Ukraine's president called the session "constructive and quite substantive," while Russia said nothing at all, and no joint statement was issued. The delegations meet again on Wednesday.

The talks ran about four hours inside the Emirates Palace hotel, with a roughly 40-minute break, according to Le Monde. They opened with separate meetings between Ukraine's team and European and American officials, followed by the trilateral session itself. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner led the American side; Rustem Umerov led Ukraine's delegation, with presidential office chief Kyrylo Budanov also at the table. Notably, Budanov sat across from Dmitry Minaev, the FSB counterintelligence chief whom Ukrainian intelligence says orchestrated a 2019 assassination attempt against him — a detail Le Monde reads as Moscow signaling dominance rather than compromise.

The substance, as far as either side has described it: Zelensky said on X after being briefed by his team that the talks covered prisoner releases, security guarantees for Ukraine, and "mechanisms to block any recurrence of the war." Speaking to reporters in Copenhagen later in the evening, he said a prisoner swap of 1,000 for 1,000 — possibly 1,500 for 1,500 — was discussed, and that Kyiv has offered a ceasefire at sea and on energy sites and is waiting for Moscow to answer. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who was not in Abu Dhabi, called the first day "very productive." Zelensky said his team has "clear instructions" for day two, when de-escalation, energy, the Black Sea, the prisoner exchange, and a possible leaders' meeting are on the agenda.

What each side argues: Kyiv and its European allies insist on a ceasefire first, then security guarantees — a joint European-American statement on Tuesday put that in writing, insisting on respect for Ukraine's "sovereignty and territorial integrity." Russia, per Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, will not agree to an immediate ceasefire without knowing what comes after it, and the Kremlin continues to demand Ukraine cede the Donbas, which it controls roughly 80–85% of. The NY Post notes Russia has been unable to take the rest of the Donetsk fortress belt by force, while Ukraine fears surrendering it would open the way for a future advance. Le Monde reports the U.S. envoys have been floating a new European security architecture that would accommodate Russia — language that echoes Putin's goal of rolling back NATO's reach — and that the E3 (Germany, France, and the U.K.) traveled to Abu Dhabi to make sure Witkoff and Kushner didn't offer further concessions for a hasty settlement. Trump, per Rubio, has made resolving the war "a priority" after the November 3 midterm defeat, with a new Congress hostile to his administration taking office in January.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the talks changed nothing on the ground: Russia's grid campaign continued into a second night, with 850,000+ people without power in the east as of the evening brief, and scheduled outages are set to last at least through Monday as repair crews work through the damage. The war is set to mark its five-year anniversary next February.

In short: day one produced a meeting, not a breakthrough — a prisoner swap and a sea-and-energy ceasefire are on the table, the ceasefire-first question is still unresolved, and the real test is whether Wednesday's session moves past warm adjectives.

Sources: NY Post, Le Monde, AzerNews, Reuters

Hormuz: Iran claims a third tanker hit a mine — still unverified

IRGC claims third tanker struck a mine in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Navy said Tuesday afternoon that a third oil tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine on an "unauthorized route" in the eastern Strait of Hormuz, and was left burning. Earlier Tuesday it said two other tankers were left ablaze after explosions on the same route — one described as a supertanker that struck a mine, the other immobilized under U.S. military escort. The IRGC named no tanker, flag, or crew in any of the three claims, gave no casualty figures, and none of the incidents has been independently confirmed. The IRGC also claims U.S. coastal radar and navigation systems south of the strait were destroyed, raising the risk for ships on unauthorized lanes. The U.S. answer to Iran's seven conditions is still due, and Tehran denied a report of an $8B frozen-assets-for-Hormuz deal. Brent traded around $91 a barrel Tuesday, WTI around $85.

Sources: PressTV, safety4sea, Unbiased Headlines

CISA adds six actively exploited flaws to its KEV catalog

Six new CISA KEV entries, November 10 — patch now. CISA added six vulnerabilities with evidence of active exploitation: an Outlook flaw (CVE-2026-124194) that leaks NTLM hashes, a Chrome zero-day (CVE-2026-125350, use-after-free, fixed in Chrome 157.0.8103.50), two Barracuda flaws (CVE-2026-116606, CVSS 9.3 pre-auth auth bypass in Email Security Gateway, and CVE-2026-111560, CVSS 8.6 OS command injection across six Barracuda products — Barracuda confirmed exploitation hitting a small number of orgs on Nov 9), Cisco BroadWorks CVE-2026-20320, and Openwave Messaging Webtop CVE-2026-111793. Federal deadlines: Barracuda flaws by November 13, the other four by November 24. This supersedes the evening brief's "no new KEV additions" note — the additions landed after it.

Sources: SecurityOnline, Windows Forum, Circuits

Anthropic IPO roadshow launches — up to $64.4B at a $1.02T valuation

Anthropic's IPO roadshow is officially underway. Reuters reports the company launched the roadshow Tuesday, aiming to raise as much as $64.4 billion by selling 825 million shares at $70–$78, targeting a valuation of up to $1.02 trillion — $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. A separate $26 billion private placement is lined up with cornerstone investors including Google ($10B), Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft ($1B) and TSMC. Reuters calculates Amazon's and Google's existing Anthropic stakes could be worth a combined $411 billion at the top of the range. Pricing is expected next week. Separately on Tuesday, Anthropic announced Claude Meetings, bringing Claude into Google Meet calls as a visible participant for Team and Enterprise customers. The earlier $69.3B figure from Monday's draft filing was retracted by the company as inaccurate.

Sources: Reuters, Reuters, Reuters

Nvidia names the lawyer running the Huang investigation

Nvidia's board retained Joon H. Kim of Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead the independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang. Kim is a former acting U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York who previously co-led the investigation into former New York governor Andrew Cuomo. The board announced the probe Sunday after reports Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort through a relationship with a woman U.S. officials allege has Chinese intelligence links; Huang admitted infidelity in an email to staff. The board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but that is a preliminary position, not a finding. Huang stays on as CEO during the investigation. Nvidia shares fell 4.2% Tuesday to $171.16. The House briefing answer is due Wednesday, November 11; earnings are November 17.

Sources: NY Post, Morningstar / Dow Jones, JBizNews

FTC v. Amazon: both sides move for summary judgment in the Prime case

Amazon and the FTC have both moved for summary judgment in the Prime "dark patterns" case. Amazon is seeking dismissal; the FTC is seeking liability. The case — separate from the antitrust trial set for March 2027 — alleges Amazon enrolled 35M+ people in Prime without consent, and the FTC is seeking to hold three Amazon executives personally liable for potentially over $1B in penalties. The jury trial is expected this fall. (The economics hearing in the antitrust case was covered in the evening brief.)

Source: Typefully / Vidushi Dyall

Google DeepMind used AI to mathematically verify Signal's encryption

Google DeepMind researchers used an AI-assisted verification system to mathematically confirm several important security properties of the Signal encryption protocol, according to the day's tech roundup — a new approach to proving software security rather than just testing it. Details are still thin in public coverage; worth watching for the full paper.

Source: TechStartups
