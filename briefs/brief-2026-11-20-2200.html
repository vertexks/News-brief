Night brief — Friday, November 20, 2026

Houthis fire three ballistic missiles at Riyadh — the second attack on the Saudi capital in two days

The Saudi-led coalition said early Saturday it intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles fired by Yemen's Houthis toward Riyadh, the second Houthi attack on the Saudi capital in as many days, after Thursday's strike damaged the Public Investment Fund tower, the kingdom's tallest skyscraper. A Reuters witness in Riyadh saw a projectile streak across the sky shortly before hearing a loud bang, leaving a trail of smoke. No casualties or damage from the Saturday attack have been reported.

According to Reuters, the coalition said its air defences intercepted the three missiles, while the Houthis threatened to target Saudi Red Sea ports, warned airlines to avoid Saudi airspace, and claimed attacks on a power station and a port in southern Saudi Arabia. The attack followed an escalation on Friday, when the coalition said it had struck the Red Sea port of Hodeidah and carried out operations targeting Houthi missile and drone capabilities in the capital Sanaa. Al Arabiya and Al Jazeera also reported the coalition's statement that the three missiles aimed at Riyadh had been intercepted. It comes a day after Friday evening's separate Houthi attack on the southern Jazan province, where two ballistic missiles were intercepted, and Thursday's strike on the PIF tower, which blew out windows across seven or eight floors of the 80-storey building.

The two sides are locked in a widening exchange with no sign of slowing. The Houthis have vowed continued strikes on Saudi airports and oil facilities, and have extended evacuation warnings to shipping companies; their stated aim is to pressure Riyadh over the Yemen war, where Saudi-backed government forces have fallen back to defend Aden while the Houthis sit roughly 100 km west of the city. Riyadh, for its part, has stepped up retaliatory strikes on Sanaa and Hodeidah, and Reuters reported this week that Saudi planners expect the Yemen campaign to last up to six months into 2027, with the goal of gaining territory before any negotiations. The United States has so far withheld offensive support — intelligence, precision munitions and targeting help — which Saudi officials say could compress the campaign to weeks if provided, though Washington has sent more than 100 intelligence advisers under a joint command.

For people in Riyadh, the war is now arriving over the capital twice in two days: air-raid alerts, explosions heard in the city, and warnings to airlines to avoid Saudi airspace. The Houthi threat to Red Sea ports and Saudi airspace also matters for anyone flying into the kingdom — including December travel plans to Jeddah, where the main airport has so far stayed open but remains on the Houthis' target list. Oil markets have so far taken the escalation in stride — Brent was around $92 on Friday, set for a third straight weekly decline — but the repeated attacks on the capital and on shipping are keeping the risk premium in place.

In short: the Houthis fired three ballistic missiles at Riyadh early Saturday, all intercepted, a day after hitting the PIF tower and hours after a separate attack on Jazan — and with the coalition striking back at Hodeidah and Sanaa, the Saudi-Yemen exchange is escalating rather than cooling. Watch for whether the Houthis follow through on their threat to hit Red Sea ports, and whether Washington changes its mind on offensive support for Riyadh.

*Sources: Reuters via KWSN, Al Arabiya English, Al Jazeera English, Roya News English*

Short items

Poland follow-up: France calls for a UN Security Council meeting; Russian drones also violated Moldovan airspace

France's foreign minister called for a meeting of the UN Security Council over Friday's Russian missile and drone incursion into Poland, and the Institute for the Study of War reported that two unidentified drones, likely Russian strike drones involved in the attack on Ukraine, also violated Moldovan airspace on November 20 before re-entering Ukrainian airspace. Defence ministers from France, the UK and the Baltic states travelled to Poland on Friday to attend the KOALICJA 26.2 exercise in the country's southeast. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte is expected in Warsaw on Saturday; Poland has said it will formally ask NATO for more troops and surveillance aircraft for Operation Eastern Sentry. No new incursions were reported Friday evening.

*Sources: Vocemundi (BBC), ISW, Rapporteur*

Tanker hit by projectile near the Strait of Hormuz on Friday — missed in the 5 PM brief

A tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was struck on its port bow by an unknown projectile at about 3 p.m. UTC on Friday, around 8 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, and caught fire, UKMTO said in Warning 214-26; no casualties or environmental impact were reported, and the vessel has not been identified. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said shortly afterwards that a tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine — that account, carried by Iranian state media, has not been independently verified, and UKMTO has not identified the weapon. It is the latest attack in the strait this week, after the LPG tanker Tara Gas was hit twice and the Navig8 Messi caught fire earlier in the week. UKMTO also recorded a separate incident Friday: a bulk carrier eastbound in the transit corridor off Al Mukalla, Yemen, was approached by a dark-blue skiff with six armed people, who fired four shots before leaving. The Joint Maritime Information Center holds the regional threat level at SEVERE, and UKMTO counted 263 combined transits through the strait over the past week, down from 308 the week before.

*Sources: Reuters, UKMTO via ShipUniverse, Defcon Level*

Nvidia follow-up: SEC probe focus includes Singapore revenue and $118B in customer guarantees

More detail emerged Friday evening on the SEC probe that sent Nvidia down 14%: Reuters reported enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues, with renewed scrutiny of roughly $104 billion of 2025 revenue booked through Nvidia's Singapore unit and of the company's customer-financing guarantees — interim CEO Colette Kress said on Tuesday's earnings call that Nvidia's guarantees totalled $118 billion, including $105 billion of credit support tied to a single data-centre campus. The Wall Street Journal first reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors; Reuters could not determine when the inquiry began or exactly which practices are under review, and the SEC declined to comment. The Justice Department is also reviewing Nvidia's planned $12.9 billion purchase of Hugging Face, Reuters reported. Friday's sell-off was sector-wide: AMD fell about 13% and Micron almost 14%. An SEC investigation is not evidence of wrongdoing, and many close without enforcement action.

*Sources: Motley Fool, Reuters via LA Post, Dealroom*

Bell Canada says an "agentic" attacker stole enterprise customer data

Bell Canada confirmed on Friday that an intruder it described as an "agentic" threat actor breached its systems on November 17 and stole enterprise customer data, including from a pre-production environment of Maximo, the system Bell uses to order and provision telecom circuits for business customers. The exposed data includes business contact details, ticket titles, some IP addresses tied to network elements, and limited contextual information; Bell said credentials, highly sensitive personal data and attachments were not in the affected dataset. Bell said it contained the incident with no impact to customer services, and warned enterprise customers to watch for social-engineering attempts by people impersonating the company. No attacker has been identified, and the number of affected customers and data volume have not been disclosed. BleepingComputer reads Bell's "agentic" wording as suggesting AI-powered tools were used in the breach — that has not been confirmed.

*Sources: BleepingComputer via Line of Sites, DEV Community*

Chase/Visa/Mastercard vendor breach: the provider was Qubole, per BleepingComputer

BleepingComputer reported Friday that the third-party provider behind the ransomware group n0n's leak of JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard customer data was Qubole, a cloud data-analytics company owned by Idera. The group claimed it used compromised Qubole credentials between October 14 and 26 to access customers' AWS environments through IAM roles, stealing 53.9 GB across 30,743 files from Qubole itself and claiming nearly a petabyte from customer networks. Chase said about 16,000 Chase Reserve customer emails were exposed; Visa and Mastercard said no payment data was affected. The core breach was reported in Thursday night's brief; the Qubole identification is new.

*Source: BleepingComputer*

Java4Shell: still no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation

No confirmed exploitation in the wild has been reported as of Friday evening, and Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) is still not in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. GreyNoise's count stood at about 192 trigger-shaped requests from 6 IPs since November 17 — probing only. Qualys published a mitigation guide Friday, and Confluent confirmed its cloud service is already mitigated while customer-managed Confluent Platform deployments need a fixed Java build (Oracle 17.0.21.1 / 21.0.13.1 / 25.0.5.1) or the -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics workaround. The flaw only affects x86-64 CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI.

*Sources: Qualys, Confluent*

Google launched a Genie 4 API for interactive worlds

Google's Genie 4 API creates interactive 720p worlds at 24 frames per second with native audio, live keyboard controls, and the ability to change environments through text prompts, The Neuron reported Friday. It is aimed at developers building playable AI-generated environments rather than consumers. No pricing or general-availability details were in the report.
