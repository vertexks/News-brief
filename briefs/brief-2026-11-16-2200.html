World escalation

Russia hit two of Kyiv's bridges late Monday — hours after Trump said Putin is "backing down"

Russian forces struck Kyiv's North and South bridges across the Dnipro River late on Monday night, knocking out water and power in parts of the capital, in an overnight barrage that also hit Cherkasy, Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia. The strikes landed hours after President Donald Trump, leaving the White House for Asia, said he thought Vladimir Putin was "backing down a little bit" in Ukraine — and floated a possible meeting with North Korea's Kim Jong Un in China, a surprise that would put a fourth leader at the week's summit table.

The details, as reported by Reuters and France 24: Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram that, according to preliminary information, there had been hits on the North and South bridges, and later added that parts of the city had water and power cuts. Reuters witnesses reported a series of loud explosions in the capital. A Russian missile attack injured three people in the Cherkasy region, and strikes also targeted Kharkiv, Ukraine's second-biggest city, and critical and industrial infrastructure in Zaporizhzhia, local officials said. Reuters said it could not immediately independently confirm the bridge reports. Russia has attacked bridges over the Dnipro earlier this month and in October. The attacks continued overnight, with the Crimea Bridge hit again and Moscow saying it downed nearly 200 drones heading in its direction, according to Telegram statements. President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday that intelligence showed Russia planned strikes for Monday evening and throughout Tuesday — and that Ukraine had held new online talks with US presidential envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, as well as European representatives, on ending the war. Kushner said progress was made toward an agreement, though important challenges remain. The barrage followed Ukraine's record overnight drone attack into Russia on Sunday night, which Moscow said involved 1,336 drones downed across 16 regions.

The diplomacy is where the two sides' stories diverge sharply. Trump told reporters before boarding Air Force One that he thought Putin "is backing down a little bit," adding "I think he'd like to see it finished" and "I think he could be tougher." He also said Ukraine would be discussed at Thursday's trilateral with Xi Jinping and Putin in Shenzhen — scheduled for 2:30 p.m. local time at Shenzhen Kylin Villa, according to Roll Call — along with denuclearization, Iran, trade and the economy. Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov said a one-on-one meeting between Trump and Putin in Shenzhen was also being arranged, according to the Associated Press. Then the surprise: asked about Kim, Trump said "We may meet with Kim Jong Un also… He may come. I don't know. That's up to President Xi." Neither Beijing nor Pyongyang had confirmed anything by the time he spoke, and North Korea has publicly indicated it has no intention of meeting Trump at this time, according to TBS News. A US official said last week there were no plans for North Korea engagement on the trip. The White House and senior US officials have said Ukraine is not on the trilateral agenda and no agreement is expected — a direct contradiction of what Trump said on the tarmac.

For ordinary people in Kyiv, the bridge strikes are a new kind of target: bridges are how the city moves, and hitting two of them at once, on top of the energy campaign, means longer commutes, disrupted water and power, and another night of air-raid alerts. Zelensky warned on Sunday that the ballistic missile threat "remains very serious." In Russia, meanwhile, the drone war is reaching deeper — Moscow's mayor reported around 600 drones aimed at the capital region since Sunday evening.

In short: Russia answered Trump's "backing down" remark with a night of strikes on Kyiv's bridges and three more regions, while the Shenzhen summit keeps growing — a trilateral on Thursday, a possible Trump–Putin one-on-one, and now a floated Kim meeting that nobody has confirmed. Watch Tuesday: Ramstein, and whether the overnight barrage continues as Zelensky's intelligence warned.

Drones hit four ships off Romania — inside NATO waters, and Bucharest is asking allies for help

Drones struck four commercial vessels in the Black Sea near Romania's Sulina Canal over Friday and Saturday, two of them inside Romanian territorial waters, in the sharpest escalation yet of the shipping war spilling into NATO territory. Romania has declared the area dangerous to navigation, scrambled fighter jets twice, and is now asking allies for surveillance aircraft and counter-drone equipment.

The details, confirmed by Romania's defence ministry and reported by Reuters: on Saturday evening, the Greek-flagged oil and chemical tanker Evia Petrol V was hit at about 9:10 p.m. local time, roughly four nautical miles east-southeast of Sulina, and a fire broke out on board that the crew extinguished. About 35 minutes later the bulk carrier Marsol was struck about five nautical miles southeast of the Sulina Canal, also catching fire, also put out by its crew. A third vessel, the oil-products tanker Ali Najafov, reported a drone strike near the Danube's mouths the same night. A fourth ship, the Palau-flagged general cargo vessel Larissa 1, was hit on Friday evening. No casualties were reported in any of the four incidents. Romania's Maritime Hydrographic Directorate issued a radio navigational warning on Sunday advising commercial vessels to avoid the area, and the defence ministry said it scrambled F-16s on two occasions after detecting aerial targets, including one near Snake Island. The Sulina Canal is one of the main links between the Danube and the Black Sea and is used by Ukraine for exports — Evia Petrol V had been waiting to enter the canal to load about 6,500 tonnes of sunflower oil.

Bucharest has not formally named who launched the drones, but Prime Minister Luca Niculescu and Defence Minister Stefan-Radu Oprea both called the strikes a direct consequence of Russia's war in Ukraine, and Oprea said they were violations of international law. France has deployed Atlantique 2 maritime patrol aircraft as reinforcement, according to Business Recorder, and Oprea will seek allied surveillance aircraft and counter-drone equipment at a Brussels meeting. Moscow has said nothing about the incidents. The attacks follow a series of incidents involving commercial vessels near the Romanian and Bulgarian coasts through October and November.

For ordinary people, the stakes are food and fuel prices: the Sulina route carries Ukrainian grain and sunflower oil to world markets, and every incident like this raises insurance costs and pushes shippers to reroute. It also puts a NATO member's territorial waters directly in the line of fire — the first time the Black Sea shipping war has hit ships inside a NATO country's waters rather than near them.

In short: four ships hit in two nights, two of them inside Romanian territorial waters, no casualties but fires on board and a navigation warning in force. Bucharest is treating it as part of Russia's war and asking NATO allies for help watching the coast. Watch the Brussels meeting and whether any shipowner or insurer pulls out of the Sulina route.

Tankers still burning in Hormuz, Houthis 60 km from Aden — and Tehran offers a two-day plan to reopen the strait

The Gulf war's 263rd day brought burning tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, a Houthi advance to within about 60 kilometres of Aden, and a diplomatic opening from Tehran — a two-day plan to reopen the strait — while Washington signalled it is preparing its forces for a long confrontation rather than an early settlement.

The details, as reported by The Atlantic Digest and Reuters: Iranian state television, as quoted by Bloomberg, put the number of vessels that had caught fire in the strait since Sunday at six, including a supertanker said to be carrying more than two million barrels of crude. None of the Iranian mine claims has been independently verified, and the Guard has warned oil companies the southern route is mined. The one incident a neutral agency has confirmed remains the UK Maritime Trade Operations report from Monday morning of a tanker hit by an unknown projectile near Oman, causing an engine-room fire with no casualties. A tracker cited by Dawn estimated crude flows through Hormuz had fallen to about eight million barrels a day by November 15, roughly 47 percent of the pre-war level — though oil still fell on Monday, with Brent settling at $92.44, down $3.40 (3.6%), and WTI at $85.72, down $2.84 (3.2%), on recovering flows and the IEA's surplus forecast. In Yemen, the Houthis advanced to about 60 km from Aden, military and government sources told AFP, seizing the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts of Lahj province; Reuters reported Houthi fighters had seized ground on the southern coast and that Aden residents described new checkpoints and identity checks. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two Houthi drones launched toward the Mecca and Riyadh regions, and Saudi Arabia replaced the coalition's commander, Lieutenant General Fahd bin Hamad al-Salman, with General Fayyad al-Ruwaili. Late on Monday, the Iranian agency Mehr, quoted by Reuters, reported air defences had been activated in Isfahan, with no cause given.

Tehran's message on diplomacy was mixed. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi spent Monday in Islamabad at an Economic Cooperation Organisation meeting, saying it had responded positively to an Iranian proposal he described as a two-day plan for the Strait of Hormuz, and that members hoped it would lead to a definitive and comprehensive ceasefire — though Dawn reported the plan is conditional on Washington accepting Iran's terms. Araghchi is due in Muscat on Tuesday for talks with his Omani counterpart. But parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the path to ending the conflict stayed open only if Washington accepted what he called Iran's logical conditions, foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei ruled out curbs on uranium enrichment as a route to a deal, and deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran would not wait for the United States to attack first and that IAEA inspectors would not get access to damaged nuclear sites until Iran's conditions were met. Washington's posture points the same way: the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group entered Central Command waters on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington — Bloomberg noted the US last had three carrier groups in the region in 2003 — and the Army is seeking contractors to build 17 reinforced-concrete bunkers to protect troops in Israel, Saudi Arabia and Lebanon, calling the requirement urgent and high priority, with completion within 45 days of a task order. Iranian missiles and drones have killed at least 23 American personnel in the region, according to Military Times, including six in a strike on an Army command post in Kuwait in March. Trump said Iran would be discussed at this week's Shenzhen summit and that "we have Iran in very good shape," while the New York Times reported the US and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure.

For ordinary people, the disconnect between the battlefield and the market is the story: despite burning tankers and a supertanker said to have sunk, fuel prices are falling, not rising, because flows are recovering faster than feared. The human cost is concentrated in Yemen, where the Houthi advance toward Aden has displaced hundreds of thousands and blocked aid, and in Bahrain, where a court on Monday sentenced 64 people to between 15 years and life in prison on charges of forming a group linked to the Revolutionary Guard, according to ABC News.

In short: the shipping war is still escalating — six tankers claimed hit since Sunday, the Houthis closing on Aden — but Tehran is also floating a two-day plan to reopen Hormuz, and Washington is digging in for a long fight, with three carriers on station and emergency bunkers going up. Watch Tuesday's Araghchi talks in Muscat, and whether any maritime agency confirms mines in the southern lanes.

Nvidia closed at its lowest since July 2025 — the day before its first earnings without Huang

Nvidia shares closed Monday at $156.14, down $6.71 or 4.12%, the company's lowest close since July 1, 2025, according to CNN — after falling about 12% last week and now down nearly 24% this month. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 5.3%, and Intel and Micron each fell more than 7%. The broader market slid with it: the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.49% to 24,509.76, in a correction by the wire's measure, the S&P 500 fell 1.92% to 7,416.59, and the Dow fell 739 points, or 1.41%, to 51,608.17. The petition urging Huang's reinstatement has passed 180,000 signatures, but the board was reported on Monday not to be discussing his return. Nvidia reports third-quarter results Tuesday at about 4:20 p.m. ET, with the earnings call at 5 p.m. ET — the first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, presented by interim CEO Colette Kress. Consensus is revenue of about $109.1 billion and adjusted EPS of $2.47. The quarter itself ended October 25, before Huang's departure, so the numbers will show what Nvidia delivered under him; the fourth-quarter outlook will be the first look at what management expects after the handover. Also on the week's calendar: Anthropic's IPO, with trading set to start Thursday on Nasdaq and the company targeting a valuation of about $1.4 trillion.

Apple: iOS 27.3 beta 2 adds face unlock for the Duo — and Siri starts answering health questions

Apple seeded the second beta of iOS 27.3 on Monday. The headline change for the iPhone Duo is a front-camera face unlock option, usable only when the phone is closed and only on the Lock Screen — a partial answer to the most common complaint about the foldable, which shipped without Face ID. The beta also lets Siri AI answer questions about your health data (step counts, heart rate, Activity rings) and log medications, and adds an "Ask About Page" feature that lets Siri read a whole Safari page. macOS 27.3 beta 2 (Golden Gate) adds vertical tabs in Safari and native Live Activities. Separately, Bloomberg reported that Corey Fugman, a 26-year Apple veteran, will take over Apple Pay, Wallet and Apple Card from the retiring Jennifer Bailey. Apple has promised iOS 27.3 for December.

Cybersecurity

Air-raid alert apps in both Russia and Ukraine were hacked — fake nuclear warnings sent to phones

Ukraine's air-raid alert app was hacked on Sunday evening, sending users fake warnings of nuclear and chemical threats around Kyiv, warnings of street combat, and a false claim that Russia's hypersonic Oreshnik missile had been deployed. The Ukrainian Air Force said on Telegram: "The air alert app appears to have been subjected to enemy influence. There is no radiation threat!" Ukraine's Centre for Strategic Communications blamed Russia. Hours later, early on Monday morning, Russia's Emergencies Ministry said its own warnings app came under a "massive cyberattack" that spread false information about incoming drone attacks. The announcements followed the end of a localized truce in part of Russian-occupied Kherson region on Sunday — the longest of the war so far. The Kyiv-specific alert app kept working normally, according to Kyiv authorities. For anyone relying on these apps, the lesson is the same as with any emergency channel: a single app is a single point of failure, and official Telegram channels were the fallback both sides pointed to.

Major court cases

Russell Brand: foreign secretary calls his prison message "revolting" — and nearly 400 creatives sign a letter against a "culture of silence"

The fallout from Russell Brand's prison message kept growing on Monday. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told Sky News the crimes were "heinous and awful" and called it "revolting" that Brand showed no remorse in the message his wife read out on YouTube on Sunday — in which Brand said "I am not guilty for the offenses I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening." Miliband also apologised for appearing on Brand's online show in 2015. The Ministry of Justice is urgently investigating whether the message breached prison rules, which bar prisoners from making statements that cause distress to victims, and Downing Street called it "clearly appalling." Separately, nearly 400 British creatives — including Natalie Dormer — signed an open letter demanding stronger protections for people who report misconduct in entertainment and accountability for those who help conceal abuse, organised after Brand's convictions and the publication of allegations against David Walliams. The Metropolitan Police launched an investigation on November 13 into separate allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s. Brand was sentenced on November 9 to 10 years and six months for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault; no appeal has been filed, with the windows running to December 3 for conviction and December 7 for sentence.
