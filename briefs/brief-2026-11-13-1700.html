Evening brief — Friday, November 13, 2026

Ukraine confirmed it hit Russia's Volgograd refinery — its ninth refinery strike this month — as Europe finally agreed who speaks for it at the peace talks

Ukraine confirmed on Friday that it struck the Lukoil refinery in Volgograd overnight, the ninth Russian oil refinery it has hit since the start of November, in a campaign that is now choking off nearly 40% of Russia's crude-processing capacity. The same day in Dublin, European leaders settled a question that has dogged the peace process for days: who speaks for Europe at the negotiating table — a team, not a single envoy.

The details of the refinery strike are now firm. Ukraine's General Staff said its forces attacked the Lukoil refinery in Volgograd, about 900 kilometres southeast of Moscow, starting a fire that damaged four primary processing units, a hydrotreatment unit, six storage tanks and a loading rack, according to Bloomberg, reporting the Ukrainian statement. The plant processes about 300,000 barrels of crude a day. Local governor Andrey Bocharov had earlier confirmed only that drones hit the city's southern industrial zone, killing four people and injuring two, without saying whether the refinery itself was hit. Bloomberg's tally puts the campaign at nine refineries attacked since November 1, including Omsk, the country's largest, with combined nameplate capacity of around 2.6 million barrels a day — almost 40% of Russia's total crude processing. The International Energy Agency said in its monthly outlook on Friday that Russian oil processing is set to be "severely curtailed" by the Ukrainian attacks through the end of the year, weighing on Moscow's ability to export diesel. Kyiv resumed the refinery campaign in late October after a nearly two-week pause that coincided with President Trump's calls for calm ahead of the US midterms.

The toll from Thursday night's exchange kept rising through Friday. In Zaporizhzhia, a rescue worker was killed when a concrete slab collapsed as crews cleared rubble from a five-storey apartment block, and more than 50 people were wounded, including eight children, according to Reuters and Ukrainian officials — up from the 25 wounded reported in the morning. In Odesa, two women, aged 44 and 50, were killed in a strike on a private home, and a house, a veterinary clinic, a warehouse and several cars were destroyed. In Kyiv, three people were hurt when a drone hit a hospital, blowing out windows at the nearby National Academy of Arts and forcing firefighters to evacuate patients through smoke. Russia launched 117 drones overnight; Ukraine said it shot down 80. Ukraine's barrage the other way — nearly 1,200 drones — killed seven people in Russia, including a 12-year-old.

What each side is arguing about has shifted from the battlefield to the negotiating room. In Dublin on Friday, EU leaders agreed that Europe will be represented by the EU institutions plus the "E5" — Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — rather than a single envoy, European Council President António Costa announced. "Only Ukraine has the legitimacy to decide on matters concerning its territory and sovereignty," Costa said, adding that the EU would handle membership, reconstruction and prosperity while the coalition of the willing handles security guarantees in coordination with NATO. But the team's mandate is still to be set — Portuguese Prime Minister Luís Montenegro said it had not received a specific one — and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said a 22nd sanctions package and plans to close Ukraine's 2027 financing gap would come before the next European Council on November 26–27. Moscow, meanwhile, is sending mixed signals: Putin said he was open to Europeans joining the talks, reversing years of opposition, but spokesman Dmitry Peskov quickly qualified that, saying Europe must first demonstrate "genuine intention and readiness to contribute to the peace process." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas have both rejected Moscow's push to put the post-Cold War security order itself on the table. Trump and Putin are scheduled to meet during the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19.

For ordinary people, the war's grind is now measured in fuel and power. In Russia, repeated refinery strikes have already forced some regions to reintroduce fuel rationing, and a similar campaign last summer pushed Moscow to ban most exports of diesel, gasoline and jet fuel. In Ukraine, Russian strikes knocked out power across about half the country's regions on Friday, with hospitals, homes and kindergartens hit in the same wave. The diplomacy offers no quick relief: the next round of talks has no date yet, and the prisoner exchange the US envoys are pushing — potentially the largest of the war — remains an announced goal, not a done deal.

In short: Ukraine's refinery campaign is now hitting a scale that shows up in global oil forecasts, the civilian toll from the overnight exchange kept climbing through the day, and Europe finally has a team for the talks — but no mandate, no date, and a Kremlin that still hasn't clearly said yes. Watch the APEC meeting between Trump and Putin on November 18–19, and whether the next round of talks gets a date.

Iran struck back at a US military site in Bahrain — its first attack on an American base in the Gulf in two months — as Trump settles into keeping the war frozen

Iran said it targeted a US military installation in Bahrain early on Friday, in retaliation for what it described as an American attack on Iranian territory — the first Iranian attempt to hit a US military facility in a Gulf state in more than two months, and a sharp escalation the day after Trump said the US doesn't need a deal with Tehran at all.

The details, as reported by CNN, are contested on both sides. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said its aerospace forces "targeted and destroyed the early warning radar of the aggressor US military in Bahrain," in response to what it called "the enemy's aerial aggression against a communication tower in Sirik," according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency, adding that "this is a serious warning" to Washington. US Central Command said later on Friday that American forces shot down two Iranian drones launched toward Bahrain, reporting no injuries or damage — and called the claim that Iran had attacked a radar system false. Iranian media had earlier reported that Sirik, a port city in Hormozgan province on the Gulf of Oman just east of the Strait of Hormuz, was hit by "enemy fire" in a military strike, with details of the attacker and the damage undisclosed. While the US has kept striking Iranian targets around the strait in recent weeks, Tehran had largely refrained from attacking American military installations in Gulf states since early September, focusing instead on disrupting shipping — making Friday's move a deliberate break with that pattern.

What each side is saying now points in opposite directions. Trump, writing on Thursday after Iran submitted a seven-day roadmap to end the war (covered in the morning brief), said "we don't need a deal with Iran" and pulled Vice President JD Vance off the diplomacy entirely. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran's demands "absurd" earlier in the week, and NPR's analysis on Friday concluded Trump's strategy is now to keep things as they are — the blockade and sanctions doing the work, with a third US aircraft carrier and thousands more troops heading to the region, leaving a renewed bombing campaign on the table after the midterms. Iran, under intense economic pressure, has warned it will not accept a stalemate: President Masoud Pezeshkian said on Monday that Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei had instructed his government "to end the state of neither war nor peace," telling him to "finish" the war, and Tehran has warned any new escalation would prompt it to dramatically expand its attacks, potentially targeting US interests as far away as Europe. Meanwhile, the director of Mossad, Roman Gofman, was in Washington on Friday — a visit first reported by Axios — as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu pushes the Trump administration back toward war with Iran. Two Israeli sources told CNN that Netanyahu has even considered a unilateral Israeli strike on Iran, though he would still need Trump's approval, and the prospect was concerning enough that his rival Gadi Eisenkot demanded and received a security briefing from the prime minister.

For ordinary people the costs keep compounding at the pump and on the water. Attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz hit a record high in October — 37 vessel attacks, the highest monthly total of the conflict, according to Lloyd's List — with at least 14 more confirmed in November. Brent crude held around $95.50 a barrel on Friday and WTI around $88.80, after Brent settled Thursday at $95.82, up $3.06. US gasoline averaged $3.98 a gallon and diesel $6.16 on November 10, roughly 30% and 65% above a year earlier, according to AAA. The one piece of relief: the International Energy Agency said on Friday that the oil market is "showing tentative signs of rebalancing," with global oil supplies surpassing demand for the first time since the war began. But Gulf states hosting American bases are now directly in the line of fire again, and Iran also unveiled what it says is a new underground missile production site this week.

In short: Tehran broke its two-month restraint on hitting US bases, striking at Bahrain after the US hit Sirik — and both sides are now talking past each other, Trump saying no deal is needed while Iran's leadership says the neither-war-nor-peace limbo has to end. Watch whether the White House formally answers Iran's roadmap, and whether the third carrier's arrival or the post-midterm window brings the bombing campaign Trump reportedly expects.

Houthis / Saudi Arabia

Coalition intercepted a Houthi drone aimed at the Mecca region on Friday morning

The Saudi-led coalition said on Friday morning that it intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone launched toward Saudi Arabia's Mecca region, coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said, and that a missile aimed at western Saudi Arabia was also brought down earlier. The Houthis denied targeting the Muslim holy city or the surrounding region, saying their cross-border attacks focus strictly on Saudi energy and military infrastructure. The coalition had said on Thursday it destroyed a ballistic missile fired toward the kingdom's eastern region, and later two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. Houthi-affiliated media reported later that coalition air attacks were hitting districts in Marib, Taiz and al-Jawf in Yemen. No new damage or casualty figures were reported on either side. Poland

President Nawrocki vetoed the digital content bill for a second time

Polish President Karol Nawrocki vetoed legislation on Friday that would have let regulators decide whether online content is illegal and order it blocked — his second rejection of a bill implementing the EU's Digital Services Act, and his 48th veto of the Tusk government's bills since taking office. Nawrocki said the bill, narrowed and resubmitted after he vetoed an earlier version in January, gave state officials excessive power over online speech. The bill had gained urgency after a string of knife attacks at Polish schools in October. Prime Minister Donald Tusk defended it on X — "Protecting children from online criminals. It is hard to imagine a more important issue that should unite people across political divides. And yet he vetoed it" — while Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski said the veto leaves Poland without the tools the government says it needs to respond quickly, and that he would draft another bill. No new developments on the Tusk assassination plot case; Krzysztof N. remains in pretrial detention. (Reuters — Polish president vetoes digital content bill for second time)

Israel

Eisenkot vows a stable emergency government, says coalition talks are making progress

Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief tasked with forming Israel's next government after the October 27 election stripped Netanyahu of his majority, said on Friday that negotiations are progressing toward a broad, stable emergency government and that he intends to "leave no stone unturned" in talks with every elected official who agrees with his four principles. He also publicly accused caretaker Prime Minister Netanyahu of running a "racist" campaign against Palestinian citizens of Israel during the election, while extending an inclusive stance toward Israel's Arab citizens. Netanyahu said on Wednesday that a national unity government was still possible, though no opposition leader has expressed openness to serving under him. Eisenkot is pursuing support from ultra-Orthodox parties to reach the 61 seats needed in the 120-seat Knesset, and has six weeks from his November 8 mandate to form a government. (Jharkhand Times — Eisenkot vows to form stable govt · RocketNews — Eisenkot slams Netanyahu's 'racist' campaign)

Cyber

Two new KEV additions today — Check Point and UnrealIRCd, both due Monday

CISA added two exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog today, both with a federal patch deadline of Monday, November 16, bringing the catalog to 1,781 entries. Check Point CVE-2026-123039 (CVSS 7.8) is a local privilege escalation in the Gaia OS DynamicCLI service, exploited since late October chained with the PHP2Shell flaw in the Mobile Access portal — Check Point says a couple of dozen gateways were compromised, with attackers in some cases wiping servers behind them. Fix: install the November Jumbo Hotfix (R82.20 Take 14, R82.10 Take 52, R82 Take 132 or R81.20 Take 174 or later). UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713 (CVSS 8.6) lets any normal user on a linked IRC network run commands with server authority; update to 6.2.7.1 or hot-patch. (Carthage Electronics — November 13 CVE threat brief · HOL — Check Point CVE-2026-123039 on CISA KEV)

The Barracuda pair and an Ivanti flaw hit their federal deadline today

The Barracuda Email Security Gateway pair — CVE-2026-116606 (9.3, pre-auth authorisation bypass) and CVE-2026-111560 (8.6, OS command injection via a timezone value) — chain into root access, and Barracuda confirmed on November 9–10 that attackers are actively exploiting them against a small number of organisations. Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-118527 also came due today. The six flaws added November 10, including the Chrome zero-day CVE-2026-125350 and the Outlook zero-day CVE-2026-124194, are due November 24. (AI Agent Update — Barracuda flaws exploited as federal deadline lands)

N-able published indicators for the 4EverAble2Shell attacks — but there's still no fix

N-able published indicators of compromise today for the N-central "4EverAble2Shell" zero-day chain (Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436 plus an N-central privilege escalation to root), exploited within hours of a proof of concept on Thursday — 21 systems compromised, 19 of them on the latest November 10 hotfix. A real fix is still in the works; a follow-up hotfix is planned. N-able also confirmed that Agent2Shell, the related chain, needs the Legacy API enabled, which is off by default. (Carthage Electronics — November 13 CVE threat brief)

Courts

Murdaugh: no ruling today — the judge will decide by next Friday

Judge Debra McCaslin did not rule at Friday's dismissal hearing in Lexington, after nearly two hours of arguments over whether SLED agent David Owen misled the July 2022 grand jury about blood spatter on Alex Murdaugh's shirt. She said she will issue a written order by next Friday, November 20, and pressed ahead with plans for the April 5, 2027 retrial, setting January 20, 2027 as the deadline for both sides to agree on the juror questionnaire to be mailed to potential Sumter County jurors. Even if she grants the long-shot dismissal, prosecutors can seek fresh indictments from the Colleton County grand jury, which next sits December 10, and the defence says it won't oppose that — so the retrial is likely to happen either way. (Greenville Online — defence wants murder charges sent back to grand jury · ABA Journal — ruling expected next week)
