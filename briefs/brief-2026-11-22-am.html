Bahrain takes Iran to the UN after Saturday's strike; tankers still burning in Hormuz

Bahrain demanded United Nations Security Council action and compensation from Iran on Sunday, a day after an Iranian missile-and-drone attack on US positions in the kingdom set fire to a civilian industrial facility — and Reuters now reports two people were hurt in that fire, one of them critically. The US says its forces and equipment were not affected. Meanwhile the tanker the attack sequence left burning in the Strait of Hormuz is still at anchor, and Iran announced rolling power cuts starting Sunday.

The attack itself was covered in last night's brief: Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it hit two US Patriot radars at the al-Riffa and Awali Sakhir air bases in retaliation for alleged US drone violations of Iranian airspace, while Bahrain said its defences intercepted a number of incoming attacks. What's new this morning is the fallout. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry said on Sunday it is demanding action by the UN Security Council and compensation from Iran, according to a developing report, and Reuters reports the industrial-site fire injured two people — a Pakistani resident in critical condition and a Bahraini citizen in stable condition — revising last night's preliminary account of no injuries. A US official separately told CBS News the latest Iranian attack attempts had not affected American forces or military assets, and the US military has still not commented publicly. The Saudi-led coalition also intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile early on Sunday headed toward western Saudi Arabia, and Iran's navy again claimed a tanker caught fire after hitting a mine in a restricted area — an account Reuters could not verify or link to the reported incidents.

At sea, the picture is settling but not resolved. Two tankers were struck near Oman's Khasab on Saturday — one on the stern about 11 nautical miles north, the second in its engine room 17 nautical miles east — and a navigational warning has now named one of them, the TORKEL, as burning at anchor. No casualties were reported in either strike and the environmental impact is still unknown. No new tanker attacks were reported on Sunday. Iran's navy says a ship hit a mine; the UK maritime agency says the vessels were hit by unknown projectiles, and the two accounts have not been reconciled. Tehran also confirmed a Qatari mediation delegation visited Iran for talks, and Iran's deputy foreign minister repeated that its proposal for a two-day ceasefire and reopening of the strait is still on the table. Separately, the head of Iran's national electricity company told state television the country will be forced into rolling power cuts starting Sunday.

The two sides are still talking past each other. Tehran frames its strikes as retaliation for airspace violations and calls the US blockade of its oil exports an act of war, saying it will take every necessary step to break it. Bahrain's Defence Force called the attacks a "systematic hostile approach" and its Interior Ministry called the strike "heinous Iranian aggression." Washington's public line remains minimal — no damage to US forces, no comment from the military — which leaves open whether the US treats Saturday as a one-off retaliation or the start of a new round. Iran, for its part, is publicly keeping a diplomatic door open through Qatar while its military keeps hitting Gulf targets.

For people in the region, the cost is now showing up in daily life on both sides of the Gulf: another night of sirens and shelter warnings in Bahrain, two people in hospital, and rolling power cuts in Iran as its grid strains under sanctions and war. For everyone else, the shipping war keeps the risk premium on oil intact — Brent was trading around $90–92 a barrel — and every tanker hit near Hormuz is another reason fuel prices stay jumpy.

In short: Bahrain is taking Iran to the UN Security Council over Saturday's strike, which injured two people at a civilian industrial site; the US says its forces were unaffected, Qatar is mediating in Tehran, and the TORKEL is still burning at anchor in the strait — with Iran now announcing rolling power cuts at home.

Sources: Reuters (via HeadlinesXtra), Daily Inference, DeepDraft, The Chronify, Indrajit Global News

Russian drones collapse a Kyiv-region apartment block; a week of strikes totals 2,700 drones

A Russian drone struck a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, about 45 km north of Kyiv, on Sunday morning, collapsing part of the building from the first floor to the fifth. At least 14 people are unaccounted for and may be trapped under the rubble, President Zelenskyy said, and one body had been recovered by Sunday morning. Nine residents, including a child, were evacuated, and two people, including a teenager, were injured. It was the sharpest single incident in another heavy overnight attack — Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 drones, 161 of which were shot down, with hits at 18 locations.

The overnight barrage caps a brutal week. Zelenskyy said on Sunday that Russian forces launched around 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 aerial bombs and 165 missiles of various types, including ballistic missiles, at 20 regions across Ukraine during the week. Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces struck port infrastructure at Kiliia in the Odesa region, the 330-kV Pravoberezhna electrical substation in Zaporizhzhia, and a cargo vessel in the Black Sea northeast of Odesa that it alleged was carrying dual-use goods for the Ukrainian military. Ukraine also kept hitting back inside Russia: Belgorod region's governor said the region came under 138 attacks in 24 hours, leaving one person dead and six injured, and Russia's Defence Ministry said its air defences intercepted 170 Ukrainian drones overnight over several Russian regions and the Black and Azov seas.

Kyiv and Moscow are describing the same week in opposite terms. Zelenskyy says Russia is deliberately escalating strikes on energy infrastructure to pressure Ukraine's position ahead of talks, and has repeatedly pointed to the low interception rate on ballistic missiles and the strain on air defences. Moscow says it is striking military-industrial facilities, energy sites and transport infrastructure that supply Ukraine's armed forces, and presents its own air defences as intercepting the bulk of Ukrainian drones. Neither side's claims about interception rates or targets can be independently verified in full, and both sides' casualty and damage figures come from their own officials.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the week has meant another round of emergency outages, damaged homes and rescue crews digging through rubble on a Sunday morning. Dymer is a small town, not a front-line city — the strike hit a residential entrance, and the search for the 14 missing was still going on as the brief was written. Power generation and substations were hit in nine regions in earlier attacks this week, and emergency outages remain in force in Kyiv and several other regions. In Russia's Belgorod region, civilians are likewise living under daily attack warnings, with one person killed and six injured in the past day.

In short: a Russian drone collapsed part of an apartment building north of Kyiv on Sunday morning, leaving 14 people unaccounted for, capping a week in which Zelenskyy says Russia fired about 2,700 drones and 165 missiles at 20 regions — while Ukraine kept striking back into Russia's border regions.

Sources: Reuters, SANA (via Europe Says), Brussels Reporter

Ukraine — other developments

Sparta II still burning off Finland; sinking risk now the worry — The fire aboard the Russian cargo ship Sparta II, hit by two drones on Saturday in the Gulf of Finland, burned through the night and into Sunday, Reuters reports. Finnish, Estonian and Russian vessels are still spraying the ship with water cannons under Finnish command, and by midday Sunday the visible flames had subsided but the cargo stayed hot. The Finnish Border Guard warned the water being pumped aboard risks destabilising the hull — the ship has developed a slight list and sinking cannot be ruled out. The plan is for Russian rescue vessels to tow it out of Finnish waters once conditions permit. No oil has been reported entering the sea. Finland's military still says Ukraine was likely behind the strike; Kyiv has not commented. Estonia's navy commander said further attacks in the Gulf of Finland cannot be ruled out. Sources: Reuters, Helsinki Times

Poland/NATO — Council meets Monday; no Article 4 invoked — NATO's North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels on Monday to discuss Friday's violation of Polish airspace by two suspected Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone, with Poland represented by Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski. New details: a Swedish Gripen pilot patrolling Polish airspace received NATO authorisation to shoot down the suspected Kh-101 cruise missile but held fire because severe weather prevented visual confirmation, and Poland has not formally invoked Article 4 — Warsaw instead requested extra NATO assets and allied consultations. Polish authorities also warned on Sunday of Russian disinformation around unverified reports of a drone attack on the Kaliningrad exclave; Lithuania rejected allegations its territory or airspace was used for any such attack, and Moscow has not commented on Friday's incursion. Sources: Europe Says, Europe Says

Google's Gemini can now control Mac computers and perform tasks — Google rolled out "computer use" in the Gemini app for Mac on Thursday, November 19, Bloomberg reports: the assistant can click, type, navigate menus and fill in forms across desktop apps, local files and websites, working in the background while the user keeps control of the screen. It stops for logins and payments and needs confirmation for sensitive actions. For now it is US-only, English-only, for paying subscribers on Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra plans, on Apple silicon Macs running macOS Sequoia 15 or later — no Windows version or expansion timeline announced. It matters because it moves Gemini from answering questions to doing work on a personal computer, and it raises the pressure on Apple to make Siri more capable. Catch-up item — the launch was Thursday and was missed by Friday's briefs. Source: Bloomberg (via The Hindu BusinessLine)

Cybersecurity

Ruby Ships Fixes for 7 Flaws, Including Critical Ruby2Shell, Said to Hit Default Rails Apps Pre-Auth — Ruby released fixes on November 18 for seven memory-safety flaws in its regex engine and string case-conversion code. The worst, CVE-2026-132825 ("Ruby2Shell"), is a one-byte heap overflow when Ruby changes the case of a string in single-byte encodings like ISO-8859-1 or Windows-1252, and researchers at Veria Labs say it is reachable over the network, without authentication, in default Rails and classic Sinatra apps — Rack changes the case of a request field during normal form processing, so a fresh Rails app hits the flaw before its own code runs. The Ruby team says it is aware of proof-of-concept exploits showing code execution for some of the seven flaws. Fixed in Ruby 4.0.10, 3.4.13 and 3.3.14; no known in-the-wild exploitation yet, and the researchers plan to publish their PoC about a week after disclosure. Self-hosted software that bundles Ruby — GitLab, GitHub Enterprise Server, Mastodon, Discourse — is also exposed. Sources: The Hacker News, Orca Security, Red Hat

USDA cyber incident disrupted farmer payments for a week (catch-up, Nov 19–20) — A cybersecurity incident at the US Department of Agriculture took Farm Service Agency loan software and the Natural Resources Conservation Service's ProTracts payment system offline for most of last week. USDA's security operations centre uncovered the incident on Monday, November 16, and reset all database passwords in all environments, which is what took the applications down. Two former USDA officials told NBC News hackers breached a payment system; USDA has not confirmed a breach, and Under Secretary Richard Fordyce said no personal information was compromised and no data was lost, with most systems expected back by Friday evening. The disruption hit just as farmers face a December 11 deadline to enrol in 2026 ARC/PLC programmes. Sources: DTN, AgWeb

Huge Baltic SMS leak exposed 125 million messages (catch-up, Nov 11–12) — Cybernews researchers found an unauthenticated ClickHouse database run by CSC Telecom's Latvian branch holding 125 million+ SMS messages from October 2022 to September 2026, updating in real time — including one-time login codes, password-reset links, medical test results, and bank alerts with IBANs, amounts and balances from Swedbank and SEB, plus airBaltic flight details and messages from Latvia's 112 emergency line. The database was found September 28 and closed October 27; CSC Telecom says its preliminary investigation found no data was downloaded, and Cybernews says it has no evidence of malicious access. Swedbank has stopped using the provider and opened an investigation. Source: Cybernews

F5 BIG-IP emergency patches are out — F5 released out-of-band hotfixes on Wednesday, November 18, for six critical (9.8) stack-based buffer overflow flaws in BIG-IP's data plane that let an unauthenticated attacker crash the Traffic Management Microkernel and possibly execute code remotely. Each flaw needs a specific virtual-server setup (an iRule using a particular command, or an APM policy with two particular agents). No exploitation has been reported and there is no workaround — install the engineering hotfixes. Follow-up to the November 18 disclosure already covered. Source: BleepingComputer

TfL Oyster photocard breach now confirmed — Transport for London has confirmed that details of about 265,000 Oyster photocard holders may have been exposed after suspicious activity was detected on the photocard website on November 16, including names, emails, phone numbers, home addresses, dates of birth and security questions and answers. Passwords, payment card details and journey information were not affected, TfL says, and the incident has been reported to the Information Commissioner's Office. This corroborates the single-source report from November 18. Source: Daily CISO

Java4Shell: still no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation, not in CISA KEV. The week's reviews describe it as proving tricky to exploit in practice; GreyNoise is seeing only trigger-shaped probing.

Also: Rclone's serve command has an authentication bypass in versions 1.51.0–1.75.0 — update to 1.76.0 or later. Researchers demonstrated a Ghostscript sandbox-escape RCE via crafted file uploads in popular apps; the flaw is now patched. Source: appsec.fyi

Reminder: the CISA KEV patch deadline is tomorrow, Monday, November 23, for the four flaws added Friday — VMware vCenter, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver and Apache Roller (covered in Friday's briefs).
