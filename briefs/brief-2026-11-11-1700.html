Evening brief — Wednesday, November 11, 2026

Nvidia's board is preparing to oust Jensen Huang — hours after a second affair was reported

Nvidia's board is preparing to remove its founder and chief executive, Jensen Huang, after reports of two extramarital affairs — one with a woman US intelligence officials believe is a Chinese spy, and a second, reported today, with a Nvidia executive who reports to him. The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the matter, that the board is preparing to meet as soon as today to determine Huang's future. No final decision has been announced, and Huang remains CEO. Nvidia's shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading on the news.

The second affair was reported earlier on Wednesday by The New York Times, citing five people who worked at the company. The other party is Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports to Huang. Two of the sources told the Times the pair were videotaped being affectionate at a San Jose hotel during a Nvidia conference in March — the paper says it has not seen the video — and three people briefed on the incident said Huang's security then cornered the person filming and tried to get them to sign a non-disclosure agreement. The Times contacted Huang by phone; he replied, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly," and hung up. Neither Nvidia nor Powell responded to the Times's request for comment. Such relationships are barred under Nvidia's corporate policy.

The first affair, with Alice Huang — a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer whom US officials believe was recruited by Chinese intelligence — was admitted by Huang in a staff memo on Sunday, November 8, the same day the board opened an independent investigation, now led by former acting US Attorney Joon H. Kim and the firm Wachtell Lipton. The board has said it has "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but called that a preliminary position. On Wednesday, Nvidia told CNBC that "Jensen is committed to continuing to lead NVIDIA as CEO without interruption." The New York Times also reported on Tuesday, via Reuters, that Secretary of State Marco Rubio briefed President Trump aboard Air Force One in May about US intelligence indicating Huang was involved with a woman believed to be a Chinese spy.

The pressure is also coming from Congress, and today is the deadline. Three House Republican chairs — Brian Mast (Foreign Affairs), John Moolenaar (China committee) and Rick Crawford (Intelligence) — asked Huang on November 4 to attend a closed-door joint briefing on November 18 and to answer by today. Moolenaar said on Fox Business on Wednesday that Huang had until 5 p.m. ET to agree or offer another date, and that if he does not, "I believe there will likely be subpoenas for him to appear." Mast said his committee was preparing a subpoena, and Reuters reported, citing a person familiar with the discussions, that the lawmakers are unlikely to let Huang send his general counsel in his place. No subpoena has been issued, and Nvidia has not publicly said whether Huang will attend. One complication for the lawmakers: Republicans lose control of House committees in January, so a subpoena issued now would likely lapse and need to be reissued by Democrats. Senator Kevin Cramer, a North Dakota Republican, became the first sitting senator to call on Huang to step down on Tuesday, and Baird downgraded Nvidia to Neutral on Wednesday, slashing its price target to $200 from $500.

What each side argues has barely changed, but the stakes have. Lawmakers say the reported relationship raises a national-security question about who has access to the world's most important AI chips and want answers under oath or in a closed briefing. Nvidia's position is that its own checks found no evidence its chips were diverted, that Megaspeed denies diverting them, and that the board's independent inquiry will examine all available information. The board's move today is about the second affair as much as the first: a subordinate relationship is a straightforward policy violation, and it is what appears to have pushed a usually deferential board toward acting before its own investigation finishes. For ordinary people, the immediate impact is market, not product: Nvidia is down roughly $1.7 trillion in value since October 6, and the company reports earnings on November 17 — the day before the requested Capitol Hill briefing.

In short: the board is preparing to decide Huang's future as soon as tonight, after the Times reported a second affair with a subordinate, while Congress's 5 p.m. deadline for him to agree to a November 18 briefing passes with a subpoena threat on the table. Nothing is final yet — no decision announced, no subpoena issued — but the founder-CEO of the world's most valuable AI chipmaker is now fighting on three fronts at once: his board, Congress, and the market.

Abu Dhabi follow-up — "biggest" prisoner swap agreed, numbers to be set in Moscow tomorrow

The Abu Dhabi talks ended Wednesday with one concrete result beyond what this morning's brief reported: Jared Kushner said the sides agreed to "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners" of the war, with the exact numbers to be finalised when he and Steve Witkoff meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday. Both delegations confirmed the exchange in principle — Ukraine's Rustem Umerov and Russia's Vladimir Medinsky — though no figure was given; the scale discussed was 1,000-for-1,000, possibly 1,500. The Kremlin confirmed Thursday's meeting, and the envoys will then fly to Dublin to meet Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who is attending the European Political Community summit. Zelenskyy said on Wednesday that progress "hinges on US approach to Russia" and that Kyiv hoped for concrete results this year. The one open fight is Europe's role: Russia declined to meet European officials in Abu Dhabi and, per EU reporting, rejected an EU role in the talks; Kushner said he will raise broadening the talks to include the Europeans with Putin. (NY Post · Reuters · EU Perspectives)

Ukraine grid — follow-up to last night's strikes: 3 killed in Belgorod, Kyiv on water fountains, Crimea blacked out

The overnight strikes covered in this morning's brief kept rippling through Wednesday. Al Jazeera reports Ukrainian drone strikes killed three people in Russia's Belgorod region — up from the one death reported this morning — while in Ukraine, Russia's attacks on power generation and substations in nine regions left hours-long power and water outages across Kyiv and surrounding areas. Residents in the capital were urged to use public drinking-water fountains, and emergency centres offering hot tea and power points to charge devices were set up in the worst-hit areas. There were also power outages in Crimea, home to about 2.5 million people, after Ukrainian strikes hit energy infrastructure overnight; Russia's Energy Ministry said temporary outage schedules were in force. Russia's Defence Ministry said it downed 709 Ukrainian drones across 14 regions, Crimea and the Black and Azov seas — a Russian claim, unverified. (Al Jazeera)

Hormuz — another tanker incident Wednesday, and two named ships hit this week

The attacks on shipping have not stopped while the US and Iran argue over whether they are even talking. Iran's army said on Wednesday that a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine in the eastern Strait of Hormuz, in what it called a prohibited area — an Iranian claim, not independently confirmed. Britain's UKMTO confirmed only that it received a report of an incident involving a tanker in the strait, without identifying the vessel, the crew's status, the damage or the cause. Two ships are now named this week: India's shipping minister said the Saudi-flagged tanker NCC FAJR, carrying three Indian seafarers, was involved in a "possible attack" while transiting the strait on Wednesday — all crew safe — and the Liberia-flagged VLCC MT FUJAIRAH ENERGY was hit by a projectile on Tuesday while travelling from Al Ruwais to Dubai, injuring four crew including one Indian national. Reuters reports at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November after 37 in October, the highest monthly total since the war began, and the International Maritime Organization has confirmed 133 incidents in the region since late February, with at least 25 seafarers killed. Oil rebounded on Wednesday after touching two-month lows, as the fresh attacks weakened hopes that a US-Iran de-escalation would quickly restore normal energy flows. On the diplomacy side, Iran's foreign ministry said Tehran was reviewing a US proposal and would convey its view to mediator Pakistan, while Rubio, speaking in Paris after G7 meetings, said he asked other G7 countries to help protect shipping through the strait — and got no takers. (BOE Report / Reuters · Daily Jagran (NCC FAJR / FUJAIRAH ENERGY) · Pollar News · HeadlinesXtra (oil rebound))

Houthi/Saudi — Wednesday's Riyadh attack confirmed by Reuters: 3 cruise missiles, plus drones aimed at Mecca

What this morning's brief had as a single-source developing item is now confirmed by Reuters: Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles and drones launched towards Riyadh, Mecca and other regions on Wednesday. The coalition said it destroyed three cruise missiles headed for the Riyadh region, two drones aimed at Mecca and southern areas, and two missiles pointed west. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree later denied targeting Mecca, saying the group's operations target oil facilities and military bases. Saudi civil defence issued alerts warning of potential danger in Taif, Yanbu and Khamis Mushait later on Wednesday, then announced the danger had passed. No casualties or damage were reported. Wednesday's interceptions were the second consecutive day of Houthi missiles launched towards Riyadh, and the third attack on the capital in four days — an all-cruise-missile salvo, which gives radars far less warning than a ballistic missile fired from Yemen. (Reuters via KTWB · Silent Dossier)

Poland — Nawrocki marched with nationalists on Independence Day; no Tusk meeting happened

Polish President Karol Nawrocki joined hundreds of thousands of right-wing demonstrators at the annual Independence Day march in Warsaw on Wednesday — city officials put the crowd at about 250,000, organisers at half a million or more — days after the Krakow shooting that authorities attribute to a far-right gunman. Nawrocki, a conservative nationalist, walked at the front of a crowd stretching more than 3 km, under the slogan "Poles are masters of their own land." Prime Minister Donald Tusk attended a separate rally in Szczecin and said in a video posted to X: "Today, on November 11th, we were supposed to reach out to one another. Mr. President, we were meant to meet" — the meeting the two had been expected to hold before Independence Day did not happen. Tusk later wrote that Nawrocki "could have tried to unite Poles rather than divide them." Warsaw police said 59 people were detained in the capital, mostly for drugs, drone-ban breaches and pyrotechnics. Justice Minister Waldemar Zurek said the Krakow attacker had been interested in Anders Behring Breivik, the Norwegian far-right extremist who killed 77 people in 2011. (AP via EuropeSays · Reuters via KWSN)

Israel — Eisenkot pledges fast legislation before a government is even formed

With no coalition yet, Gadi Eisenkot's bloc is moving to use the Knesset it now effectively controls: Le Monde reports the bloc aims to move quickly on legislation, with the first major law creating a commission of inquiry, with investigative powers, into the October 7, 2023 security failure and Netanyahu's personal responsibility. The coalition also intends to begin rolling back laws passed by the previous Knesset affecting the judiciary, police and media. Matan Kahana, Eisenkot's candidate for Knesset speaker, is expected to be elected in a vote scheduled for November 17 — Arab-list representatives confirmed on Tuesday they would vote for him — and Avigdor Lieberman is set to head the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee. Negotiations continue behind the scenes to persuade one or two ultra-Orthodox lists to join a majority, possibly at the cost of softening the conscription-reform promise. Eisenkot has up to 42 days from his appointment on Sunday to form a government. (Le Monde)

Cyber

Trump nominated Palantir's security chief to run CISA — catch-up from Monday

President Trump nominated Palantir chief information security officer Michael LoSapio on Monday to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, seeking to end a vacancy that has stretched nearly 22 months since Jen Easterly departed in January 2025. LoSapio has been Palantir's CISO since September 2024, after leading its infrastructure and network security team since 2017. Trump's first nominee, Sean Plankey, withdrew in April after waiting more than a year. The nomination faces a crowded lame-duck Senate calendar, and if LoSapio is not confirmed before the current Congress ends, Trump would have to nominate him again for consideration by the incoming Democratic majority. Acting director Nick Andersen has run the agency since February. (Nextgov · Cybersecurity Dive)

Courts

FTC expands Amazon Prime refunds — cap raised to $200, payments now automatic

The FTC announced today that Amazon will accelerate and expand payments to eligible consumers under last year's $2.5 billion Prime settlement, after a federal court approved a joint FTC-Amazon motion this week. More consumers now qualify for refunds — including those who used between 11 and 20 Prime benefits in a year, who were excluded from earlier phases — the maximum payment cap rises from $51 to $200 total, and all future payments will be distributed automatically, with no claim or paperwork needed. As of September 2026, Amazon had issued more than $845 million in redress. The settlement resolved FTC allegations that Amazon enrolled millions in Prime without consent and made cancellation deliberately hard; Amazon did not admit liability. (Tourism TAS)
