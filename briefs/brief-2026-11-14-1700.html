Evening brief — Saturday, November 14, 2026 (~5 PM CT)

Ukraine hits Russia's biggest oil-blending hub and a bomber airbase — as Moscow rules out any land swap

Ukraine struck two high-value targets deep inside Russia on Friday, its General Staff announced on Saturday: the Samara oil transfer station — the hub where Russia blends its flagship Urals export crude — and the Lipetsk-2 military airfield, home to the Su-34 bombers that drop glide bombs on Ukrainian cities. The same day, Moscow's chief negotiator flatly ruled out the territorial trade at the heart of this week's diplomacy, saying there is "no possibility" of exchanging land.

According to Ukraine's General Staff, long-range drones hit the Samara linear production and dispatch station near the village of Prosvet, about 950 kilometers east of Ukrainian-controlled territory, setting several crude oil storage tanks ablaze. Ukraine's Security Service (SBU) said its Alpha special operations center carried out the strike and confirmed hits on storage tanks. The station is one of the most important nodes in Russia's energy export system: it receives crude by pipeline from Tatarstan, western Siberia and Kazakhstan and blends it into Urals, which accounts for up to half of Russia's crude oil exports. It covers more than 216 hectares and holds 71 storage tanks with a combined capacity above 1.6 million cubic meters. Samara's regional governor, Vyacheslav Fedorishchev, confirmed a drone attack on an industrial site in the region and said four people were killed, though he did not say what was hit. The facility has been struck repeatedly — this was the sixth attack since October 2, and Ukrainian outlets report roughly 40 percent of its tank system was already damaged or destroyed by October 11. A separate analysis notes that in the last 15 days Ukraine has hit 13 refineries and oil-and-gas facilities, including all six of Russia's largest refineries.

The second strike hit Lipetsk-2 airfield, more than 340 kilometers east of the Ukrainian border. According to the General Staff, a hangar storing specialized aircraft maintenance equipment, aviation fuel tanks and the airfield's central command-and-control post were hit on November 13. The base hosts Su-34, Su-35 and MiG-31 aircraft, and Lipetsk-2 has been targeted repeatedly before — Ukrainian military intelligence says pro-Ukrainian partisans set fire to two Su-30 jets at a Lipetsk-area airfield in December 2025. There is no independent picture of the damage at either site, and Moscow has not issued a response on the strikes themselves.

The two sides are now publicly talking past each other on the diplomacy. On Wednesday, after two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, Russia's chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky told state news agency TASS there is "no possibility" of a territorial exchange between Russia and Ukraine, adding that "every inch of Russian land will be recovered, one way or another" and that Russia's buffer zone along the border would keep expanding. That directly answers the idea President Zelensky revealed this morning — that US negotiators in Abu Dhabi had floated Ukraine withdrawing from Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. Zelensky said Ukraine will not give up territory; Medinsky says Russia will not give any back. The Abu Dhabi talks did produce one thing both sides reported: an agreement in principle on a major prisoner exchange. US mediators said the talks showed a lack of trust between the warring sides, with territory the central unresolved issue.

For ordinary people, the Samara strike is the war's economic front made visible: Urals crude pays for Russia's war effort, and Ukraine's campaign against refineries and blending hubs is aimed at the money, not just the battlefield. Russian regions have already begun reintroducing fuel rationing as processing capacity falls, and the International Energy Agency said Russian oil processing will be "severely curtailed" through year-end. On the Ukrainian side, the overnight toll reported this morning — seven killed and 80 injured across the country, including a family killed in Belgorod and 50,000 without power in Odesa — is a reminder that the strikes go both ways and civilians pay for both.

In short: Ukraine hit the heart of Russia's oil-export system and a bomber base in one day, while Moscow ruled out any territorial swap — closing the door on the Donbas-for-NATO idea Zelensky says Washington floated. Watch whether the prisoner-exchange agreement holds, and whether the November 19 trilateral in Shenzhen produces anything beyond the nuclear-arms and Iran agenda it is officially about.

Gulf escalation goes to the UN as the US warns Americans in Saudi Arabia — and the fighting shifts to Yemen's south

The Gulf standoff moved into the UN Security Council on Friday, the US Embassy in Saudi Arabia issued a nationwide warning to Americans the same day, and the Houthis opened a new front against Yemeni government forces in the south — a day in which every side told a different story about the same war.

The Security Council met on Friday at Bahrain's request to discuss threats to maritime security in the Persian Gulf. The Gulf Cooperation Council's secretary-general told the Council that since February 28, GCC states have faced Iranian strikes involving more than 7,000 missiles and drones targeting airports, ports, oil and gas facilities, power stations, desalination plants and residential areas, and that roughly 1,600 vessels have been disrupted in the Strait of Hormuz with more than 23,000 seafarers stranded. A UN official cited International Maritime Organization data: 134 incidents and 25 seafarer deaths in the strait and the wider Middle East maritime area as of November 11, with October the worst month for shipping attacks since February. Lloyd's List put the October figure at 37 ship attacks in the Hormuz area — the highest monthly total since the war began — with at least 14 confirmed so far in November. Iran's ambassador blamed the instability on US and Israeli military action; the Gulf states accused Iran and the Houthis.

Hours earlier, the US Embassy in Saudi Arabia issued a nationwide security alert on November 13 warning American citizens of an increased Houthi attack threat, urging "extreme caution" at the kingdom's airports and telling Americans to stay away from sites linked to Western interests. It landed the same day the Houthis claimed drone strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and the East-West oil pipeline, and as the Revolutionary Guard claimed more tanker mine strikes in the strait — claims no maritime authority has confirmed.

On the ground in Yemen, the Institute for the Study of War reports the Houthis launched ballistic missile salvos on November 13 and 14 at Yemeni government-aligned forces' staging areas and weapons depots in Lahij and Aden governorates — Jabal Kharaz and Khor Amira in Madaribah wa al Arah district, and Aden city — aiming to disrupt the logistics behind a government offensive in southeastern Lahij. Government-aligned forces have nonetheless kept recapturing territory, consolidating control of the Sarih junction, and Saudi aircraft have provided close air support, striking Houthi commanders, fighters and vehicles on the frontline and hitting reinforcements moving south from al Turbah. The Houthis also claimed strikes on Saudi targets on November 13 — drones at King Khalid International Airport and an East-West Pipeline pumping station, and ballistic missiles at Saudi forces in Tuwaal, Jazan province. Saudi Arabia separately intercepted Houthi drones near Riyadh and southern Saudi Arabia on November 14, according to the coalition. The Houthi-run Health Ministry said 12 civilians were killed in a Saudi airstrike on a market in Taiz on Saturday — a claim with no independent confirmation.

Meanwhile Iran's mine claims keep coming with no verification. The Revolutionary Guard Navy claimed on November 13 that a liquefied gas carrier and two oil tankers struck mines and exploded or caught fire on "unauthorized routes," and the regular navy claimed another tanker hit a mine east of the strait on November 14. ISW notes Iran has recently started emphasizing in its claims that the vessels were under US escort, in what looks like an information operation to discredit US escort operations — and that it has seen no indicators Iran has successfully re-mined the southern route, which US Central Command said it fully demined on August 27.

For ordinary people, the practical picture is unchanged but hardening: shipping through the strait is still running roughly 65 percent below pre-war volume, the US Embassy is telling Americans to avoid Saudi airports and Western-linked sites, and the fighting in Yemen is now hitting government forces' staging areas in the south as well as Saudi cities. Oil, for now, is ignoring it — Brent settled Friday at $95.84, down about 2.5 percent on the week, with the International Energy Agency saying supply exceeded demand for the first time since the war began.

In short: the Gulf crisis is now a UN argument, a US travel warning, and a ground war in southern Yemen at the same time — while Iran's daily mine claims remain unverified and oil prices keep falling on oversupply. Watch for any UKMTO confirmation of the latest tanker claims, and for whether the government offensive in Lahij holds.

Nvidia's board has approached Arm's CEO about the top job, FT reports

Nvidia's board has approached Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas as a potential successor to Jensen Huang, the Financial Times reported on Friday. There is no guarantee he will be offered the job or would accept it, and the board said it was in the process of selecting an executive search firm and had not formally engaged any potential candidates. Colette Kress remains interim CEO while also serving as CFO, and longtime board member Harvey Jones has taken over as chairman. Other names floated by industry watchers include AMD chief Lisa Su, Micron chief Sanjay Mehrotra, Marvell chief Matt Murphy and Apple's hardware chief Johny Srouji — and President Trump has publicly pushed former Apple CEO Tim Cook. Earnings are Tuesday, November 17, Kress's first call as interim chief. Why it matters: Nvidia is the world's most important AI chip company and its stock is down more than 30 percent since early October; who runs it decides how the export-control fight around its China sales plays out.

Cybersecurity

SilentAir: a state-level actor has been hijacking mobile networks to intercept calls and 2FA codes

SilentAir hijacks Asian telecom nodes to intercept SMS 2FA codes — Telecom security firm Enea disclosed on November 11 that a state-level cyberespionage actor it calls SilentAir has been using compromised equipment inside mobile operators in at least six Asian countries to intercept the calls and text messages of selected subscribers — mainly in Taiwan, including political and government figures — and to harvest incoming SMS two-factor authentication codes. The group sends forged SS7 and Diameter location-update messages into the shared IPX interconnect network, telling the target's home network that the attacker's node now serves the target's phone, so inbound calls and SMS get rerouted through it. Enea observed 2FA codes being intercepted immediately after some diversions took effect, pointing to attempts to access accounts registered to the same phone number — though it did not establish whether any account takeover succeeded. Interception made up 18.3 percent of SilentAir's observed activity, versus 2.1 percent for a typical commercial surveillance firm. Enea has tracked the activity since early 2025 and assesses with high confidence that SilentAir is state-level, but with low confidence that it is China-nexus; it says the group is disrupted for now but could return, and the compromised equipment has not been confirmed cleaned up. The same Enea report documents GTPDoor v3, a Linux implant inside operator core networks that now tunnels its command-and-control through GTP-U, the mobile data protocol still used in 5G. Why it matters: there is no patch for this — the flaw is the trust model of the global signalling network itself, and SMS-based 2FA is only as safe as the carrier routing it. If you rely on SMS codes for anything important, an authenticator app or hardware key is the fix.

Sources: Enea via Malaysian Wireless, ThreatAft, Undercode News, Hacker Posts, Reef Sentinel on GTPDoor v3

Israel: Eisenkot rejects Netanyahu's unity offer; talks set for Sunday

Caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu renewed his appeal on Saturday for a national unity government with Gadi Eisenkot, the rival now negotiating a coalition of his own — and Eisenkot rejected it within minutes as a political ploy. Netanyahu said a meeting with Eisenkot on Thursday had strengthened his view that Israel urgently needed a broad-based government, citing Iran as the central security challenge, and urged talks without preconditions. Eisenkot said Netanyahu's appeal was a bluff, pointing out that negotiating teams from Likud and his Yashar party had already scheduled a meeting for Sunday, November 15 — and said he would sit with Likud members if their commitment to unity was sincere, but ruled out serving in a government led by Netanyahu. Under Eisenkot's plan, his 48-lawmaker bloc would back a minority government while the 46 who support Netanyahu vote against and 26 members from Arab and ultra-Orthodox parties abstain. Lawmakers are due to elect a Knesset speaker on Tuesday, November 17; Eisenkot has nominated Matan Kahana and challenged Netanyahu to back him as a test of good faith. Why it matters: the October 27 election produced no majority, and whether a minority government relying on Arab-party abstentions can take office decides who governs Israel while the Iran standoff runs.

Sources: Times of Israel via LaPresse, France 24 via Globe TV, Anadolu via Ungated
