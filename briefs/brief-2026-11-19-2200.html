Night brief — Thursday, November 19, 2026

Russia hits Kyiv with ballistic missiles overnight — the retaliation Putin promised

Russia launched a large-scale overnight missile and drone attack on Kyiv and regions across Ukraine in the early hours of Friday, November 20 — hours after Vladimir Putin vowed that Russia's response to Ukraine's strikes on the Kerch Bridge would be "far more painful" than the attacks themselves. Explosions began in the capital at about 1:25 a.m. local time (5:25 p.m. CT Thursday), and multiple waves of missiles followed through the night.

Ukrainian monitoring channels reported Iskander ballistic missiles inbound, and Russia launched Tu-160 strategic bombers from Ukrainka Air Base in Russia's Far East — aircraft that can carry conventional and nuclear weapons, prompting warnings that every region of Ukraine was at risk. Kyiv's military administration reported fires at residential buildings in the Obolonskyi and Podilskyi districts, and Mayor Vitali Klitschko said an infrastructure facility in the Holosiivskyi district was struck, with an office building on the site on fire; nonresidential buildings in Podilskyi were also burning, and a strike hit an open area near one of the city's bridges. A Reuters witness heard a series of explosions across the city through the early hours. The attack extended beyond the capital: a "massive enemy drone attack" hit a major infrastructure facility in Kryvyi Rih, according to the city's defence council, and in Kharkiv a Russian strike hit a medical facility in the Shevchenkivskyi district, with people reported injured. No casualties had been reported from the overnight strikes on Kyiv at the time of the initial reports, and the full extent of the damage was still unclear. This remains a developing story.

The two sides are arguing from opposite ends of the same logic. Kyiv says its bridge and refinery strikes are retaliation for Russia's nightly campaign against Ukrainian power plants and cities, and that they will continue until Russia stops hitting Ukrainian infrastructure. Putin, speaking in Shenzhen after his trilateral meeting with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, said Russia would not agree to any ceasefire covering energy infrastructure or Black Sea shipping after the bridge attacks, and warned of a response "far more painful than what (Ukraine) did to the bridge." Moscow calls the bridge civilian infrastructure — even as it is a key supply route for Russian forces in occupied Crimea — while its own energy strikes on Ukraine continue. Washington and its European allies are split on the diplomacy: Trump embraced Putin as a "friend" at the Shenzhen summit, where the three leaders signed a four-point "declaration of peace" that did not mention Ukraine or Iran; European leaders in Berlin warned on Thursday against any attempt to decide a peace settlement over Ukraine's head, and after the summit Zelenskyy and European leaders issued a joint statement urging Russia to accept a ceasefire covering energy infrastructure and Black Sea shipping corridors.

For people on the ground, the war has removed the idea of a safe hour. Thursday's daytime drone attack on Kyiv killed a 26-year-old man, injured five others, and damaged two European embassies and St. Volodymyr's Cathedral; the overnight barrage then hit homes and a medical facility in Kharkiv while much of Kyiv is already living on around four hours of electricity a day. In Crimea, the bridge's rail link remains disrupted, with ferries due to start running from Friday as the fallback.

In short: Putin's promised retaliation arrived within hours of his Shenzhen warning, in the form of a multi-wave ballistic missile attack on Kyiv and strikes across Ukraine — while the diplomacy meant to stop it produced a declaration that said nothing about Ukraine at all. Watch for the casualty and damage tolls as daylight comes, and whether the attacks continue into Friday night.

Short items

JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard customer data leaked after third-party vendors hacked

Ransomware group n0n leaked customer data tied to Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase on Thursday after breaching third-party service providers — not the companies' own systems. JPMorgan said attackers accessed marketing data held by its vendors, potentially including customer names and email addresses, and will contact affected customers directly with complimentary credit monitoring. A Reuters review of the leaked data found about 16,000 customer email addresses — some with phone numbers and postal codes — plus 124 employee email addresses, apparently tied to the bank's invitation-only Reserve credit card program. Visa said the incident involved a third-party provider in Asia-Pacific and no payment data such as card numbers was affected; Mastercard said a limited amount of non-payments information from a small subset of benefit-program cardholders was exposed. None of the three firms named the breached vendor. n0n, which emerged in September, listed the three companies on its leak site on November 16 with a 48-hour countdown before publishing.

*Sources: Wall Street Journal, Reuters via WTBX, New York Post*

VMware patched a vCenter zero-day exploited since September by a Chinese-linked group

Broadcom patched a critical VMware vCenter Server authentication bypass, CVE-2026-123890, on November 19 in VMSA-2026-0009. Google's Mandiant reports that suspected Chinese state-sponsored group Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221) exploited the flaw in zero-day attacks starting in early September, using a custom Golang tool called BONESAW to compromise vCenter servers — part of the same intrusion campaign as a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day exploited since late August. Broadcom calls it an emergency update and urges immediate patching. The advisory also fixes four other flaws in SDDC Manager and VMware Tools for Windows.

*Source: BleepingComputer*

Nvidia investigating a possible partner breach after ransomware gang lists it

Nvidia is investigating an incident that may have affected a third-party partner after the Gentlemen ransomware group added the chipmaker to its dark-web leak site, with a countdown ending November 28. The gang has posted no proof or data samples, and Nvidia says it has found no indication its own systems or networks were affected. "We continue to monitor the situation," a spokesperson said. The claim is unverified. Nvidia confirmed a separate partner breach earlier this year, in May, when a regional GeForce NOW Alliance partner was hacked.

*Sources: BleepingComputer, The Register*

California attorney general subpoenaed Nvidia over Huang and China ties

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said Thursday his office served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia related to reports of former CEO Jensen Huang's alleged affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Bonta said the office is examining whether Nvidia's or Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information, and whether the company misled investors and the public about the risk. The House Foreign Affairs Committee said Thursday the Trump administration had approved shipments of nearly 900,000 advanced Nvidia and AMD AI chips to Chinese companies between December 2025 and May this year, though it was unclear how many were ultimately shipped. Nvidia said the approvals were subject to strict conditions and limited volumes, and that it had stopped shipping H200 chips to China. The SEC is also reportedly probing Nvidia.

*Source: Reuters via WNCY*

NGINX2Root: 8.8 million services still running exposed versions

Around 8.8 million NGINX services may still be open to the NGINX2Root flaws, according to Censys' live tracker — services running an affected version with HTTP/2 enabled. The five memory bugs, disclosed November 18, include CVE-2026-123007, a decade-old HTTP/2 flaw present since 2015 that lets an unauthenticated remote user run commands as the NGINX worker; Xint chained it with a Linux kernel flaw to get root and break out of a Docker container. Patch to NGINX 1.30.7 (stable) or 1.31.9 (mainline), or NGINX Plus 37.0.8.1 / 37.3.0.1. Censys has built a small cleartext HTTP/2 test so defenders can check patches without sending the 60–119 GB of traffic Rapid7's proof of concept needs. No exploitation in the wild had been seen as of November 18, and none of the five CVEs are in CISA's KEV catalog.

*Source: Cybersecurity News*

Infoblox investigating a breach that came in through a supplier

Infoblox told The Register it is investigating unauthorised access to company information through a compromised third-party data migration vendor. The vendor had trusted access to Infoblox's SharePoint, and intruders reportedly used its accounts to take data including saved NIOS snapshots for some customers (backups of DNS, DHCP and grid configuration) and employee data. Infoblox says it found no sign its products, cloud services, software updates or build process were involved, and has not said how many customers or staff are affected or named the vendor.

*Source: KB Technologies*

Anthropic's seven founders cashed out nearly $2B in the record IPO

Anthropic's seven founders each sold about 3.4 million shares in Thursday's IPO, pocketing roughly $280 million apiece before fees and taxes — $1.95 billion combined. The founders pledged to dedicate 80% of their personal equity to philanthropy, though the filings say those pledges are voluntary and revocable. All told, Anthropic raised about $104.8 billion for its balance sheet: $79.38 billion from newly issued public shares plus the $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N shares to Google, Nvidia ($10 billion each), AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC. Amazon remains the largest disclosed shareholder at about 14.6%, Google about 13.4%. Anthropic expects to need roughly $70.7 billion for compute and infrastructure over the next 12 months.

*Source: TechCrunch*

Gulf: Qatar calls a Hormuz closure a 'global crisis' as the US widens its blockade

Qatar warned on Thursday that a closure of the Strait of Hormuz would be a "global crisis" and urged diplomacy with Iran, while the US expanded its blockade of Iranian ports and coastline to include any ship that receives cargo via ship-to-ship transfer, according to the Joint Maritime Information Center. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed Thursday that two supertankers carrying more than 4 million barrels of oil caught fire after hitting naval mines on an "unauthorised route" — still unverified, with no vessel names, flags or casualties released, and no independent confirmation. The LPG tanker Tara Gas, struck twice earlier in the week, had its fire extinguished. Oil edged up Friday — Brent $92.22, WTI $83.40 — but both benchmarks are set for a third straight weekly decline, and crude shipments through Hormuz have returned to roughly pre-war levels.

*Sources: Reuters, Straits Live, The Library Estate*

Google is putting AI chats on its homepage

Google is adding a collapsible sidebar to its homepage so people can see and continue their AI Mode searches, with past AI threads sitting next to the search box. The change reflects Google's own diagnosis that search is turning from a one-shot tool into an ongoing conversation. No rollout date was given.

*Source: The Verge via Daily AI Thread*
