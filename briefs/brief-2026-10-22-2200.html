1. Iran/US + Yemen/Saudi + Hormuz

A second outlet confirms the post-midterm strike planning — and Tehran answers that it is ready for war or diplomacy

The reported US-Israel plan for renewed strikes on Iran, covered in the evening brief from the New York Times, gained a second, independent confirmation on Thursday. According to NPR, a person briefed on US and Israeli war planning said the two countries are preparing a possible attack in early November, after the US midterm elections on November 3, with US forces being amassed in the region to be ready by then. The source added a detail the Times account did not: Israel had initially planned to strike in mid-October, about two weeks before Israel's own elections on October 27, but President Donald Trump objected.

The same source cautioned, as the Times reporting did, that the preparations could also be a pressure campaign to force a diplomatic deal — and that no strike has been publicly approved. The White House line remains that the US will not attack Iran before the midterms. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, meanwhile, is campaigning on a threat to topple Iran's regime, NPR notes, and told a rally this week that regime change is "the only way to end this."

Iran's answer came from Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, who told Iranian state media on Thursday: "We are just as prepared for diplomacy as we are for war," adding that "any scenario is conceivable, including the possibility of an attack by the US and Israel." Iranian state media also quoted a security source warning that if Iranian officials or civilian infrastructure were attacked, Iran would retaliate against US interests in the region and against countries participating in the war — and the Revolutionary Guard has repeated its threat to target Gulf data centres and undersea cables. The Institute for the Study of War adds that Iran reportedly conducted more than 30 tests of medium-range solid-fuel Emad and Kheibar Shikan ballistic missiles on October 11, almost certainly in preparation for a possible renewed conflict.

For ordinary people, the cost of the war that is already happening — not just the one being planned — got a stark new measure on Thursday. NPR cites a new International Monetary Fund report saying disruptions to energy production, shipping, tourism and trade have delivered substantial shocks across the Gulf: Iraq, Kuwait and Qatar are all expected to see economies contract by around 16% this year, and Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates are also in recession this year. The IMF warns that if the war continues and energy disruptions persist, oil will average $130 a barrel in 2027. That is the backdrop to Brent still sitting above $113 and US gasoline still above $4 a gallon.

Caption: An Institute for the Study of War map of the Houthis' October 22 claimed strikes on Saudi targets, including the defence ministry in Riyadh, Yanbu, Abha and Dammam. It matters because the geographic spread — from the capital to the Red Sea coast — is the point: the Houthis are trying to make the whole kingdom feel exposed, not just one airport or oil field.

In short: this is still a reported plan, not an order — but now two outlets, the Times and NPR, have independently described the same early-November window, and both Tehran and Washington are talking as if the decision point is real. Watch for any White House confirmation or denial beyond the pre-midterm pledge, for Netanyahu's election on October 27, and for whether the force buildup NPR describes becomes visible in the Gulf.

2. Ukraine/Russia + Poland

630 drones at Moscow, a prisoner exchange — and still no trust in the talks

Hours after Russia's 21-missile barrage on Kyiv, Ukraine answered with its own mass strike, and the two sides still managed a prisoner exchange — a combination that captures where this war is: talking about talks, fighting harder. According to the New York Post, citing Russian officials, Ukraine fired 630 drones toward Moscow overnight, with at least two people injured in the Russian capital, according to Mayor Sergei Sobyanin, and at least one death and one injury in the Belgorod region. Russia said its air defences intercepted more than 800 drones in total, per other reporting of the same exchange. The Associated Press, meanwhile, puts the Kyiv toll at one killed and 22 wounded in the capital itself, while Ukrainian officials put the wider toll at two killed and 34 injured across 12 regions, including five children — the difference is geography, not contradiction: the AP figure is Kyiv, the wider figure is nationwide.

Zelenskyy framed the Kyiv strike as deliberate, not incidental. According to the Post, he said at least three of the missiles carried cluster munitions, that the targets included Kyiv's historic centre, monastery buildings, homes, shops and cafes, and that "the choice of weapon was deliberate, the choice of target area was deliberate, and so was the intent to inflict as much loss and damage on civilians as possible." Russia has not publicly addressed the cluster-munition claim. Moscow's position, via Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, remains talks only after November 10, possibly in Abu Dhabi, with major disagreements over Donetsk and NATO membership unresolved; Ushakov added that the "nuances" from the Oslo round "do not entirely satisfy" Moscow. Zelenskyy said he would support talks but added, "We'll see what happens this time," shortly before air-raid alerts sounded over Kyiv.

The one concrete, verifiable step in the other direction was the exchange of war dead: Moscow handed over 252 bodies and Ukraine handed over 25, according to Belarusian state agency Belta, as reported by the Times of Earth. Such exchanges have accompanied moves toward talks in the past, but they are not a ceasefire, and they did not stop either night's bombardment. No clean lead image could be retrieved from the Kyiv article pages tonight, so this article runs without one rather than with a guessed image.

For ordinary Ukrainians, the human impact is unchanged and grim: homes, a monastery, a hypermarket, a postal cargo facility and transport infrastructure hit across Kyiv's districts, with winter approaching and energy infrastructure already under repeated attack. For ordinary Russians near the border, the Belgorod casualty shows the exchange runs both ways.

In short: the retaliation was as large as the original strike, the exchange was real but small against that scale, and the talks date is still disputed — Moscow after November 10, Kyiv wanting early November. Watch for a confirmed talks date and venue, for any US response on the reported Patriot interceptor shortage, and for whether the drone exchanges escalate further before any meeting.

4. Open source

Cloudflare's security-audit skill tops GitHub Trending — an agent auditor that tries to disprove its own findings

Cloudflare's open-source security-audit-skill turns a coding agent into a security auditor through a structured six-phase audit: reconnaissance mapping architecture and trust boundaries, coverage-led hunting by isolated agents, candidate validation where every finding goes to a fresh verifier whose job is to disprove it, structured output to findings.json, re-verification of source claims, and a final report. Each finding ends as confirmed, needs_validation (with the exact unresolved fact, no severity assigned), or rejected. It had 38,827 GitHub stars on October 22, topped GitHub Trending that day with 2,979 new stars, and is MIT licensed. Why it matters: it is the skill Cloudflare says seeded its own vulnerability-discovery harness, and the disproof step is exactly what most AI security tools skip. Digital Soul Hub

Also trending: strelov1/freehire (+396 stars, an open-source search engine for job seekers) and tgeorgiadis/quiver-launcher (+163, a launcher for downloading, installing and running apps from GitHub and GitLab releases, with a personal library and community catalogues). GitDiscover

5. Cybersecurity

Fortinet patches critical flaws — but won't say what they are until November 10

Fortinet released patches on October 22 for FortiOS, FortiManager and FortiAnalyzer (FortiOS 8.0.2, 7.6.8, 7.4.13, 7.2.14, and matching FortiManager/FortiAnalyzer builds; FortiMail and FortiAuthenticator patches followed on October 21) covering critical and high-severity flaws — but the CVE IDs and full advisories will not be published until November 10, nearly three weeks later. Fortinet urges customers not to wait: patch internet-facing devices first, and if you cannot patch, restrict admin access to trusted IPs, keep FortiManager off the internet, and enforce MFA. WatchTowr's warning, quoted by Cybernews, is blunt: the flaws will be rapidly reverse-engineered from the patches and exploited in the wild, and three weeks without details gives attackers a head start. No active exploitation has been reported yet. Cybernews

PHP2Shell fallout, Thursday evening: the patch gap in real time — The PHP flaws covered in the evening brief are now visibly knocking services offline while they patch: Pokémon Champions, Home and Sleep went down for maintenance while fixes roll out, and Wikimedia and BBC services were temporarily affected, according to Thursday reporting. Cloudflare observed exploit attempts rising from thousands to millions, though that counts attempts, not confirmed compromises. If you run PHP, the evening brief's advice stands: patch to 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35 or take the system offline. TheGamer · TBS News

Japanese cloud provider hacked — up to 26,739 customer servers affected — Attackers compromised the infrastructure of Onamae Desktop Cloud, a Japanese VPS service run by GMO Internet, and installed malicious programs on up to 26,739 customer servers. The company shut down customer servers from October 21 with no restart date given, says payment information stored separately was not affected, and has not confirmed data misuse. Customers should rotate any secrets stored on their servers and watch for phishing. YouTube / Cyber News roundup, October 22

Lazarus uses post-quantum cryptography to hide its malware — Kaspersky says North Korea-linked Lazarus used the ML-KEM post-quantum key mechanism to protect payload delivery in attacks on South Korean organisations, starting with phishing pages leading to crafted GitHub pages, abusing a legitimate Korean security tool, and delivering malware including LPEClient, ClientHub and the Cubridge backdoor, mostly in memory. At least three companies were identified, many more suspected. Why it matters: post-quantum crypto is now a tool for hiding attacks, not just defending against them. YouTube / Cyber News roundup, October 22

Yonsei University breach: 10,000+ people exposed — Yonsei University in Seoul confirmed Thursday that unauthorised external access to its Yonsei Portal Service exposed personal information of more than 10,000 students and alumni, including student ID numbers, mobile numbers and email addresses. The access route has been blocked and law enforcement asked to investigate. It follows an August breach at Sogang University affecting about 180,000 records. Aju Press

PingCAP breach, with an unverified extortion claim — Database vendor PingCAP disclosed attackers used stolen service-account credentials to access internal systems between September 9 and October 7, copying data and source code. PingCAP says no customer databases were accessed, but billing data, contract details, account metadata, email addresses and SQL editor text were exposed. The extortion group FulcrumSec claims far more; that claim is unverified. YouTube / Cyber News roundup, October 22

6. Major court cases

Russell Brand trial, Thursday: complainants' evidence concludes, defence case next week

All six women have now testified at Southwark Crown Court, and the defence case is set to begin in the week of October 26, according to a trial tracker updated October 22. Thursday's evidence, covered in the evening brief, included a friend's account of an alleged 2009 encounter and a 999 call from an MTV worker about an alleged 2001 incident. Brand, 51, denies all seven charges (3 rapes, 3 sexual assaults, 1 indecent assault, 1999–2009); it is still unclear whether he will testify. No verdict — the trial is expected to run up to 10 weeks. Pollar trial tracker

Apple and Shyamalan keep their 'Servant' copyright win — The 9th US Circuit Court of Appeals on Thursday upheld Apple and director M. Night Shyamalan's jury win against filmmaker Francesca Gregorini, who argued their Apple TV+ show "Servant" copied her 2013 movie "The Truth About Emanuel." The court rejected her challenges to the jury's finding that the makers lacked access to her film before making the show. Gregorini sued in 2020; the case was revived in 2022 after a judge dismissed it prematurely. Why it matters: a definitive end to a long-running streaming-era copyright fight, and a precedent on how access is proved. Reuters
