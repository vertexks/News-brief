1. Ukraine/Russia + Poland

Witkoff met Putin in St. Petersburg for ~3 hours — and Russia released an American prisoner

US envoy Steve Witkoff carried the Oslo framework to Vladimir Putin on Wednesday morning at Constantine Palace, pushing to reopen direct US–Russia–Ukraine trilateral talks. The Kremlin said the talks lasted about three hours, with no immediate comment afterward from either side. One concrete outcome did emerge: Witkoff announced Russia released 74-year-old American prisoner Stephen Hubbard, detained for more than four years. The agenda also covered Russia helping resolve the Iran war and potential Russian fertilizer exports to the US. Moscow, meanwhile, struck a cool tone in advance — Lavrov said Russia "does not expect a miracle" and will not make "political concessions." Zelensky's line before the meeting: "Let's see what Putin has to say," and that much depends on whether Washington hears "something new from Russia today — about peace, rather than the continuation of the war." CNN · AP via Military.com · Devdiscourse

Overnight Russian strikes killed 4 and hit energy sites in four regions

Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said overnight strikes hit power facilities in the Kyiv, Volyn, Odesa and Mykolaiv regions, plus civilian enterprises in at least two others. A drone slammed into a 24-story apartment building in Kyiv, killing two; in Korotych, outside Kharkiv, strikes killed an 81-year-old woman and an 83-year-old man and injured 11, including two children. A university building and a shopping centre in central Ukraine were also hit, with injuries reported in Poltava, Lviv, Cherkasy and Chernihiv. Russia's defence ministry gave a different account: a drone-storage warehouse complex in Poltava, the Nivki substation in Kyiv, and a cargo ship carrying military cargo to Odesa. Reuters via 94.1 Duke FM · The Orbit Post

First Russian diesel tanker to the US sails Thursday — the Trump diesel deal, made concrete

Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Russia will send its first 40,000-ton diesel tanker to the US on Thursday, with more shipments to follow. That's the delivery mechanism behind the deal at the center of the last two weeks of US–Ukraine friction — and it lands the morning after Witkoff's Putin meeting. Oilprice

Starshield row sharpens: WSJ says Musk ordered the slowdown; Rubio says it's Musk's call, not the White House's

The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that Elon Musk ordered the speed slowdown on Starshield, Ukraine's military-grade Starlink service, after discussions with Trump and White House officials — one source said beforehand, two said in the days after. On Wednesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said decisions on Ukraine's Starlink access are up to SpaceX and Musk, not the White House, calling it "governed by a private commercial contract" — an apparent shift from his earlier position that no one ordered a slowdown. Defence Secretary Hegseth said Tuesday the Pentagon "hasn't pulled anything back" and issued no directives. Meanwhile the practical problem is unresolved: reporting says Ukrainian drones still cannot fly faster than 130 km/h without losing connection. Zelensky said Tuesday that US early warnings are working again; the Starshield question is separate and still open. Reuters via 94.1 Duke FM · Pollar News

Poland: quiet — no fresh developments; Krzysztof N. remains in 2-month pretrial detention.

2. Iran/US + Yemen/Saudi + Hormuz

Houthis deny any drones toward Medina on Wednesday — after the coalition said it intercepted them there

Yemen's Houthis said they launched no drones toward the holy city of Medina on Wednesday and conducted no military operations toward Saudi Arabia at all, denying the Saudi-led coalition's claim that it intercepted drones over Medina and the Eastern region. The denial matters because the Houthis had previously said Mecca and Medina airspace would be exempt from their airline warning — a Medina strike claim, if true, would cross their own red line. Within Yemen, the Houthis claimed an attack Wednesday on a military camp near Aden airport, and a government source told Reuters they also attacked Riyan airport in Mukalla. Attribution on the Medina/Eastern intercepts is contested — developing. Reuters via Devdiscourse

Coalition says it destroyed the launcher behind a Riyadh-bound ballistic missile

The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed the launch platform used to fire a Houthi ballistic missile toward Riyadh, after earlier announcing the missile itself was intercepted. No location or operational details were given. Al Arabiya via Aalimi Nation

New tanker hit off Oman overnight — fire out, crew safe, attacker unidentified

UKMTO reported that at about 0145 GMT Wednesday, an unidentified projectile hit a tanker roughly 11 nautical miles west of Buha, Oman. The fire was later put out, the tanker is at anchor, the entire crew is safe, and no environmental impact was reported. A separate Tuesday incident saw a projectile hit a tanker 30 nautical miles from Al Faw, Iraq, with no casualties and no damage. The pattern keeps widening beyond the strait itself into the Gulf of Oman. Srpske Novine (UKMTO)

Bloomberg: Iran's ship attacks are getting more frequent and more damaging — engine rooms now the target

At least 23 incidents involving ships attacked in or near Hormuz were logged in the first two weeks of October, with at least five reporting engine-room impacts, according to naval groups and security consultants. Engine-room hits cause fires and leave ships without power or propulsion for months of repairs — an echo of the 1980s Tanker War. Officials say Iran has shifted from drones to missiles, which steer toward hotter parts of a ship and carry larger payloads. The IRGC on Tuesday claimed a third tanker in days — a supertanker carrying over 2 million barrels — exploded on a mine south of the strait after switching off radar and navigation; that claim remains unconfirmed, and CENTCOM has disputed the earlier mine claims. US Energy Secretary Wright said Sunday shipments are at about 80% of normal — against IMF PortWatch's 4 transits on Oct 18 vs ~85/day baseline. Bloomberg via Rigzone · Malay Mail (IRGC claim)

Oil back above $110 as talks rumours fade and US stocks draw down

Brent rose 1.5% to $110.38 in early Wednesday trading (WTI $99.18) after settling Tuesday at $108.79 off a five-month high of $111.99. Tuesday's dip came on unconfirmed reports of Qatar- and Pakistan-brokered mediation — Pakistan's army chief met Iranian leaders in Tehran on Oct 20 — but Iranian outlet Nournews reported Iran saying there would be no talks until its demands are met, and no official confirmation of a Qatari delegation emerged. API data showed US crude stocks fell 4.9 million barrels last week, on top of an 11.12 million draw the week before. Araghchi reiterated the strait stays closed until Washington accepts Tehran's seven-day plan; indirect talks via Pakistan and Qatar are reported to be continuing, but nothing is confirmed. Reuters via 106.5 Jack FM

3. Tech: Apple, Nvidia, Google

Nvidia GTC Berlin keynote: OpenShell to leash AI agents, DSX POD factories in 14 days, Vera Rubin in full production

Jensen Huang's keynote Wednesday unveiled the OpenShell Reference Architecture — models think on Vera Rubin systems, agent tool-use runs on Vera CPUs, and BlueField-4 chips run Sentry software to watch agent behavior from outside the system and enforce policy in hardware — plus DSX POD, prebuilt container AI factories Nvidia says cut onsite deployment from six months to 14 days, with deliveries worldwide in early 2027. Nvidia says Vera Rubin is in full production, its European commitments now approach $10 billion, and its DSX designs can cut cooling water use by up to 100%. The Register's caveat, noted in coverage: OpenShell requires Nvidia hardware, still has open security issues on GitHub, and collects telemetry by default unless users opt out. Separately, Nvidia announced an Isaac robotics expansion — a reference humanoid (Agile ONE SN, 1.40m, 31 degrees of freedom, Jetson Thor onboard), Isaac OS runtime and Isaac Lab 3.0 — and supply-chain capacity expansion including 2.5x substrate capacity over three years. Tesla News Wire · GlobeNewswire via Morningstar (Isaac) · GlobeNewswire via Morningstar (supply chain)

iPhone Duo reviews are out: "coolest phone in years" — but apps aren't ready for it

First reviews landed Wednesday ahead of Friday's Oct 23 launch: CNN's verdict is that the $2,000 foldable is genuinely fun — giant inner screen (near iPad mini size), doubles as bedside clock, mini monitor and tripod camera — but many apps still aren't built for the big screen, and developers haven't updated them yet. Apple also capped US pre-orders at two units per customer, after reports that launch-day walk-in stock will be effectively nil. CNN · Gadgets360

Google rolls Gemini Personal Intelligence out to Android Auto today

Gemini in the car can now pull from Gmail, Calendar, Photos, Maps, YouTube and Search with the user's permission — ask for a flight gate and on-time status in one question, navigate to a doctor's appointment without the address, or find a car wash from a photo taken last week. It's opt-in (auto-on if already enabled in the Gemini app), rolling out on Android Auto starting today; cars with Google built-in get it in the coming months. Excluded: Workspace accounts, and the EEA, Switzerland, the UK and Nigeria. FoneArena

Nvidia DeepStream 9.2: more of it open source, smarter frames for vision-language models

DeepStream 9.2 is live, and more of the SDK is now open source — the monorepo includes 12 GStreamer elements and 30+ libraries as source, including codecs and Re-ID vision/text embedding plugins. New: intelligent frame selection (send only the most relevant frames to VLMs), an active-learning pipeline with runtime model switching, and GB300 support plus JetPack 7.2.1 for Jetson Thor/Orin. 5. Cybersecurity

CISA's Tuesday KEV additions, consolidated: the full Plex2Shell chain plus Siemens and SiteGuard — deadlines Oct 23 and Nov 3

CISA added all four Plex2Shell flaws to KEV on Tuesday (CVE-2026-96652/96654/96655/96656), chained by a remote attacker for unauthenticated RCE on Plex Media Server; fixed in 1.43.3.10861 (Aug 3) — victims are unpatched installs, with miners and proxy malware already deployed in the wild. Also added: Siemens Software Center CVE-2026-110936 (DLL sideloading, ROMULUS ClickFix) and SiteGuard Server Edition CVE-2026-106931 (OS command injection to root/SYSTEM). Federal deadlines: Oct 23 for SiteGuard and two Plex flaws, Nov 3 for the rest. (Individual additions were in the night brief; this is the consolidated chain and deadline picture.) The Hacker News

SafePay detail: how the 600-victim ransomware operation gets in

The joint FBI/CISA/USSS/Germany/UK advisory (flagged in the night brief) puts SafePay's initial access at credential exposure, brute-force, phished accounts, and VPN abuse — notably Mullvad VPN IP addresses — plus automated exploitation of CVEs in edge and internet-exposed devices. Once in: double extortion, plus deleting shadow copies and resetting NAS backup devices to break recovery. Zot News

Overnight ransomware-leak CLAIMS (unverified tracker reports)

Emperador claims Bruin Group LLC (US telecom expense management firm) — $1M BTC/XMR demand, claims full ticket repository including client wire instructions, W-9s and call traces; Scarlettgroup claims nbcuniversal.com; N0n and Clop listed two more unnamed victims. All are leak-site claims only — no victim confirmation. HookPhish (Bruin) · HookPhish (NBCUniversal)
