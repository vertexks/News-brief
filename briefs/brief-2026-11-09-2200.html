Night brief — Monday, November 9, 2026

Houthis hit Riyadh again on Monday night — missiles intercepted, oil pipeline struck

The Houthis fired more missiles at Saudi Arabia on Monday night, hours after the kingdom said its key East-West oil pipeline had been struck — the latest round in a campaign that has now hit Riyadh's financial district, its main airport, Aramco's headquarters and now a pipeline in the space of a few weeks.

The Saudi-led coalition said on Tuesday morning that its air defences intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile and a cruise missile fired at Riyadh, and a second ballistic missile aimed at the country's southern region. Saudi Civil Defence had issued early-warning alerts for the capital and for the southwestern cities of Abha and Khamis Mushait before the interceptions, then said the danger had passed. Multiple explosions were heard in Riyadh, according to AFP, and residents received text-message alerts telling them to take shelter. There was no immediate comment from the Houthis on Monday night's launches, and no casualties were reported.

The pipeline strike came earlier on Monday. Saudi Arabia said its East-West oil pipeline was struck in the Medina region, causing a limited fire that was later extinguished. The attack caused no casualties and did not affect pipeline operations, the state news agency SPA said. The East-West line is one of the kingdom's most important pieces of energy infrastructure — it moves crude from the Gulf coast to the Red Sea, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz — so even a limited strike on it is a deliberate escalation in targeting. It followed Sunday's attack on Riyadh, when falling debris from an attempted missile strike blew out windows across several floors of the Aramco Tower in the King Abdullah Financial District. Early on Tuesday, Riyadh's deputy governor, Prince Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Abdulaziz, inspected the damaged Aramco building and said the attack showed the Houthis were targeting civilian facilities.

The two sides' accounts of the damage keep diverging. Saudi officials have described the Aramco Tower damage as limited and caused by falling debris, while BBC Verify's footage showed windows blown out across several floors — more than "minor damage" — and the Houthis have claimed direct hits on the tower, the airport, Aramco storage facilities and the eastern city of Abqaiq. The Houthis say they are hitting Saudi airports, ports and oil facilities in support of Iran's war with the US and Israel; Riyadh says it is responding with airstrikes on Houthi targets in Yemen, including launchers in Saada and Sanaa. Neither side's claims about what was hit and what was intercepted can be fully verified from the ground, and the coalition has not said whether Monday night's missiles were aimed at the airport, the financial district or elsewhere in the capital.

For ordinary people in Riyadh, the pattern is now familiar and wearing: sirens and shelter texts, explosions overhead, then an all-clear — twice in two days, and the fourth attack on the capital's infrastructure in about a week. King Khalid International Airport has been closed or disrupted for most of the past month, and the pipeline strike is a reminder that the campaign is widening beyond airports and office towers toward the energy system itself. The Houthis have stepped up their strikes since advancing toward the Bab el-Mandeb Strait in September, and there is no sign either side is easing off — the coalition keeps destroying launchers, and the Houthis keep launching.

In short: the Houthis fired three more missiles at Saudi Arabia on Monday night — two at Riyadh, one at the south — all intercepted according to the coalition, and the East-West oil pipeline was struck earlier in the day with a limited fire and no disruption to operations. No casualties were reported, the Houthis have not commented on the latest launches, and the pattern of near-daily attacks on Saudi cities and energy infrastructure continues.

Sources: Reuters via SRN News · Trinidad Tribune (AFP) · SRN News (Reuters)

Iran — US reportedly offered a phased deal; Tehran's own officials split on talks

Two new wrinkles in the Iran standoff on Monday. The Emirati outlet Erem News, citing diplomatic sources familiar with the mediated talks, reported that the United States has proposed a phased plan: a partial lifting of its naval blockade of Iran first, then nuclear talks, with a gradual lifting of sanctions and the release of frozen Iranian funds to follow. Iran has not responded to the offer, according to the report, and wants written commitments and tangible steps from Washington first. Neither Washington nor Tehran has confirmed the report — treat it as unconfirmed for now. It is, however, the first sign of a concrete American sequencing offer after weeks of deadlock over whether nuclear talks or sanctions relief comes first.

Tehran, meanwhile, is speaking with two voices. President Pezeshkian said Monday that Supreme Leader Khamenei had ordered officials to end the state of "neither war nor peace" and that Iran would negotiate with dignity. But the Supreme National Security Council's secretary, Mohsen Rezaei, wrote on X the same day rejecting reports of talks as "fake news" and telling Washington to "surrender to the seven conditions Iran has repeatedly set out." Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei defended the two-day proposal at his weekly press conference, asking whether it was unreasonable for Iran to demand sanctions and the blockade be lifted before negotiations start. President Trump, after meeting Qatar's prime minister at the White House, said there was progress toward a deal and described the war as militarily near its end — but in a separate Reuters phone interview he kept the strike option open and said he was content with the naval blockade. A US answer to Tehran is still expected by Tuesday, with Qatari officials due in Tehran the same day.

Sources: Erem News via blog report · Life News Agency · NewsPlainly (AFP/AP, Reuters)

Ukraine — ISW: Russia claims a village, neither side advanced, and Kyiv's bridges are the target

The Institute for the Study of War's November 9 assessment adds a few details to Monday's picture without changing it. Russia's defence ministry claimed the seizure of Vasylkove, northwest of Hulyaipole — its first claim of taking a settlement since October 28 — but ISW records no confirmed advance by either side on November 9. The assessment also notes Russia is leaning into highly manoeuvrable quasi-cruise missiles and modifying anti-aircraft and ballistic missiles to expand ballistic-trajectory strikes, with a Russian military outlet claiming converted RM-48U missiles launched from S-300/S-400 systems were used against Kyiv's Northern and Southern Bridges on November 7 and October 27. The overnight picture stands as reported earlier: 317 drones including 105 jet-powered ones, more than 30 ballistic missiles, four substations hit, and over 600,000 without power in Dnipropetrovsk. No new overnight tally for November 9–10 had been reported by reliable outlets by 10 PM CT. US-brokered talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday.

Sources: ISW · AP via Military.com

Israel — Eisenkot and Lieberman find common ground; Knesset sworn in Tuesday

Gadi Eisenkot's meeting with Avigdor Lieberman on Monday produced the first concrete agreements of the coalition talks: both men described the talks as positive, they agreed the next Knesset Speaker will come from Eisenkot's Yashar party, and Lieberman will get the first opportunity to negotiate with the ultra-Orthodox parties. Lieberman has ruled out supporting a government that depends on Arab parties, and Eisenkot's bloc holds 48 of 120 seats against the 61 needed. The new Knesset is sworn in on Tuesday, with 54 new members, and there are concerns the proceedings could be disrupted as Netanyahu continues to promote claims that the election was stolen — claims that, outside of Channel 14, do not appear to be gaining traction. Eisenkot has 28 days to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension.

Sources: Marc Schulman (Substack) · Particle

Khor Mor — still shut, no restart date

Production at the Khor Mor gas field in Iraqi Kurdistan remained suspended on Monday while operator Dana Gas assesses damage from Sunday night's drone strike, which cut 2,500–2,800 MW — roughly 70–80% of the region's power. Baghdad has ordered household gas supplies to the region until the field restarts, and a joint investigation has been ordered. No group has claimed responsibility, and no restart date has been given.

Sources: The Insight International · Silent Dossier

Tech

Google takes over Crusoe's troubled Wyoming data centre — $17 billion planned

Google has taken over an unfinished AI data-centre project in southeastern Wyoming from Crusoe, the Denver-based developer that had failed to land Google as a customer for the site. Crusoe confirmed the sale on Friday, November 6, a day after Google announced plans to invest $17 billion to build its first data-centre campus in the state. Google did not disclose what it paid for the site — the $17 billion is its planned investment in the campus, not a purchase price — and it has not committed to the original 10-gigawatt scale Crusoe had planned with Blackstone-backed Tallgrass. Google says it will cover 100% of the project's energy needs and infrastructure costs, sourcing power from Bloom Energy natural-gas fuel cells with carbon capture. Crusoe is still developing another data centre for Google near Amarillo, Texas. (TechCrunch · Inc. · Telecompaper)

Nvidia — Huang scandal becomes a White House problem, lawmakers demand details

The scandal around Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset has moved beyond the company's board investigation and into Washington. TechCrunch reported Monday evening that it is now a concern for the White House, and lawmakers from both parties are demanding details from Nvidia. The board's independent investigation continues, House chairs want an answer by Wednesday, November 11, on a closed-door briefing November 18, and Nvidia reports Q3 earnings November 17. Nvidia shares fell 4.89% in premarket trading Monday. (TechCrunch via SaveDelete · TokenPost)

Cyber

Who Gives A Crap — customer data exposed through a marketing supplier

Toilet-paper brand Who Gives A Crap told customers over the weekend that their personal information was accessed without authorisation in a breach at one of its US-based marketing software providers. The access began on October 30, was detected on October 31 and secured by November 1, but the company was not told until November 6 and began notifying customers on November 7. Affected customers are in Australia, the UK and the US; the data includes names, email and postal addresses, phone numbers and total spending with the brand. Payment details, order details and passwords were not involved, and customer accounts were not directly accessed. The company has not said how many customers were affected, has not named the supplier, and does not yet know whether the data was copied. The main risk is phishing and scam messages using real customer details. (The Register · Company notice)

"SSHole" exploitation count ticks up

GreyNoise now records 17 confirmed exploitation chains for the "SSHole" OpenSSH flaw (CVE-2026-123158), up from 16, including attempts to read password hashes and SSH keys, probe cloud metadata and deploy a Python implant. Alpine's advisory covers releases 3.16 through 3.24 and edge; the fix is OpenSSH 10.6p2 or a distribution patch. CISA's KEV deadline for the four flaws added Monday is November 12, and no new KEV additions were reported after those four. (Nerds.xyz · CISA via CTXSec)

Courts

Lindsay Clancy can be retried for murder, judge rules

A Massachusetts judge ruled on Monday that Lindsay Clancy, the mother of three who killed her children in Duxbury in January 2023, can be tried again for murder. Judge William F. Sullivan found that the September 4 mistrial — declared after a holdout juror blocked a unanimous verdict — was justified by manifest necessity, so a retrial does not violate the double-jeopardy clause. Clancy's lawyers had argued she could not be tried again. A hearing on November 16 will discuss timing; the judge has suggested the second trial could be held in March. (Reuters via Duke FM · USA Today)

Farwell jury deadlocked, sent back to deliberate Tuesday

The jury in former Stoughton police detective Matthew Farwell's federal murder trial — accused of killing Sandra Birchmore, 23 and pregnant, in 2021 and staging it as a suicide — told the judge on Monday it was deadlocked and did not expect that to change. Jurors had deliberated about 7½ hours on Monday on top of around three hours on Friday. Judge Denise Casper excused the jury at 4:30 p.m. and said she was inclined to let them keep deliberating on Tuesday without further instructions, given the scope of the evidence. (Enterprise News)

No fresh developments in the Netanyahu trial or the Murdaugh retrial (next hearing November 13). The FTC v. Amazon Prime jury trial continues in Seattle; no fresh developments Monday evening beyond the summary-judgment motions reported earlier.
