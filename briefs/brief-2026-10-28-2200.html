World escalation

Israel's count is nearly done at 53–48–19 — and Thursday's leftover ballots decide what moves, if anything

Israel's election count has moved from the evening's near-final picture to a nearly complete one, and the headline has not changed: Benjamin Netanyahu leads, nobody has a majority, and the next step belongs to the ballots not yet counted. A live tracker updated October 29 put the count at 98.17% of ballots, with the Netanyahu bloc at 53 seats, Gadi Eisenkot's Change bloc at 48, and the Arab parties at 19 — the same 53–48–19 split as the evening brief, now on a near-complete count. Double-envelope ballots — soldiers' votes and others — are counted Thursday, with definitive results expected Thursday and official results by November 4, so parties near the 3.25% threshold can still shift the margins.

Reuters' Wednesday account frames the result as a paradox that both camps are now spinning in opposite directions. Netanyahu, described by a former adviser as staging a political "resurrection," has restored his primacy even while losing his majority — and in his post-election speech he called for a "strong, right-wing national government" and a renewed joint move against Iran with the United States, calling it a "singular historical opportunity." President Donald Trump, who had urged Israelis to keep Netanyahu in power to "finish the job" in Iran, said the prime minister had done "very well" and credited his own endorsement. Eisenkot, for his part, has acknowledged Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister but says he has lost public support, has urged him to retire, and has warned security chiefs to resist political pressure. Analysts quoted by Reuters see two broad paths out: a broader unity government led by Netanyahu — potentially bringing in Eisenkot or Naftali Bennett — or another election in 2027, which several analysts said could strengthen Netanyahu further rather than change the outcome.

The other side of the story is the Arab parties, whose unprecedentedly high turnout handed them their decisive position. Reuters notes Arab Israelis — 21% of the population — have "never been in a position where they held this much power," in the words of former US negotiator Dennis Ross, and a tracker of the count reports Arab-led lists signalling openness to helping form an alternative government. The catch, Reuters stresses, is that Eisenkot's route to power likely requires direct or indirect Arab support, a prospect that could prove difficult for potential right-wing partners such as Bennett and Avigdor Lieberman — and both main camps have so far ruled out relying on them. Meanwhile the Gulf is already pricing the uncertainty: Reuters' Gulf analysis notes Brent crude at $115 a barrel on Wednesday, with Riyadh's airport disruption (86% of flights cancelled across October 8–25, per Cirium) still hanging over the region.

For ordinary Israelis, the practical consequence is a stretch of caretaker government while President Isaac Herzog consults the party groups — with the Iran confrontation, the Gaza aftermath and Netanyahu's own corruption trial all sitting with a government that has lost its old majority. For everyone else in the region, the question is whether Netanyahu's lead hardens into a government that can move on Iran after the US midterms on November 3, as the New York Post's observers now expect.

In short: the count is now 98.17% complete at 53–48–19, the Arab parties hold the balance and are signalling openness to a change government, and Thursday's double-envelope ballots plus Herzog's consultations are the next real events — not another projection.

Sources: Reuters, October 28; Live tracker, October 29; Reuters Gulf analysis, October 28; New York Post, October 28

Tech products

Alphabet's cloud doubled — and the market still sold it off 9%

Alphabet reported third-quarter results after the close on Wednesday, and the quarter split cleanly in two: an operating business that beat expectations in the cloud, and a spending bill that spooked investors. According to the earnings release Alphabet filed with the SEC, revenue for the quarter ended September 30 rose 25% to $127.6 billion, the 13th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, and operating income rose 30% to $40.6 billion. Google Cloud was the star: revenue jumped 103% to $30.7 billion, with operating income more than tripling to $11.1 billion, led by enterprise AI infrastructure and TPU system sales, according to Securities.io's read of the release. Search grew 12% to $63.4 billion, YouTube ads 9% to $11.2 billion, and CEO Sundar Pichai said the company is "now on a half-trillion dollar revenue run rate," with the Gemini app at one billion users.

The other side of the ledger is why the stock fell about 9% in after-hours trading, to around $293–294 from a $324.89 close. Capital expenditures hit $51.4 billion in a single quarter — more than double a year earlier — pushing free cash flow to negative $9.07 billion, far worse than the roughly flat figure analysts expected, according to CNBC market data carried by SourceFirstNews. Net income available to common shareholders fell 25% to $26.2 billion, or $2.12 per share, missing estimates, but much of that was an $8.7 billion paper loss on equity securities, not operations. Alphabet also sold $12.4 billion of stock, raised $28.7 billion in notes, and repurchased no shares in the quarter — a financing posture that reads as a company funding its AI build-out with other people's money while it lasts.

For ordinary users, the practical upshot is less about the share price than about where the money is going: Google's AI infrastructure spending is now running at a pace that outstrips the cash the business generates, for a second straight quarter. The bull case is that Cloud's doubling proves the spending is already paying off in enterprise AI revenue. The bear case is that a $51 billion quarterly capex run rate, if sustained, is a bet that AI demand keeps doubling too — and the market, at least overnight, priced the risk rather than the growth.

In short: Cloud doubled and operating income rose 30%, but a $51.4 billion capex quarter drove free cash flow to negative $9.1 billion and the stock down ~9% after hours. Watch Thursday's analyst calls and whether the sell-off holds into the regular session.

Sources: Securities.io, October 28; SourceFirstNews/CNBC, October 28; TickerRoast, October 28

Open source

  • MiniMax released the MCode Stack as open source (October 28). 17 modular agent skills (six adapted from Google Engineering Practices) plus 5 engineering checklists for automated coding workflows, MIT-licensed, installable into MiniMax Code or Claude Code via SKILL.md folders or a marketplace. Requires Node.js 22.19+. Why it matters: standardized engineering practices dropped straight into agent workflows, from a company whose skills are already widely copied. Source: Wisevoter, October 28
  • Cognitum One unveiled MetaHarness at GitHub Universe 2026. An open-source "factory" for branded, governed AI agent harnesses: start from a repo or blank page and it generates a harness with its own CLI, MCP server, scoped memory, governance policy and witness-signed releases, with swappable models. From the same creator behind Ruflo (74k stars). Vendor announcement, so treat the capability claims as untested until developers try it. Source: Access Newswire, October 28
  • SolidCraft trending on GitHub. An open-source parametric 3D CAD app in pure Rust (desktop, web, automation via MCP), +185 stars in 24h, ~11.2k stars total, 20 days old. Worth a look if you want a self-hosted CAD alternative. Source: GitDiscover, October 28

Cybersecurity

  • Node2Shell: Brotli decoder flaw added to CISA's exploited list, deadline November 10. CISA added CVE-2026-111497 (Google Brotli decoder, CVSS 9.2) to KEV on Tuesday after honeypot hits and live exploit traffic observed by ELLIO and Previdian, with Imperva demonstrating RCE in a vulnerable Node.js image. An unauthenticated attacker can corrupt decoder memory via a crafted Brotli stream, potentially reaching remote code execution. Node.js, PHP, Bun, Envoy, Istio, .NET and Python have all issued patches or advisories — update binaries, rebuild container images and restart services; there is no single runtime workaround. Sources: Particle News, October 28; ReconBee, October 28
  • Adobe Commerce PolyShell also added to KEV, deadline November 10. CVE-2026-48356 (CVSS 9.3, unrestricted file upload) has been used since at least July 2025 to breach hundreds of online stores, per Sansec, injecting a CMS loader that fetches an attacker-controlled payload. If you run Adobe Commerce/Magento, patch and hunt for the loader — a storefront that looks fine can already be compromised. Source: ReconBee, October 28
