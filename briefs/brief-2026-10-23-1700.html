1. Ukraine/Russia + Poland

A local ceasefire, a talks date, and a prisoner swap — while the Starlink fight stays unresolved

Friday produced the most concrete diplomatic movement in weeks, and it came in three pieces at once. According to Reuters, Ukraine and Russia said on Friday they had agreed on a ceasefire in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky, brokered through US First Lady Melania Trump, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said peace negotiators would meet next month for new talks. The two sides said the Oleshky ceasefire takes effect on October 27, allowing aid deliveries and civilian evacuations through humanitarian corridors. The White House said both nations agreed to temporarily stop all military action in Oleshky to allow aid in and prepare a civilian evacuation through a designated safe passageway, and Russian chief of staff Valery Gerasimov said Russian troops would halt fire from 4 a.m. local time on October 27. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets told Reuters the operation would involve demining roads to the town, delivering food and evacuating civilians.

The talks piece is now dated, if still soft. Zelenskyy said negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States had agreed to technical-level meetings in the first half of November in Abu Dhabi, and that he hoped a meeting of the countries' leaders would follow. A US official later told Reuters the three countries may hold their next talks in a couple of weeks in Abu Dhabi or Switzerland. Zelenskyy, speaking to the Hudson Institute via video link, said any peace agreement would need strong security guarantees for Ukraine and funding for 10 years to maintain the Ukrainian army, and stressed that Europe should also be involved. He also cautioned that much would depend on Russia, noting that a similar meeting expected this month had not materialised — a reminder that a talks date is not a talks outcome.

The arguments underneath have not moved. Zelenskyy is still holding the freeze-along-current-lines offer he made earlier in the week, while Moscow has only committed to talks after November 10, with the Donbas question — the gap between those two positions — untouched by Friday's announcements. And the third piece of Friday's news, a prisoner swap of 270 servicemen each, is the first since August, according to the Russian Defence Ministry; Zelenskyy called it a "very important result." Ukraine's acting prosecutor said on Telegram that more than 19,000 Ukrainian civilians, including 762 children, have been killed since Russia's full-scale invasion began, with 2,960 civilian deaths confirmed in 2026 alone.

The Starlink dispute, meanwhile, is now a direct contradiction rather than a misunderstanding. According to CNN, Ukrainian officials said on Friday morning that speed restrictions on Starlink and Starshield remain in place despite President Donald Trump's claim on Thursday that Elon Musk had restored full access. Trump told reporters on Friday that he had not spoken to Musk about the restrictions but would do so if required: "If there is a problem, I'll get it straightened out." Zelenskyy, speaking on Friday afternoon Kyiv time, said plainly: "The speed limits on Starlink and Starshield are still there, and the speed has not been restored." A Ukrainian Defence Ministry source told CNN there is "currently no confirmation that the restrictions have been lifted" and signal loss is still observed for ordnance travelling above 100 mph; one officer said connections drop for 10–15 seconds and "we lost a lot of assets, much more than usual." Musk has denied taking unilateral action, saying limits "are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS." Trump, pressed on who ordered the slowdown, said: "I have no idea, but I wouldn't tell you if I did." Zelenskyy also said Friday that delivery of already-agreed Patriot interceptors "is still delayed."

For ordinary Ukrainians, Friday's fighting did not pause for the diplomacy. According to CNN's tally of local officials, at least nine civilians were killed and 76 injured in the last day; Russia launched 176 attack drones on Thursday night and overnight; energy attacks in six regions left more than 500,000 people without power on Friday; and Sumy region saw strikes on civilian infrastructure and residential areas. In Russia, the defence ministry said it intercepted 443 Ukrainian drones overnight, with three civilians injured by falling debris in Nizhny Novgorod. Oleshky itself shows the human stakes of the local deal: the White House put its remaining population at 1,700–2,000, down from 24,000 before the war, mostly elderly residents needing medicine, food and clean water — and Melania Trump, who has been pressing the return of Ukrainian children seized by Russia, called it "a front-line war city on the brink of disaster." Pope Leo spoke to both Zelenskyy and Putin about the town in the past week, per Reuters.

In short: Friday gave the war a dated talks window, a local humanitarian ceasefire and a prisoner swap — real movement, but all of it narrow, and none of it touching the Donbas disagreement or the Starlink and interceptor disputes that are shaping the fighting right now. Watch for whether the Oleshky ceasefire holds from October 27, for a confirmed Abu Dhabi venue and date, and for any actual change in Starlink speeds or interceptor deliveries. 2. Iran/US + Yemen/Saudi + Hormuz

No new verified strike on Friday — but the coalition reports more interceptions, and Egypt's role sharpens

Friday brought no newly verified Houthi hit on Saudi Arabia beyond what the morning brief already covered (the overnight Riyadh/Najran interceptions and the claimed four operations of the past 48 hours). The coalition did report further interceptions during Friday: two drones headed for the southern region were intercepted and destroyed, per the coalition spokesman via the Saudi Press Agency, and a separate coalition statement summarised nine drones and a ballistic missile intercepted in the preceding hours. No casualties or damage were specified for these interceptions, and the claims remain coalition-side only.

Two context items moved Friday. First, Egypt's move to defend Saudi Arabia got fuller detail in an OilPrice analysis published Friday morning: Egypt's offer is defensive, driven by a $5.3 billion Saudi deposit at Egypt's central bank falling due this month and by Suez Canal revenues hit by Red Sea shipping attacks, plus the loss of Israeli gas exports since the war began, which forced Cairo to buy replacement LNG. Egypt's parliament vote on authorising military support is still set for October 26, as reported this morning. Second, one Gulf roundup published Friday reported that Riyadh airport has reopened, a week after the serious Houthi strikes, but with flights remaining limited — which conflicts with Etihad's note this morning that Riyadh remained closed while Dammam had reopened. Treat the reopening as unconfirmed until an airline or airport notice settles it.

Quiet: No independent confirmation of Iran's seven-tanker Hormuz claim from Thursday, and no CENTCOM word on the claimed Sirik strike. The US-Israel post-midterm strike plan remains as reported Thursday night, contested inside Israel by its own army chief (see morning brief).

3. Tech: Apple, Nvidia, Google

Apple briefly retakes the world's most valuable company crown — on the Duo's launch day

Apple overtook Nvidia as the world's most valuable company on Friday, riding the iPhone Duo's launch day to a record stock price. According to 9to5Mac, Apple traded above $351 per share, setting a new intraday record, with a market cap around $5.12 trillion. Seeking Alpha put the early-Friday figure at $349.55 and about $5.10 trillion, against Nvidia at about $5.06 trillion. Multiple outlets note the crown changed hands again during the same session — Nvidia has since moved back in front, per Business Today — and that the two companies sit within 1% of each other, so a fraction of a percent swings the ranking.

Caption: An Apple logo over banknotes, the lead image on 9to5Mac's report of Apple retaking the top market-cap spot. It matters because the ranking flip is a market story, not a product one: it shows investors rotating toward Apple while Nvidia slides.

The arguments about what the flip means are already split. The rotation case, put by market coverage on Friday, is that Apple is up about 4% since October 6 while Nvidia has fallen about 11% from its October 6 record close, with the Nasdaq down 2.6% on Thursday in a broad AI-stock selloff — investors are questioning how much AI infrastructure spending customers can actually finance, and Apple, which is not spending heavily on AI data centres, benefits by contrast. The sceptical case is that this is the fifth time Apple has grabbed the crown from Nvidia, and it has rarely stuck: since the start of 2024 the top spot has changed hands roughly two dozen times, and only once, during the DeepSeek scare in January 2025, did Apple hold it into the spring.

For ordinary buyers, the Duo story from this morning stands: $1,999 and up, January waits for new online orders, and a Saturday 10 AM ET restock check in the Apple Store app. One new detail Friday: Apple's marketing chief Greg Joswiak told CNBC pre-orders were "off the charts" and pushed back on production-problem reports, saying Apple is in a normal production ramp; Morgan Stanley's supply-chain checks point to about 6.5 million Duo units produced in the second half of 2026 — a limited volume next to conventional premium iPhones.

In short: the crown is a snapshot of a market rotation, not a verdict on either company, and it will likely flip again before the weekend is out. Watch for whether Apple holds the lead at a weekly close, and for the Saturday restock check.

The catch: Nvidia's own marketplace lists the 128GB version at $6,950, a $1,951 gap for double the memory, and the headline feature is two units pooling into 128GB. Google Cloud + Nvidia: Vera Rubin A5X instances generally available in November — Catch-up, announced October 21 at GTC Berlin and not covered in the morning brief: Google Cloud's A5X instances, built on Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with ConnectX-9 SuperNICs and Google's liquid cooling, reach general availability next month, with expansion to Europe planned. Google plans to scale Vera Rubin infrastructure to as many as 960,000 GPUs across multiple sites — a stated plan, not installed capacity. First customer named: Ineffable Intelligence, founded by AlphaGo creator David Silver, using it for large-scale reinforcement learning. Why it matters: this is the first cloud-scale rental route to Nvidia's newest architecture, and the November rollout is the near-term test of whether AI demand converts into deployments. 4. Open source

Hi3D V1.0 Goes Open Source: The First Production-Validated Open-Source AI 3D Model — Hi3D (formerly Sparc3D) open-sourced its V1.0 AI 3D model on October 23, with source code and model weights available and free generation on its website. The training set grew from about 500,000 to 2 million curated 3D assets, geometry reconstruction now reaches an effective 1536³ sparse-grid resolution, and the release adds AI texturing, split-for-3D-printing with connectors, multicolor print conversion, and a "Hi3D Agent" for generating, editing and optimising 3D content — plus a 50%+ cut in credit consumption. Why it matters: this is a production-tested 3D model, not a demo, aimed at creators who need printable, usable geometry rather than pretty renders. PR Newswire

OpenSSL 4.1.0 released October 23 — The new release adds DTLS 1.3 support, RFC 8701 GREASE, DTLS support in the SSL listener API, an IKEv2 KDF, initial Elbrus2000 architecture support, and performance work on ML-DSA/ML-KEM and AES-CBC on x86_64 with AVX-512/VAES. Why it matters: DTLS 1.3 is the headline for anyone running UDP-based TLS (VPNs, WebRTC, IoT), and OpenSSL upgrades touch nearly everything. Details via a Hacker News-sourced daily digest; check the release notes before upgrading production. Marcus News digest

GitHub Apps enterprise installs are generally available (October 23) — GitHub made enterprise installations for GitHub Apps GA, adding app permissions for enterprise policies, rulesets, two-factor requirements, verified domains, IP allowlists and access restrictions. Why it matters: enterprise admin automation has been one of the last places where classic personal access tokens were hard to avoid — now the token can belong to an app instead of a person. Metarix

OpenBB goes fully open source — The OpenBB product suite (Workspace, Open Data Platform, Copilot, Excel Add-in) is being released under a permissive open-source licence, stewarded by the OpenBQ non-profit with about a third of the OpenBB team, and the Workspace codebase by FINOS under the Linux Foundation. Why it matters: five years of a Bloomberg-terminal-style investment research platform is now a foundation anyone can self-host — though, as one builder noted Friday, there is no hosted Workspace to sign up for; you have to build and host the image yourself. 5. Cybersecurity

PwC confirms cybersecurity incident involving employee learning records — PwC confirmed on Friday that it was aware of a cybersecurity incident involving a third-party vendor used to host company employee learning records, the day after the extortion group Everest claimed to have stolen 590 gigabytes of company data. A PwC spokesperson said the affected site "does not house client data, sensitive personal data or financial information," that the company acted promptly to secure the environment, and that the incident "does not impact our network or any of our operations." Everest's claim, posted October 22 with a five-day countdown timer, describes "regional HR and learning exports, integration documentation, software components and PwC e-learning courses," including names, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses and employment history. The scope is still Everest's claim versus PwC's narrower description — watch whether PwC confirms any of the claimed data types. Reuters

Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator flaw added to CISA KEV — federal deadline Monday, October 26 — CISA added CVE-2026-114625, a SQL injection flaw in on-prem Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (VCO), to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 23, with a due date of October 26 and forensic triage required. Any authenticated VCO user, down to the lowest read-only tenant role, can gain "superuser operator" privileges; Arista says it is actively exploited — the third exploited VCO zero-day in four months, per SecurityWeek via the threat brief. Fixed in VCO 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10 and 6.4.2.12; hosted and Dedicated VCO were already patched. Arista-listed indicators: 74.82.197.86 and 38.54.50.151. If you run on-prem VCO, upgrade and check for prior compromise — an orchestrator under attacker control can push configuration to every Edge it manages. CISA via IT Security News · Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, Oct 23

Quest Diagnostics confirms patient data compromised in Quanum lab portal breach — Quest Diagnostics, one of the largest US lab-testing companies, confirmed that patient records were exposed after unauthorised access to its Quanum Lab Services Manager (LSM), the portal doctors use to order tests and view results. Quest learned of the access on Wednesday, October 21, disclosed it in an SEC filing Thursday, and says protected health information was taken, including "personal information and patient records" — but has not said how many patients are affected, what specific data was taken, when the access began, or whether a ransom was demanded. No group had claimed the attack as of Thursday afternoon, and Quest says the incident is unrelated to this week's PHP vulnerabilities. It paused LSM access and, as a precaution, other tools including Essential Insights and LabHQ. This is Quest's second major breach since 2019, when a third-party billing hack exposed 11.9 million records. TechCrunch · BleepingComputer

PHP2Shell, day three: FBI says take unpatched systems offline — The FBI's IC3 public service announcement on October 23 repeats that CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 let attackers run code in the PHP engine itself, and goes further than most vendor advice: if you cannot update right away, take the system offline until it is fixed. Trackers count more than two dozen public proof-of-concept repositories, and broad exploitation has run since October 21. The patch versions are unchanged: 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35. Separately, PHP warned October 23 of a new potential RCE, CVE-2026-115560, in an unusual session-serialisation configuration (php_binary/php_serialize handler switched to php at runtime with Session Upload Progress enabled) — no reported exploits, and PHP considers the configuration uncommon, so this is a check-your-config item, not a second emergency. Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, Oct 23 · LinkedIn / Tarek ElFarmawy on CVE-2026-115560

Strands Agents SDK flaw exposes conversation history across sessions — CVE-2026-115419, disclosed October 23, is a failure to isolate conversation state between callers in the Strands Agents SDK's A2A server (multiagent.a2a module), letting an authenticated remote user access other callers' private conversation history and influence model behaviour in multi-tenant environments. Why it matters: session isolation is the whole trust model for multi-tenant AI agents, and this one fails at it. Fixed in strands-agents 1.43.0 and @strands-agents/sdk 1.5.0. Wisevoter
