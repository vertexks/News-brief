World escalation

Ukraine hits a Russian refinery again — ending the US-pressed pause — as Oleshky evacuations finally begin

Ukraine struck a Russian oil refinery before dawn on Friday for the first time since October 15, breaking a pause that the Wall Street Journal reports was held under sustained pressure from President Trump ahead of next Tuesday's US midterm elections — while, in the war's starkest contrast, residents of the Russian-held town of Oleshky began boarding evacuation buses on Friday under the local ceasefire both sides are still observing.

Fire Point, the Ukrainian company that builds the FP-1 drone, said its drones scored several hits across Rosneft's Novokuibyshevsk refinery in the Samara region, including on a unit that hydrotreats diesel fuel. Footage posted by the monitoring channel Exilenova+ showed at least 11 separate fires on the site, and European Copernicus Sentinel satellite imagery published by Reuters showed smoke drifting from the refinery. Samara's governor, Vyacheslav Fedorishchev, confirmed only that air defences and mobile fire groups were working against a drone attack on the region, and flights at Samara's airport were delayed. The plant sits on the Volga about 1,000 km from Ukraine's border, can process about 8.3 million tonnes of crude a year, and makes jet fuel for the Russian Aerospace Forces — the air arm that drops glide bombs on Ukrainian cities, according to Reuters reporting cited by Yac News.

The Journal's account of the pause is the other half of the story. It reports that Ukrainian long-range units were told in mid-October that the refinery campaign was effectively on hold once missions already under way were complete, with the pause expected to last until after the November 3 midterms, possibly deeper into November. At a meeting in Oslo on October 20, Trump's envoy Jared Kushner pressed President Volodymyr Zelenskyy for a 30-day pause as a way to show gratitude for US mediation, one US official told the Journal, describing the conversation as "very tough." European allies, the Journal reports, advised Zelenskyy to wait until after the vote to ease tension with Trump. Zelenskyy himself told reporters on October 27 that units had no order against hitting refineries and had simply tried "to be a little more careful during these days." Moscow's response on Friday was blunt: Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov told Russian state television that Trump had been insistent about refinery strikes and that the Ukrainian government ignored him. "Once again they disobeyed," Ushakov said.

What each side says it wants in return has not changed, and that is why the pause broke. Zelenskyy told Trump, the Journal reports, that Ukraine would need something in return for halting refinery strikes: first choice a mutual energy ceasefire under which Russia would stop hitting Ukrainian power plants, alternatives Patriot missiles to protect the grid or permission to use Starlink inside Russia to destroy missile launchers. Ukraine received 24 Patriot interceptors from the US over 90 days against a need of about 350 a month, Zelenskyy said on October 26. Throughout the pause, Russian forces kept hitting Ukrainian power plants and cities — on Thursday a Russian jet-powered drone killed 15 people on a crowded platform at Dnipro's railway station — and the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry told the UN General Assembly on October 26 that Russian attacks on Ukraine's energy system followed a state policy of depriving civilians of electricity and amounted to war crimes and crimes against humanity. Russia, for its part, still hopes Trump can pressure Kyiv into a deal meeting many of Moscow's conditions, including full Russian control of the Donbas and a bar on Ukraine joining NATO, while Kyiv wants US intelligence-sharing to continue and European-bought US hardware, including Patriot interceptors, to keep flowing.

For ordinary people, the two Friday developments pull in opposite directions. A refinery that fuels Russian warplanes is a military target with a civilian shadow: the same campaign has already cut Russian diesel output by about 30 percent by International Energy Agency estimates, feeding the fuel-price politics Trump cited when he announced a Russian diesel supply deal on October 9. In Oleshky, the human side is immediate and visible. Reuters reports that groups of residents began boarding evacuation buses on Friday — some heading to Russia, others to Ukraine — under the ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump with Pope Leo's involvement. Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi told parliament that residents headed for Ukraine would travel through Russia to Belarus before entering the Kyiv and Chernihiv regions, though he later retracted an early figure of 527 people planning to leave, saying no exact figures exist yet and the operation is ongoing. Newsmax's correspondent on the ground reported 750 people preregistered for evacuation from a town that had about 24,000 residents before the war and roughly 2,000 left. The ceasefire runs only until Sunday evening, and how many people actually get out before it expires is still unknown.

In short: the refinery pause lasted about two weeks, broke four days before the US midterms, and broke because Kyiv says it got no mutual energy ceasefire or air defences in return — while in Oleshky, the war's rarest thing, a ceasefire both sides are keeping, is now moving real people out of a town that has waited years for it. Watch whether Washington reacts to the Novokuibyshevsk strike, and whether the Oleshky buses keep rolling through the weekend.

Sources: Yac News, drawing on the Wall Street Journal and Reuters, SFG Media, Reuters via Devdiscourse, Newsmax

Iran claims three more tankers hit mines in Hormuz — unverified — as a Mecca drone claim is denied and Qatari mediators return to Tehran

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that three oil tankers, including a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of oil, exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz — claims the US military disputes and the Associated Press could not verify — on the same day the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi drone near Mecca, a claim the Houthis flatly denied, and Qatari mediators returned to Tehran for talks.

The Guard's account, carried by Iranian state media, is that the tankers were using what Tehran calls unauthorised routes through the strait, had switched off their navigation systems, and ignored repeated radio warnings. Earlier on Friday the Guard's navy said two other tankers caught fire after striking mines, and later said a third — the supertanker — had done the same. No vessels were named and no crew details were given. The US military denies the mine claims, saying all internationally recognised primary transit routes through the waterway have been cleared of sea mines by US forces. Separately, Oman's Maritime Security Center said Oman evacuated 25 crew members from a Panama-flagged tanker that was attacked 1.6 nautical miles off the Musandam coast and caught fire — but it is unclear whether that incident is the same as any of the three Iran described. That ambiguity matters: Thursday's verified limpet-mine attack on the tanker Louise 1, covered in the morning brief, was a different, confirmed incident, and Friday's claims must not be folded into it as if they were one event.

The Saudi claim and denial follow a pattern both sides have repeated for weeks. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said a drone launched by the Houthis was intercepted and destroyed in the Mecca region on Friday. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree denied it on X and Telegram, saying the group carried out no operation targeting Saudi territory on Friday at all, that its operations target Saudi oil facilities and military bases, and calling the Mecca claim a "gross and outrageous lie." The Houthis have repeatedly denied targeting Mecca and Medina, Islam's two holiest cities, and the coalition has repeatedly reported interceptions headed toward them. Neither side offered evidence on Friday beyond its own statement.

Diplomacy ran in parallel. Iranian state media said Qatari mediators, headed by the country's foreign minister and a senior adviser to the prime minister, returned to Tehran on Friday for talks with senior Iranian officials. The visit follows outreach from US Vice President JD Vance, who sent a letter in recent days to Maj. Gen. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council; Tehran acknowledged receipt on Thursday but said it had not responded, and Trump suggested the letter was about possibly allowing humanitarian aid rather than peace negotiations. Diplomats say US officials have told Middle East countries and regional allies to be prepared for potential renewed military action against Iran after next week's midterm elections.

For ordinary people, the stakes are fuel prices, shipping safety, and, in Yemen itself, displacement. Brent crude had already pushed toward $117 on the week's tanker and refinery risk, as covered in the morning brief, and every new unverified claim moves that price before any damage is confirmed. In Yemen, the UN humanitarian office said the number of displaced people has passed 281,000 between August 1 and October 25, the overwhelming majority fleeing fighting in Taiz and along the west coast, with 263 civilian casualties documented including 67 killed — children accounting for 97 of the casualties and about half of those killed.

In short: Friday brought three Iranian tanker claims with no names, no crews and no independent verification, a disputed Mecca drone claim both sides are using to frame the other, and a fresh Qatari mediation visit — all against a US warning to allies to brace for possible post-midterms action. Treat the tanker claims as developing, not confirmed, and watch for a named vessel, a crew account, or satellite imagery that would settle them.

Sources: Associated Press via Military.com, Reuters via TBS News, Reuters via gCaptain, Reuters via WDSM on the Mecca denial

Open source

  • ConcatRust — a free, local-first CapCut replacement: The truly free, open-source, local-first, cross-platform video editor topped GitDiscover's October 30 daily picks at 12.7K stars (+224 on the day). It is scriptable through a JSON-RPC API and MCP, so it works as both an editor and something an agent can drive. Worth a look if you edit video and want to stay off subscription editors. Source: GitDiscover
  • Ramen Launches Aura Autopilot, its Open-Source Autonomous Game Tester for Unreal Engine, Unity and Godot: Ramen's new open-source playtesting agent plays games in real time and improves as it plays, across shooters, action RPGs, mobile, survival crafting, turn-based JRPGs and real-time strategy in early testing. It works with Claude Code and Codex subscriptions, or bring your own model key in the standalone open-source version. Niche, but genuinely useful for indie game developers who cannot afford a QA team. Source: BusinessWire

Cybersecurity

  • Ivanti's VPN chain is not finished — two more flaws hit CISA's exploited list Friday: CISA added CVE-2026-111357 (privilege escalation to root, CVSS 8.8 per Ivanti) and CVE-2026-118527 (predictable file location for uploads, CVSS 4.3) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 30. Both are pieces of the same attack chain Volexity caught in early October, in which a group it calls UTA0576 broke into a fully patched Ivanti Connect Secure VPN without a password, got root, and went after Active Directory — LAPS passwords, BitLocker recovery keys and service account credentials. Ivanti fixed the front door (CVE-2026-104935) on October 13, but the root step's patch only shipped on October 29: you need 22.8R2.6 or 25.1.1.3. If you patched in mid-October and moved on, you are not done — and because the attackers cleaned up after themselves, a clean result from Ivanti's Integrity Checker does not prove the box was not hit. Federal deadlines: November 2 for CVE-2026-111357, November 13 for CVE-2026-118527. Source: Carthage Electronics CVE Threat Brief, citing CISA and Volexity
  • Binance to resume withdrawals within 48 hours after $6.3B hack — updated timeline: Friday's update narrows the morning brief's estimate: co-founder Yi He said withdrawals are now expected within about 42–45 hours as Binance rebuilds its withdrawal service and security environment, with deposits operating normally. Binance says it has reserved more than $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the incident, $5 billion of it already held in the exact tokens affected, and will update its Proof of Reserves in the coming days. TRM Labs attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group — that attribution is from the research firm, not yet confirmed by Binance, which still describes "additional points of breach" beyond the initially reported compromised DevOps laptop. About $3 billion remains frozen with industry partners. Sources: Binance update, Coin360, Finbold
  • AU Small Finance Bank investigates suspected Salesforce data breach: AU Small Finance Bank said on October 30 it is investigating a potential data leak involving a Salesforce-managed SaaS platform holding limited data on its vehicle-loan customers. The bank says its own systems remain unaffected and banking services are operating normally. A threat-intel post claims the Scarlett Group is behind it and lists KYC records, identity documents, financial records and loan agreements among the data types — that claim is unverified, and a person familiar with the matter told BusinessLine the KYC claims are "unverified and unvalidated." Treat it as an investigation, not a confirmed breach. Source: The Hindu BusinessLine

Major court cases

  • Russell Brand trial — prosecution closing Friday; jury still to retire: Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones gave her closing speech at Southwark Crown Court on Friday, telling jurors six women who do not know one another independently came forward over a decade, that they were seeking closure after encounters that had "haunted them for years," and that Brand's behaviour changed from charming to threatening before he ignored objections — "He didn't listen when (they) said no, he didn't stop, he didn't care." She used a stopwatch to hold 20 seconds of silence for the length of time one woman said she was pulled toward a workplace toilet in 2001. The judge has told jurors only a unanimous verdict will be accepted on each charge, and deliberations are expected to begin early next week, with Tuesday, November 3 the expected retirement date reported earlier. Brand denies all seven charges (three rape, three sexual assault, one attempted indecent assault, six women, 1999–2009). Note: another low-quality blog post circulating Friday claims a verdict has already been returned; that contradicts AP/AAP court reporting showing the trial continuing on Friday, so it is not reported here as fact. Sources: AAP, Dispatch Times
