Morning brief — Friday, November 20, 2026

Russia's promised retaliation hits Kyiv's bridges — and spills into Poland

Russia launched one of the largest missile and drone attacks of the war overnight, hours after Vladimir Putin vowed a response "far more painful" than Ukraine's repeated strikes on the Kerch Bridge. Eight people were killed across Ukraine and 28 injured, and for the first time in the war Russia hit three of Kyiv's bridges across the Dnipro River — the biggest strikes yet on the routes connecting the two halves of the capital. The same barrage also violated Polish airspace, with three objects — likely two missiles and a drone, according to Poland's defence minister — crossing the border and falling in southeastern Poland.

According to Reuters, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia fired more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic, targeting energy, logistics and railway infrastructure across the country. Ukraine's Air Force put the count at 84 cruise missiles plus Iskander-M and S-400 ballistic missiles and Zircon hypersonic missiles, and 404 drones including 123 jet-powered Shaheds — and said air defences downed or suppressed 462 weapons in total. Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said six people were killed in Kyiv and 15 injured, with two more killed at a railway station in Lviv. In Kyiv, missiles hit the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi bridges, forcing closures and weight limits; a water utility facility was hit, killing three employees and dropping water pressure in four districts, and five districts lost power. In Kharkiv, Russian drones hit an emergency hospital, wounding four and damaging its MRI unit. Power outages were reported in ten regions, and emergency cuts were introduced in the west. The attack also spilled over the border: Poland scrambled jets, and its army is investigating reports of objects falling near the village of Chmielek, about 45 km from the Ukrainian border. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there was currently no air threat, while the defence minister denounced the incursion as a Russian "provocation" and Britain's prime minister condemned it.

The two sides are arguing from opposite ends of the same logic. Kyiv says its bridge and refinery strikes are retaliation for Russia's nightly campaign against Ukrainian power plants and cities, and that they will continue until Russia stops hitting Ukrainian infrastructure — Zelenskyy called the overnight attack "highly demonstrative" terror meant to compensate for Russia's "military and diplomatic impotence," and said Putin's attempt to "redraw the world" with Trump and Xi at Thursday's Shenzhen summit had been a "failure." Moscow calls the Kerch Bridge civilian infrastructure — even as it is a key supply route for Russian forces in occupied Crimea — while its own energy strikes on Ukraine continue; Russia's Defence Ministry said it struck plants and warehouses linked to Ukrainian missile and drone production, plus power, transport and port facilities. Washington and its European allies are split on the diplomacy: Trump embraced Putin as a "friend" at the Shenzhen summit, where the three leaders signed a four-point "declaration of peace" that did not mention Ukraine or Iran; European leaders in Berlin warned on Thursday against any attempt to decide a peace settlement over Ukraine's head, and after the summit Zelenskyy and European leaders issued a joint statement urging Russia to accept a ceasefire covering energy infrastructure and Black Sea shipping corridors — which Putin has now rejected.

For people on the ground, the war has removed the idea of a safe hour. Thursday's daytime drone attack on Kyiv killed a 26-year-old man, injured five others, and damaged two European embassies and St. Volodymyr's Cathedral; the overnight barrage then hit homes, a hospital and a water plant while much of Kyiv is already living on around four hours of electricity a day. In the capital, over a third of the city's 3 million residents live on the eastern bank of the Dnipro but most work on the western bank, so damaged bridges mean disrupted commutes for hundreds of thousands of people. In Crimea, the bridge's rail link remains disrupted, with ferries due to start running from Friday as the fallback.

In short: Putin's promised retaliation arrived within hours of his Shenzhen warning, in the form of a multi-wave missile attack that killed eight people, knocked out power across ten regions, hit three Kyiv bridges for the first time, and spilled into Polish airspace — while the diplomacy meant to stop it produced a declaration that said nothing about Ukraine at all. Watch for whether Russia keeps striking into Friday night, how Poland and NATO respond to the airspace violation, and whether the energy-infrastructure ceasefire idea survives the weekend.

Russell Brand sentenced to 10 years and six months — a catch-up the briefs missed

This one is a catch-up: the verdict and sentencing happened on November 5 and 9, but they were never reported in these briefs, which kept saying the trial had "no fresh developments." Russell Brand was sentenced at Southwark Crown Court on Monday, November 9, to 10 years and six months in prison after a jury convicted him of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

The jury, which deliberated for eight and a half hours after a four-week trial that began October 12, convicted the 51-year-old comedian on four of seven charges — raping a woman who had attended one of his comedy shows in 2009, and sexually assaulting three other women between 2004 and 2009 — and cleared him of two further rape counts and one count of attempted indecent assault. Judge Joel Bennathan, passing sentence, described Brand as a "sexual predator" with a "staggering arrogance" and a "casual, arrogant disregard of any woman's right to say no to you." He handed down nine years for the rape, plus nine months, 18 months and eight months for the three sexual assaults, served consecutively to the rape sentence for a total of ten years and six months. Under UK rules Brand must serve two-thirds — about seven years — before he can be released on licence. He showed no emotion as the sentence was delivered, according to the Press Association, and had spent the four nights since the verdict on remand at HMP Wandsworth.

The two sides' positions were starkly different throughout. Prosecutors presented Brand as a "ruthless, calculated and manipulative" predator who used his fame to prey on women; Brand denied all charges, told the court "I am innocent," and said he never had sex without consent. His wife Laura gave evidence calling him a "wonderful father" and an "amazing" husband. The case followed a joint 2023 investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's *Dispatches* that prompted women to come forward. Brand's father Ron disputed the verdict outside court after sentencing. Police have also said Brand is being investigated over two further rape allegations from other women.

For the women involved, the verdict closes a case that began with allegations from more than two decades ago — one complainant read her victim impact statement in person at the sentencing hearing. Brand can still appeal: the window for appealing the conviction runs 28 days from November 5, and for the sentence from November 9. No appeal had been announced as of this morning.

In short: after a trial the briefs wrongly treated as quiet, Russell Brand was convicted on November 5 of one rape and three sexual assaults and sentenced on November 9 to ten and a half years in prison — the biggest court outcome of the month, and one this watch missed until now.

Estonia is closing its road border crossings with Russia from December 1

Estonia said it will close its road border crossings with Russia on security grounds from December 1, after accusing Russian security services of an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence company. The move adds another front to the spillover from the war: Poland's airspace was violated overnight, and Estonia is now hardening its own border.

*Source: Reuters via SRN News*

The European Commission confirms an AI-assisted cyberattack took systems offline

The European Commission confirmed on Thursday it was hit by an AI-driven cyberattack that forced internal systems offline for more than ten days. Spokesman Thomas Regnier said the intrusion was detected October 22 and confirmed as an attack October 26. Systems taken down included Sysper, the HR platform staff use for payslips and leave, and MyPMO, the medical reimbursement portal; translation services also went offline. Most have been restored, though Sysper crashed again on Wednesday. The Commission says it has found no evidence of a data breach affecting staff, but has not confirmed whether personal data was accessed — that question is still open. CERT-EU identified ARTEX, a publicly available AI-powered penetration-testing framework, as a possible tool used in the attack; its developer pulled the project from GitHub in October after reports of misuse. The Commission's top internal crisis body met urgently over the incident, and tech commissioner Henna Virkkunen briefed the full College of Commissioners on Wednesday.

*Sources: Infosecurity Magazine, The Register, SecurityAffairs*

Hackers are exploiting a critical Cisco Expressway flaw in the wild

Threat actors exploited CVE-2026-76466, a critical 9.8 remote command execution flaw in Cisco Expressway, to breach a Russian organisation shortly after a public proof-of-concept was released, Russian security firm F6 reported Thursday. Attackers compromised two internet-facing Expressway devices, then reached two more cluster nodes on the internal network, and used custom Python scripts — possibly AI-assisted, judging by their comments — to scan the network, pull credentials and access GitLab repositories. Patches shipped October 21 (X15.5.20 and later); older X14 releases must migrate. The attack has not been attributed to any known group.

*Source: The Hacker News*

CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its KEV catalog

CISA listed four newly exploited vulnerabilities on November 20: Apache Axis (CVE-2026-90436, 9.8, unauthenticated remote code execution, seen in attacks deploying cryptominers via N-able N-central), REDCap (CVE-2026-90817, 9.8, code injection), LiteLLM (CVE-2026-40217, 8.8, code execution via the guardrails test endpoint, linked to LLMjacking), and Synology DSM (CVE-2024-10441, 9.8, cross-site scripting leading to code execution). Federal agencies must patch by the catalog deadlines.

*Source: ReconBee*

Anthropic slid in premarket on its second day; Nvidia fell with it

Anthropic shares fell 6–8% in premarket trading on Friday, its second day on the Nasdaq, giving back part of Thursday's 17% debut gain that closed at $97.17. Nvidia was down about 2.4% and AMD about 6.8% in the same session, as a broad AI selloff continued — Nvidia is heading for a seventh straight weekly drop. Retail investors traded roughly $772 million of Anthropic stock on Thursday, the most for any IPO debut since Vanda Research began tracking in 2012. ARK Invest bought about 935,000 Anthropic shares across six ETFs on debut day while selling 33,430 Nvidia shares.

Google selected Blue Origin to launch its AI server satellites in the first quarter of 2029, part of a plan to put AI compute in orbit. The move follows Google's Gemini for Mac computer-use launch earlier this week. No technical details or launch count were given.
