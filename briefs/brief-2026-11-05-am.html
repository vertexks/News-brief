The secret strikes before the midterms — and the war that paused

The United States was quietly bombing Iran in the weeks before Tuesday's midterm elections, and the White House worked to keep it that way. The Wall Street Journal reported Thursday that American forces carried out a series of limited, unannounced strikes against Iranian targets — missile launchers and other military equipment — in the run-up to November 3, with senior officials discussing the political cost of admitting the attacks were still going on. One operation took place on November 2, the day before Americans voted: US forces struck targets on Qeshm Island, near the Strait of Hormuz, after Iranian forces fired a surface-to-air missile at an American fighter jet, according to US officials. The Journal says the strikes were designed to limit Iran's military capability while avoiding casualties, and that Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio were part of the discussions, which President Trump approved.

The secrecy is the story. On November 2, the same day as the Qeshm strike, Trump told reporters American forces had "not been engaged in combat for months." The Journal reports administration officials worried that renewed attention to the Iran fighting would damage Republican prospects in the elections — and the elections went badly for them anyway. Democrats won control of both the House and Senate, and Vance has publicly denounced the war as the primary cause of the Republican defeats. Trump has since acknowledged the war contributed to the losses, particularly through gasoline prices, while defending his decision to launch it.

What happens next is genuinely undecided, and the two sides of the administration are pulling in opposite directions. Trump said Wednesday he has no immediate plans for new large-scale strikes, telling the Financial Times "we're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it," and describing the situation as "a brilliant situation." But Axios reports the US military is finishing preparations for a possible resumption of major combat operations, sending more assets to the region, with no final decision made and even Trump's closest aides unsure what he will do. Qatari mediators, meanwhile, are working on a draft agreement that could head off a return to fighting and lead to direct talks in the coming days. Iran's position hasn't moved: its top security official, Mohsen Rezaei, called Washington's reply to Tehran's seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz "not convincing," and the strait stays shut until they are fully met.

For ordinary people, the clearest signal is at the pump. Brent crude fell to $95.07 on Thursday, down almost 10% in a week to its lowest in two months, as the threat of renewed strikes receded. But the damage from months of war is still in the system: EA WorldView reports US diesel reserves have fallen below 100 million barrels for the first time in more than 20 years, and diesel is running about $6.27 a gallon — roughly 75% higher than a year ago, close to an all-time record. Inside Iran, the crackdown continues: Tehran executed two more participants in January's nationwide protests on Wednesday, Amirhossein Maleki and Amirhossein Ebrahimi, sentenced to death over the killing of four police officers in Isfahan.

In short: Washington was fighting a war it wouldn't admit to before the election, the election punished it anyway, and now the choice is between a deal the Qataris are still trying to broker and a new round of strikes the military is already preparing for. Nothing is decided, and the fuel-price pain that helped cost Republicans the midterms hasn't gone away.

Russell Brand found guilty of rape and three sexual assaults

A jury at Southwark Crown Court in London found Russell Brand guilty on Thursday of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, after nine hours and thirteen minutes of deliberations spread over three days. The 51-year-old comedian was acquitted of two other rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault. Judge Joel Bennathan told Brand he faced a "significant custodial term," remanded him in custody, and set sentencing for Monday, November 9.

The convictions cover four of the six women who accused Brand of offences between 1999 and 2009. He was found guilty of raping a 21-year-old fan after meeting her at a 2009 stand-up show at the Hammersmith Apollo; of sexually assaulting a Capital Radio worker in a corridor in 2005; of sexually assaulting a Channel 4 worker during a forced massage in 2009; and of groping an MTV worker at a Soho bar party in 2004 — though the jury cleared him of orally raping that same woman in a toilet afterwards. He was acquitted of raping a woman after a 1999 Labour Party conference fringe show in Bournemouth, and of trying to drag an MTV worker into a toilet to indecently assault her in 2001.

The trial turned on consent, and the two sides told the jury opposite stories about the same facts. Brand testified that he had "misused" his fame "very, very selfishly" for sexual encounters in the past, but insisted he never engaged in non-consensual activity — "consent is fundamental, essential, necessary, and in many ways, more important than sex itself." His barrister, Ian Winter, argued the case was "thoroughly contaminated" by Channel 4's 2023 Dispatches documentary, saying the complainants only came forward at the programme's invitation and the publicity made a fair trial impossible. Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones pushed back in closing, noting the six women did not know each other and had nothing to gain: no limelight, no documentary appearance, no compensation claims — only, she said, "perhaps some closure by coming before you and telling you their accounts of what happened to them so long ago."

For the women, the verdict ends a case that began with the 2023 joint investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4, and ran for three weeks of testimony in which Brand himself took the stand. Brand stood straight and still in the dock as the verdicts were read, closing his eyes when the rape conviction was announced, and told his wife Laura Gallacher "I love you" before being taken into custody. The Metropolitan Police told Sky News they are investigating Brand over other rape allegations made by two more women, so this may not be the last case.

In short: after a three-week trial and three days of deliberations, a jury believed four of the six women and disbelieved two of the most serious allegations — a mixed verdict that still leaves Brand, 51, facing a significant prison sentence on Monday.

Ukraine overnight: quieter night, but the refinery war goes on

Russia launched 2 missiles and 128 drones at Ukraine overnight, with 105 shot down or neutralized, according to Ukraine's General Staff. One person was injured in the Kyiv region when a Russian attack set a logistics facility ablaze. Ukraine hit back at the Ryazan oil refinery, where a drone attack set off a large fire — at least six hits recorded — injuring one person, damaging power lines and shattering windows at a school, prompting class cancellations. Russia's Defence Ministry claimed 496 Ukrainian drones were intercepted. The toll from Monday's strike on the Komyshuvakha aid centre stands at 40 dead. Today in Brussels, Ukraine, the US, France, Germany, the UK, NATO and the World Bank meet on a post-war prosperity plan, with a separate security-guarantees session on Thursday. Source

Saudi Arabia warns Yanbu oil hub is in danger

Saudi Arabia issued alerts on Thursday warning of potential danger in Yanbu, the kingdom's Red Sea oil export hub, as Houthi attacks continue. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a missile launched by the group towards Medina, and civil defence issued emergency alerts for Sharurah, near the Yemeni border. The Houthis had not claimed any attack on Thursday; on Wednesday they said they had attacked Riyadh's international airport while Syria's President Ahmed al-Sharaa was visiting — Saudi authorities have not confirmed it, and the airport kept operating. Another tanker hit near Qatar

A tanker was struck by two unidentified projectiles about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, at around 2:20 a.m. local time on Thursday, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre. The crew's status and the extent of the damage were unconfirmed. It is the latest in the run of shipping attacks around the Gulf as the Hormuz standoff drags on. Source

Israel: Eisenkot now favourite for the mandate

President Isaac Herzog's consultations moved decisively toward Gadi Eisenkot on Thursday. United Torah Judaism — a Netanyahu ally in the previous five elections — declined to recommend any candidate, a major setback for the prime minister. The Arab-led Ra'am list endorsed Eisenkot, as did the Democrats, while Otzma Yehudit backed Netanyahu. Herzog meets Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar party on Thursday evening to close the consultations, and has until November 11 to decide who gets the first 28 days to try to form a government. With 48 opposition seats plus 19 Arab-led recommendations, Eisenkot is now the favourite for the mandate — though a recommendation is not the same as a governing coalition, and he has ruled out governing with the Arab parties. Cyber: the TV-box botnet built by the manufacturer

Google and partners dismantled XCPE, a botnet hidden in the firmware of more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes across 200+ countries — a catch-up from Google Threat Intelligence Group's November 2 report. Google says with high confidence that the manufacturer, Videostrong Technology Co., intentionally built and ran the backdoor for financial gain, renting the boxes out for ad fraud, proxy traffic and malware delivery. The backdoor ran with system privileges, loaded only after first boot so it dodged certification checks, and reinstalled itself if removed. Google took legal action against the command-and-control domains, banned Videostrong from making certified Android devices, and says the takedown also severed access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices. Source

Cyber: WordPress upgrade flaw now has a public exploit

A working public proof of concept for WordPress CVE-2026-119977 was published on GitHub on November 4. The flaw is a race condition in WordPress Core's update process (versions 4.7 through 7.1.3) that lets an unauthenticated attacker run PHP code on the server. CISA added it to the exploited-vulnerabilities catalog on November 3, and Wordfence blocked more than 12,000 attacks in a 24-hour window. The federal remediation deadline is tomorrow, November 6 — update to WordPress 7.1.4. No new CISA KEV additions on November 5; the catalog stands at 1,768 entries. Source
