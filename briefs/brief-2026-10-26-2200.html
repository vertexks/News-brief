World escalation

Kyiv hit by ballistic missiles hours before the Oleshky truce was due to start

Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv late Monday evening, hitting a university campus, a metro-station area, homes and medical facilities — just hours before a local humanitarian truce was due to begin in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky. It is a developing story, and the casualty count is still moving.

Reuters reports that at least one person was killed and 10 injured, many of them outside a student dormitory at the State Trade and Economics University on the east side of the Dnipro River. Nine people were in hospital, three of them in serious condition, according to Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko. The Guardian's briefing, published later, put the wounded at eight and said medical facilities and an intensive care unit were damaged — a reminder that the toll is not yet settled. A second wave of missiles was reported in the middle of the night, hitting an administrative building in the Podilskyi district, according to the Kyiv Independent via Europe Says, with the full consequences still unclear.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said the missiles hit "very crowded areas, and there is not a single military facility nearby," calling it "a strike directly on the city, on its infrastructure." Moscow has long denied targeting civilians, and Russia has not commented on this specific strike in the coverage checked. Zelensky also used the attack to press his air-defence argument again: he said Ukraine has been waiting nearly a month for US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on missiles for Patriot and NASAMS systems, and that "artificial delays in air defence for Ukraine are helping terror."

For ordinary people in Kyiv, a city of about three million, this is the pattern of recent weeks: sirens and explosions at night, power cut in districts east of the Dnipro, and now a university dormitory and hospital wards in the damage reports. Paramedics were among the injured — two emergency crew members were hurt rushing to one strike scene, Klitschko said.

The timing is what makes this strike land harder than a routine nightly barrage. The Oleshky truce, agreed last Friday, was due to begin at 3 a.m. local time Tuesday (0100 GMT), to let food, medicine and evacuations reach a town occupied since 2022 where only a few thousand of its 24,000 prewar residents remain. According to the Guardian, no end date has been given, and neither Russia, Ukraine nor the US has said who would monitor it or given details of the humanitarian operation. Russia's defence ministry said Friday it would halt fire in the "Kherson sector" for the duration of the ceasefire; Ukraine has accused Russia of blocking residents from leaving, a charge Moscow denies. As of this brief, there is no confirmation in the coverage checked that the truce is holding — that is the first thing to check in the morning.

In short: a deadly strike on civilian Kyiv on the eve of a fragile local truce, with air-defence supplies still the argument in Kyiv and Washington — and Oleshky's first hours under the truce unconfirmed.

Sources: Reuters via SRN News, October 26; Guardian war briefing via Stocks99, October 27; Kyiv Independent via Europe Says, October 26–27

  • Romania: Russia-linked arson network alleged. Romania said Monday that a network "coordinated" from Russia was involved in starting a fire on September 14 near an arms factory in Brasov; two Belarusians and a Ukrainian, residents in EU countries, were involved, two were tracked down and remanded in Germany while preparing similar offences, and a third is at large in Poland, according to Romania's organised-crime office. Russia's embassy in Bucharest could not immediately be reached for comment, and Moscow has repeatedly denied such accusations. The Guardian briefing also notes at least eight cargo ships hit off NATO members Romania and Bulgaria in the past three weeks, with some 34 sailors reported dead or missing — part of what NATO and the EU describe as a Russian hybrid campaign, which Moscow denies. Source: Guardian war briefing via Stocks99, October 27
  • Israel votes Tuesday. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu faces a fight for political survival, Reuters reports, with voters weighing his leadership after the 2023 Hamas-led attacks and the wars in Gaza and with Iran. Polls show a close race between Netanyahu's Likud and its right-wing allies and a rival alliance led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot. Netanyahu is campaigning on his wartime leadership and his alliance with President Trump, who has endorsed him; Eisenkot accuses Netanyahu of "fear-mongering, lies and threats" and says Trump would work with any leader chosen by voters. Neither side has a clear path to a governing coalition in the 120-seat parliament, Reuters notes — if neither can form one, Netanyahu could stay on as caretaker until another election next year. Polls open 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. local time Tuesday. Source: Reuters via 93-5 The Lloyd, October 26

Cybersecurity

  • CISA adds two critical PHP flaws to its exploited list — federal deadline October 29. CISA added CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 26, SSJ News reports. Both are unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws in PHP's web-serving configurations, rated CVSS 9.2, with fixed releases PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 (PHP 8.1 and earlier get no patches). CISA's updated alert directs organizations that cannot update immediately, or still run unsupported versions, to take PHP-based systems offline until they can be patched. This escalates the PHP2Shell flaws already reported Monday morning from "reported exploited" to formally listed as exploited with a federal deadline. Source: SSJ News, October 26
  • State Bank of India breach disclosed. India's state-run State Bank of India, serving more than 500 million customers, disclosed a data breach after 3,897 customer records appeared online, Check Point Research reports in its October 26 threat report. Exposed information reportedly included contact details, residential addresses, birth dates, bank profiles, loan records and identity-verification documents. The bank reported no disruption to core services. Source: Check Point Research, October 26
  • Otherwise: no new critical vulnerability or major breach disclosure Monday evening beyond the PHP escalation above; the Node.js Brotli flaw (CVE-2026-111497) and BeyondTrust/eMudhra items from the evening brief remain the standing patch priorities.
