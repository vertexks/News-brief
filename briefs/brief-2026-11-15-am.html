World escalation

Russia pounds Ukraine's power grid and railways overnight; Ukraine hits back deep inside Russia

Russia launched one of its heaviest drone nights of the month against Ukraine overnight from Saturday into Sunday, hitting energy and railway infrastructure in eight regions, while Ukraine struck a major petrochemical plant and an oil refinery deep inside Russia. The exchange comes as winter closes in and with no ceasefire of any kind in effect.

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 132 long-range attack drones, 78 of them jet-powered, starting Saturday evening, and that 106 were shot down or suppressed by Sunday morning, with hits recorded at 20 locations. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the attacks mainly targeted power substations, struck with Shahed-type drones, and hit infrastructure in Odesa, Dnipropetrovsk, Kharkiv and five other regions. In the Odesa region, a DTEK energy facility was hit and about 41,000 homes lost power, with the operator warning repairs will take time. Russian missiles also struck near Vyshhorod outside Kyiv, and jet-powered drones set fire to an industrial facility near Kropyvnytskyi. Guided aerial bombs damaged apartment blocks in Zaporizhzhia, injuring six people including a two-year-old. Russia's defence ministry, for its part, confirmed strikes on substations, port infrastructure and locomotives, and said a bulk carrier heading for Odesa was hit — claims that could not be independently verified.

Ukraine answered overnight with strikes of its own. Its General Staff said Ukrainian forces hit the Nizhnekamskneftekhim petrochemical plant — a major producer of rubber and plastics — and an oil refining facility in Russia's Tatarstan region, more than 1,000 km from the front, as well as the Monolit defence and aviation plant in Bryansk region, which makes helicopters and spare parts. Neither side's claims about the extent of the damage could be independently verified, and the two militaries released sharply conflicting tallies, with Moscow claiming to have downed hundreds of Ukrainian drones.

Zelensky again pointed the finger at SpaceX's Starshield service, saying many Russian strikes are getting through because speed restrictions on Starshield terminals along the front line and in occupied territory are still in place. "The Russians are targeting substations with 'shaheds'. And many of these strikes are getting through because restrictions on the operating speed of Starshield terminals along the front line and in the temporarily occupied territories are still there," he said. Kyiv has raised the Starshield issue repeatedly since October, when Ukrainian officials said restrictions disrupted military operations during a ballistic missile barrage; SpaceX has not publicly explained the limits.

Kyiv's argument is that Russia is systematically dismantling the power supply ordinary Ukrainians depend on as temperatures drop, and that the US is undercutting Ukraine's defences at the same time. Moscow frames its strikes as aimed at military and logistics targets — railways, ports and substations feeding the war effort — and says its air defences are swatting down Ukrainian drones in large numbers. Washington, meanwhile, has said nothing new about the Starshield dispute, and Trump has accused Zelensky of "not wanting" to end the war, a charge Kyiv rejects.

For ordinary people, the practical effect is the same as it has been all autumn: power cuts, damaged homes and disrupted trains. Around 41,000 households in the Odesa region woke up without electricity on Sunday, repairs are expected to take time, and the Zaporizhzhia apartment strike is a reminder that the campaign is hitting residential areas as well as infrastructure. Ukraine's deep strikes into Tatarstan are meant to raise the cost for Moscow, but they have not stopped the nightly drone waves.

In short: the nightly war of attrition on infrastructure is grinding on with no pause in sight — Russia hit power and rail in eight regions, Ukraine hit a petrochemical plant and refinery far behind Russian lines, and the Starshield dispute between Kyiv and Washington is still unresolved.

Three tankers ablaze in Hormuz as Iran confirms a mine strike; Riyadh intercepts more Houthi missiles

Iran's navy has confirmed that a mine strike in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday set three oil tankers ablaze, and Saudi air defences intercepted two more Houthi ballistic missiles over Riyadh on Sunday, as the Gulf conflict keeps tightening its grip on shipping, markets and daily life across the region.

The Iranian navy confirmation, reported by state media and carried by Reuters on Sunday, is a notable shift: for weeks Tehran has claimed tanker mine strikes that maritime authorities could not verify, and it gave no vessel names, flags or owners this time either. Separately, the Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday it had downed two ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh along with three drones over the kingdom's oil-rich Eastern Province. The UK's maritime trade body, UKMTO, also issued a fresh warning (205-26) about an attack on a tanker transiting the Gulf eastbound before it entered the strait, based on an Indian shipping report; the crew are safe and the vessel, the means of attack and the attacker are not named. Gulf stock markets slid on Sunday on the news — Saudi Arabia's benchmark down 0.7%, Qatar down 1.8%.

Inside Yemen, the ground war shifted as well. Yemeni government forces withdrew from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb strait, and Houthi media claimed control of the Lahj coast — a claim no independent party has verified. Local sources reported that a missile hit cars carrying displaced people in Khor Omaira, Lahj, killing 8 civilians and wounding 14, though there is no official confirmation and it is unclear who fired it. Houthi strikes on Saudi airports, seaports and energy installations have intensified in recent days, and Riyadh has answered by expanding its air campaign against Houthi targets in Yemen.

Tehran's position, as stated by its navy and foreign ministry, is that vessels using what it calls unauthorised routes with their navigation systems switched off are fair game, and that it will not leave attacks unanswered. Riyadh and its coalition partners say the Houthi attacks are indiscriminate — aimed at airports, holy sites and civilian infrastructure — and that their interceptions are defensive. Washington has stayed out of direct strikes, offering intelligence and targeting help instead, and US officials have said they expect no US airstrikes before the November 3 midterms. The wider region is hedging: Turkey and Qatar sealed a mutual defence pact on Friday, pledging a united front against any foreign armed attack on either country, and a long-standing Qatari LNG contract has been cancelled on force majeure grounds — the first since the conflict began — with analysts saying Poland's Orlen could save up to $2 billion from the cancellation.

For ordinary people in the region, the war is now a daily fact of life: Riyadh residents face regular missile alerts, Saudi airports remain disrupted, shipping crews transit one of the world's most important oil chokepoints under threat of mines, and Gulf markets are sliding. The displaced families killed at Khor Omaira are a reminder that the heaviest price is still being paid by Yemeni civilians caught between the two sides.

In short: Iran has now confirmed a mine strike that set three tankers ablaze, the Houthis keep firing at Riyadh, the coalition keeps intercepting, and the ground war in Yemen is moving in the Houthis' favour at Bab el-Mandab — with no sign of de-escalation from any side.

Trump–Putin–Xi trilateral confirmed for Thursday in Shenzhen — but Ukraine is off the agenda

The Kremlin confirmed on Saturday that Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump will meet on Thursday, November 19, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen — the first-ever trilateral meeting of the three leaders. The White House announced the meeting on Friday; Beijing has still not confirmed it will take place. A senior US official said the agenda is nuclear arms control and Iran, and that Ukraine is not on it — Washington says the Ukraine war is handled on a separate US–Russia track. No agreement is expected; officials describe it as a first conversation on nuclear reductions since the last US–Russia treaty, New START, expired in February. Zelensky has objected to Ukraine's future being discussed without Kyiv at the table, and European leaders in Dublin reiterated that European security cannot be decided without Europe. No separate Trump–Putin one-on-one is scheduled.

Cybersecurity

Microsoft 365 outage and FAA ground stop both resolved — and both blamed on routine upgrades, not attacks

Microsoft says its Microsoft 365 outage is over. The disruption ran about 36 hours from Thursday into Friday, cutting off Outlook, Teams and Copilot for users in more than 70 countries; Microsoft says it was introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade and surfaced after a more recent network change, exposing a latent bug. The company says there was no cyber activity involved and promised a preliminary report within two business days. Separately, the FAA's nationwide ground stop on Friday — about four and a half hours, with roughly 7,000–10,000 flights delayed or cancelled — was caused by a router flooding the air traffic control network during a contractor's copper-to-fiber telecom cutover at a remote site. Officials say it was not a cyberattack, and the FAA administrator said schedules should be back to normal by late Sunday. Both incidents were still unfolding in Saturday's briefs; the causes are now confirmed as technical failures, though neither formal report has been published yet.

Israel government formation

Likud and Yashar negotiating teams meet today; committee chairs being carved up

The Likud and Yashar negotiating teams meet today (Sunday) to discuss a possible unity government, a meeting fixed before Netanyahu's public appeal on Saturday — which Eisenkot dismissed as "spin" and "tricks." The teams: Yashar sends Yoav Horowitz and Chili Tropper; Likud sends Ofir Katz and Michael Rabello. Meanwhile the Eisenkot bloc is consolidating control of key Knesset committees: Yair Golan's Democratic Party will chair the special committee reviewing Netanyahu's judicial reform, and Avigdor Liberman is set to chair Foreign Affairs and Defense. The Knesset speaker vote is Tuesday, November 17 at 4 PM, with Matan Kahana (Yashar) nominated — the first real test of whether Eisenkot has the numbers. Eisenkot's mandate runs to December 6, with a possible 14-day extension.
