1. Ukraine/Russia + Poland

Houthis claim a coast-to-coast strike wave; Saudis report intercepts over Taif and Yanbu, and Sanaa reports 11 dead in retaliation strikes

The Saturday fighting in the Saudi–Houthi war widened from the capital to the Red Sea coast. In a statement carried by the Houthi-run Saba news agency at about 7 PM local time, Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed seven missile and drone operations: King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, an Aramco facility in Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery, pumping stations and the East-West crude pipeline, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, Najran airport, and the air base at Khamis Mushait. Saree claimed every target was hit and that air traffic at Dammam was disrupted. None of that has been independently verified, and Saudi authorities had not confirmed any hits on oil sites at the time of writing, according to Defense and Tech's summary of the day's statements.

Caption: This is a summary graphic, not a photograph — it lays out the three competing accounts from Saturday: what the Houthis claim they hit, what the Saudi coalition says it intercepted, and the death toll Sanaa's health ministry reports from Saudi retaliation strikes. The graphic matters because, with no independent verification of the oil-site claims, the claims themselves are the story so far.

What the coalition does confirm is a second wave of intercepts after the morning's Riyadh missile, which was covered in the morning brief. Later Saturday, the coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Taif and Yanbu, and Saudi Civil Defence briefly issued alerts for Taif, Yanbu, Mecca and Jeddah, lifting them about ten minutes later, Reuters reports via Defense and Tech. Civil Defence said debris from the intercepted missiles fell in Wadi al-Mughammas in Mecca, Jeddah's al-Nuzha district, Wadi Jalil in Taif, and the Mastoura area of Rabigh governorate. No casualties were mentioned in the Civil Defence statement, and it did not address the Houthi claims about Aramco, the pipeline, or the Rabigh refinery.

The two sides tell very different stories about the retaliation. Saree said the strikes were retaliation for what he described as 100 Saudi air strikes on Sanaa and other Houthi-held areas in the previous 24 hours. The Houthi-run Health Ministry in Sanaa said Saudi aircraft hit a house in the capital's Asr area, killing three people and injuring three, and hit workers' housing at a cement factory in Hamdan district, killing eight people — including three women and four children — and injuring 15. Defense and Tech notes it had not seen a coalition statement on those reports. Separately, Yemen's internationally recognised government said three Houthi ballistic missiles hit coast guard sites in Aden, Reuters reports.

Why Yanbu and Rabigh matter: Yanbu, on the Red Sea, is the western end of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, which lets the kingdom export crude without sending it through the Strait of Hormuz. Rabigh, on the same coast north of Jeddah, hosts a large refining complex. Any confirmed damage there would matter well beyond Saudi Arabia — which is exactly why the gap between the Houthi claim and the Saudi account matters, and why neither should be taken as confirmed yet. One earlier claim did gain partial Saudi confirmation Saturday: on Thursday, Oct 22, the Houthis claimed attacks on the defence ministry in Riyadh, Aramco facilities in Yanbu, and Dammam airport, and Saudi authorities said two people were killed in Dammam but did not confirm the other two claims, according to Reuters.

In short: Saturday's war of claims stretched from Riyadh to the Red Sea coast, with confirmed intercepts over Taif and Yanbu, unconfirmed Houthi claims of hits on Yanbu, Rabigh and the East-West pipeline, and 11 reported dead in Sanaa from Saudi retaliation strikes. Watch for any Saudi confirmation or denial on the oil sites, and any independent evidence of damage at Yanbu or Rabigh.

Crimea's blackout is now a rationing regime — power and water restricted, trolleybuses halted, no restoration timeline

Saturday brought the first official picture of how bad the Crimea blackout is, and it is worse than a one-night outage. Sergei Aksyonov, the Moscow-installed head of Crimea, said restrictions on electricity and water are now in force across the peninsula after what he called an accident on the main power lines outside Crimea caused by a drone attack. Sevastopol's Russian-installed governor, Mikhail Razvozhaev, said only about 40% of the city's basic mobile-communication base stations are working, trolleybus service has been suspended, and water has been supplied at reduced pressure in parts of Sevastopol's Leninsky district since 9 AM Saturday, with water trucks organised for residents. Social facilities are running on backup power, and Razvozhaev said generator reserves last about four hours before mobile crews recharge them. Kyiv24 reports the timeline for full restoration is currently unknown.

Caption: The lead image from Kyiv24's report on the Crimea blackout shows the peninsula dealing with the aftermath of the overnight outage — the image matters because it is the visible side of a blackout that Russian-installed officials now confirm has forced electricity and water rationing across Crimea, not just a few dark hours.

The details behind the blackout are still contested. Monitoring channel Crimean Wind reported a fire at the Taman substation in Russia's Krasnodar Krai, through which all four lines of the Kerch Strait energy bridge run, and NASA FIRMS satellite imagery indicated damage there, according to earlier reporting. Nightwatch's Saturday intelligence summary says Russia's Energy Ministry confirmed new power disruption affecting consumers in occupied Crimea and occupied parts of Kherson, Zaporizhzhia, Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, but cautions that the Taman fire is corroborated while its connection to the full outage footprint and the extent of physical damage remain unresolved. The Kyiv Independent could not independently verify the extent of the blackouts, and Ukrainian military officials have not commented. For ordinary people, the distinction matters less than the rationing: rolling outages, patchy mobile service, suspended electric transport, and reduced water pressure are now the daily reality until the grid is repaired.

Elsewhere in Ukraine on Saturday, Ukrainian officials said Russian drone and missile strikes across eastern Ukraine killed at least five people, according to Storysh, and a Russian strike on Chernihiv set a disused building ablaze and injured two people, EGazette reports. In Russia, Nightwatch notes imagery supporting fires after UAV activity over Omsk, but says identification of the affected facilities remains preliminary.

In short: Crimea has gone from blackout to rationing — power, water, mobile service and trolleybuses all restricted, with no restoration date — while the exact damage at the Taman substation and its link to the full outage remain unverified. Watch for restoration claims versus renewed blackouts in Simferopol, Yalta and Alushta, which already flickered once after initial restoration claims.

Oleshky — new details on the Oct 27 deal: Reuters, CNN and The Times have now filled in the diplomacy behind the local ceasefire reported in the morning brief. The deal was brokered with US First Lady Melania Trump acting as what her office calls the "exclusive intermediary," working directly with Ukrainian and Russian leadership; she said Oleshky's remaining 1,700–2,000 residents are mostly elderly in urgent need of medicine, food and clean water, with 30–50 children also thought to be in the town, and that residents who evacuate will get transport to their preferred destination — "some will want to go to Russia, and some to Ukraine." Newsmax says it was granted exclusive TV access to document the operation. On the wider war, Zelensky said technical-level talks with Russia and the US are set for the first half of November in Abu Dhabi, while a US official later said the next round could be in Abu Dhabi or Switzerland within a couple of weeks. The Oct 27 vs Oct 29 start-date dispute flagged in the morning brief is still unresolved. Sources: Reuters · CNN · The Times

Hormuz — the oil paradox: Total oil flows actually reached 24.5 million barrels per day in the week ending Oct 18 — slightly above the 24.4 million pre-war average from Feb 22 — according to Kpler data reported by TuzaraPost, because Gulf producers rerouted exports around the strait via the Red Sea and other alternatives. But traffic through the strait itself is a fraction of normal: about 20 ships a day versus 100+ before the war, with many running dark (transponders off). US Central Command's claim of about 400 merchant transits in the preceding week is far higher than Kpler's count — the gap itself is a measure of how murky the strait picture is. The IRGC says the strait is closed; CENTCOM says it is open; both can be half-right at once when "open" means a trickle of dark ships. Source: TuzaraPost — Oil flows return to pre-war levels

2. Tech: Apple, Nvidia, Google

iPhone Duo launch day: lines returned, walk-ins sold out within hours, resales hit $10,000 — and Apple briefly retook the world's most-valuable crown

Apple's first foldable had the kind of launch day the iPhone hasn't seen in years. MacRumors reports queues of dozens to hundreds of people at Apple Stores worldwide on Friday, with some lining up a day or two early; Apple's CEO John Ternus and retail chief Deirdre O'Brien greeted customers in Austin, while marketing chief Greg Joswiak did the same at Fifth Avenue in New York, where video showed a massive queue as early as 4:15 AM. Apple had warned walk-in stock would be "limited," and it was: the NY Post reports Fifth Avenue was out of stock for walk-ins by around 10 AM, King of Prussia (Pennsylvania) sold out shortly after 8 AM, and other stores followed within hours. The scarcity fed instant resale markups — eBay listings up to $10,000 for a 2TB Night Sky model against a $3,199 purchase price, and a Craigslist listing at $7,999, per the NY Post.

The launch day also briefly reshuffled the market-cap leaderboard. 9to5Mac reports Apple traded above $351 on Friday, a new intraday record, reaching a market value around $5.12 trillion and retaking the title of world's most valuable company from Nvidia. The lead did not clearly hold: one tracker had Nvidia back ahead by roughly $20 billion at the close, and Ainvest notes the flip was driven mostly by Nvidia's 4.4% fall on Oct 22 in a broad AI-stock sell-off rather than by Duo sales alone — Apple rose only about 1% to its record close. For buyers, the practical takeaway is simpler than the market-cap drama: Duo demand is real, walk-in supply is thin, and Apple had pointed shoppers to a Saturday check of the Apple Store app for pickup availability.

In short: the Duo's launch day combined genuine demand (lines, same-day sellouts) with scalper markups up to 3x retail, and a market-cap crown that Apple grabbed intraday and may not have kept at the close. Watch for restock timing and whether the hinge-crease reports from early owners turn into a real defect pattern.

3. Open source

NVIDIA OpenShell — the agent sandbox runtime, trending: OpenShell, Nvidia's open-source runtime for running AI agents safely, hit GitHub trending on Saturday with about 18.7k stars. It is Apache-2.0, written in Rust, latest release v0.1.5 (Oct 20). Each agent runs in its own kernel-enforced sandbox, every network connection passes a policy check before leaving the sandbox, agents never see real credentials (OpenShell injects them only into requests to approved endpoints), and risky policy changes get flagged by formal verification before a human approves them. Why it matters: it is the practical answer to "let the agent do things, but not everything" — the same OpenShell name appeared in Nvidia's GTC Berlin reference architecture this week, but this is the runtime itself gaining traction. Sources: DigiSoulHub — NVIDIA OpenShell · SiliconNoon — Open Source Radar Oct 24

4. Cybersecurity

PHP2Shell — first confirmed break-in, Palo Alto affected, patch lag now the story: The flaw is no longer just a warning. Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 reports the first confirmed PHP2Shell compromise, at a European government body, carried out with an AI attack agent, according to Carthage Electronics' Oct 24 threat brief. Palo Alto confirmed on Oct 24 that the flaws affect its PAN-OS and Panorama management interfaces (product impact rated 8.7, below the underlying PHP 9.2), with no malicious exploitation against its own products known so far — fixed hotfixes are dated Oct 26 (11.1.16-h4) and Oct 28 (12.2.4-h1), with Panorama versions still to be confirmed; until then, restrict management-interface access to trusted addresses only. Meanwhile the patch lag is measurable: more than 21,000 Danish websites still lacked PHP security updates as of Saturday, per Danish industry reporting via NordiskPost, with Denmark's Aula school platform and the Danish Meteorological Institute among precautionary shutdowns (Norway and Finland took similar precautions), and South Africa's Presidency website remained offline as a precaution, with a spokesperson telling Reuters there was no indication of a breach. Cloudflare's Matthew Prince said exploitation attempts on Cloudflare's network rose from thousands before disclosure to millions after. Most of what researchers see is still scanning, not confirmed mass break-ins — but the first confirmed compromise means the window for "we'll patch Monday" is closing. Sources: Carthage Electronics — Oct 24 CVE threat brief · AIAgentUpdate — PHP2Shell mass exploitation · NordiskPost — 21,000 Danish websites still lack PHP updates

HPE Aruba — eight CVSS 10.0 flaws, patch this weekend if you run Aruba gear: HPE's advisory HPESBNW05162 (Oct 22) patches 72 vulnerabilities in the ArubaOS systems running wireless networks, and eight carry the maximum CVSS score of 10.0 — authentication bypasses, command injection, SQL injection and buffer overflows letting an unauthenticated attacker run commands on AOS-8 and AOS-10 Mobility Conductors, Controllers, Gateways and Access Points, several via the PAPI access-point management protocol on UDP port 8211. HPE says it is not aware of exploit code or attacks. Fixed releases: AOS-10 10.8.1.2, 10.7.2.7, 10.4.1.14 and AOS-8 8.13.3.1, 8.10.0.24. Aruba gear sits in a lot of schools, clinics, warehouses and offices, so this is a weekend-planning item, not a Monday item. Source: Carthage Electronics — Oct 24 CVE threat brief

Quest Diagnostics breach confirmed — patient records accessed, scope still unknown: Quest told the SEC (8-K filed Oct 22) that it learned on Oct 21 of unauthorised access to its Quanum Lab Services Manager platform, used by healthcare clients to order lab tests and retrieve results. Its Oct 23 security notice confirmed protected health information and personally identifiable information were involved, including "personal information and patient records," but the company has not disclosed how many patients are affected, which data types were taken, or when the intrusion began. Clinics relying on Quanum LSM are being told to place orders on paper forms or through Quanum eLabs, per BleepingComputer reporting. If your doctor sends samples to Quest, this is one to watch — not a reason to panic, but a reason to watch insurance statements and unexpected messages. Sources: TechyCluster — Quest breach guide · BallerAlert — Quest breach · Safeguard — Quest Quanum LSM 8-K analysis

LiteLLM shipped a malicious release — check if you pulled 1.107.0: LiteLLM disclosed on Oct 24 that a master key for its Agent Platform, committed to a public repository on May 27, 2026, sat unrevoked for 136 days and was used from Oct 10 to steal an org owner's GitHub token — ending with a malicious LiteLLM 1.107.0 published to PyPI, Docker Hub and GHCR on Oct 21. The poisoned PyPI release was live for roughly 1 hour 45 minutes before quarantine; the container tags stayed poisoned for about two and a half hours. Anyone who installed LiteLLM 1.107.0 or pulled the affected container tags on Oct 21 should treat exposed inference keys (Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, Together AI and more, per the report) as compromised and rotate them. Single-source disclosure report — treat the details as reported, but the remediation advice stands regardless. Source: DEV — Leaked LiteLLM master key
