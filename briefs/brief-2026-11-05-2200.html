Turkey ratifies the Mecca defence pact — Saudi Arabia now has a treaty ally on paper, but no troops yet

Turkey's parliament ratified the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement on Thursday evening, voting 279 to 119 to approve the trilateral pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan that treats an attack on any one of the three as an attack on all three. The vote, reported by Reuters and the Associated Press, settles the question left open in the evening brief, when only one low-quality outlet had claimed ratification and the major wires had not confirmed it. The agreement was signed in Mecca on August 7 by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, and it now goes to the Official Gazette before the president formally approves it.

The pact itself is 16 articles. Article 1 commits the signatories to use "all necessary means" to eliminate threats to each other's territorial integrity, and Article 2 says that if one party suffers an armed attack or military aggression, the others will immediately assist "utilising the full range of mutual assistance measures available to them" — language that deliberately mirrors NATO's Article 5. The other articles commit the three to joint training and exercises, intelligence sharing through secure channels, defence-industry cooperation and joint production, and a committee of their defence ministers and chiefs of staff. The secretariat will sit in Saudi Arabia, with retired Pakistani General Nauman Mahmood named as its first secretary-general for a three-year term. Under Article 16 the agreement stays in force indefinitely, with no party allowed to withdraw for the first 10 years and three months' written notice required after that. It also leaves the door open for other countries to join with the existing members' approval — Syria's government has already expressed interest.

The two sides of the argument are about what ratification actually changes. Critics, including some in Turkey's own parliament — the opposition CHP backed it, but the İYİ Party, the DEM Party and the YENİ Party voted against — have raised the obvious tension: Turkey is a NATO member, and committing its military to a second collective-defence pact raises questions about where its forces and loyalties go if the two obligations ever clash. Turkish officials have dismissed that concern as unfounded, and the pact's own text is written to blunt it: the collective-defence clause is not automatic, decisions are taken by consensus after consultation, and each party acts within its own constitutional obligations. Defence Minister Yaşar Güler said in October that Turkish support would be about "strengthening deterrence," adding: "We absolutely do not foresee entering into any conflict with the Houthis." Under Article 92 of Turkey's constitution, sending troops abroad still needs a separate parliamentary vote, and the AK Party's Fuat Oktay stressed during the debate that ratification does not mean troops are sent automatically.

That matters because the other two members have already drawn their own lines. Pakistan has refused to join strikes inside Yemen, and Egypt — whose parliament approved sending military support in October — says any mission would be defensive only. The US has so far declined to offer direct support against the Houthis after its own weekslong campaign against the group in 2025. So Riyadh now has a ratified treaty with two partners, but neither partner has committed to fight on the ground in Yemen, and the Houthis are still about 50 miles from Aden, closer than at any time since 2015.

For ordinary people, the pact lands in the middle of a war that is already disrupting daily life in the kingdom. The Houthis attacked Saudi targets almost every day in October, hitting civilian airports and Aramco facilities, and the conflict monitor Acled recorded 26 incidents in the kingdom in recent days alone. Riyadh's King Khalid airport has been repeatedly hit and partly closed, airlines have cancelled flights for weeks, and on Thursday Saudi Arabia issued alerts warning of potential danger in Yanbu, the Red Sea oil export hub. Inside Yemen, fighting since July has killed and wounded thousands and displaced more than 250,000 people, according to the UN.

In short: Turkey has now ratified the Mecca pact, 279–119, giving Saudi Arabia a treaty that says an attack on one member is an attack on all — but the clause is not automatic, troops need a separate vote, and neither Turkey nor Pakistan has agreed to fight in Yemen. It is a deterrent on paper while the Houthis keep firing and Riyadh keeps waiting for an ally willing to act.

Ukraine: Russia's energy campaign is running at a pace unseen all war

Ukraine's largest energy company DTEK says Russian forces struck Ukrainian power plants and electricity transmission facilities 78 times since around November 2 — compared with 11 such strikes in all of 2022 — according to the Institute for the Study of War's November 5 assessment. Three DTEK power plants suspended operations after the November 3 strikes, and Kyiv's water outage that day was the most serious since the war began. The November 4–5 overnight barrage itself — 128 drones (65 of them jet-powered) and two Kh-35 missiles — killed a woman in the Sumy region and injured several people elsewhere, including one man at a logistics facility fire in Sofiivska Borshchahivka outside Kyiv; air defences downed or suppressed 105 of the drones. No new overnight barrage has been reported yet for November 5–6. Sources · Kyiv Post via Mastodon

Poland: von der Leyen publicly snubs Nawrocki at Visegrad summit

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen skipped a joint press conference with Polish President Karol Nawrocki at the Visegrad Group presidential summit in Trenčín, Slovakia, Bloomberg reported on Thursday — a public show of support for Prime Minister Donald Tusk in the Constitutional Tribunal crisis. Von der Leyen reportedly considered boycotting the summit altogether before deciding that would attract more attention. Meanwhile Tusk's coalition partner PSL has proposed a constitutional reset to resolve the standoff, and European Parliament President Roberta Metsola is due in Warsaw in the coming days for separate meetings with both Nawrocki and Tusk. The Tusk–Nawrocki one-to-one meeting still has no date. Source

Tech: Google unveils a code-security agent and a borderless data lakehouse

Google CodeMender: Google unveiled an autonomous code-security agent at its Cloud Security Summit on Thursday — an agent that reviews code as autonomous coding agents commit it faster than humans can keep up. It is part of Google's pitch for securing software development in the agentic era, where the review bottleneck is the problem, not the writing. Source

Google Agentic Data Cloud: Google also announced a borderless Lakehouse built on the open Apache Iceberg format, letting enterprises query data across multi-cloud, on-premises and SaaS systems without moving it or paying variable egress fees — aimed at running AI agents at scale over data where it already lives. Cyber: update Chrome — a sandbox-escape flaw has exploitation reports, and OFBiz has a critical unauthenticated RCE

Chrome 157 fixes 213 flaws, including 7 critical. Google's Chrome 157 stable release (157.0.8103.38/.39) fixes 213 vulnerabilities, seven of them critical. Separately, in-the-wild exploitation has been reported for CVE-2026-119264, a use-after-free in Dawn (Chrome's WebGPU implementation), CVSS 8.8, that could let a crafted page run code outside the sandbox — the report is logged by BSI CERT-Bund, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security and Malwarebytes Labs, though Google itself reported no known in-the-wild exploitation for the 157 batch. Update Chrome (and Edge) and make sure the browser restarts. Sources · Chrome 157 advisory roundup

Apache OFBiz critical code injection (CVE-2026-83542). A remote unauthenticated attacker can execute code on OFBiz 24.09 before 24.09.08 by staging crafted widget XML through a multipart upload. No exploitation has been recorded, but two earlier OFBiz CVEs are already in CISA's KEV catalog — patch if you run it. Source

No new CISA KEV additions on November 5. The catalog stands at 1,768 entries. The WordPress CVE-2026-119977 federal patch deadline is tomorrow, November 6 — update to WordPress 7.1.4. Source

Binance Recovery Update #8: Binance restored withdrawals for 62 tokens (69 network-asset pairs) from 04:00 UTC on November 5, and says replenishment is still at about 98% of the ~$6.3B stolen. The 44-token zero-knowledge proof-of-reserves snapshot from November 4 is still computing — due around November 6–7 — so the 98% figure remains Binance's own claim until the proof lands. Elliptic's November 5 count has $3.35B of the loot still sitting in attacker-linked addresses (up from $3.21B on November 4, because its address set changed) and $2.85B frozen or seized — single-vendor figures, treat them as one tracker, not a settled total.
