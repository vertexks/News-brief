Putin agrees "in principle" to trilateral talks after Nov. 10 — but not at the leaders' table

The readout from the Witkoff–Putin meeting finally landed this afternoon: Kremlin foreign-policy adviser Yuri Ushakov said Putin agreed "in principle" that trilateral US–Russia–Ukraine talks will continue after Nov. 10, focused on "building a security system for Russia and Ukraine after the conflict ends." A US official added the talks would happen "at the technical level" — meaning Putin himself won't sit down with Trump or Zelensky. Both sides accepted the trilateral format (Ukraine in Oslo Tuesday, Russia in St. Petersburg today), and the US readout cited "progress" on a post-war economic plan and European security architecture. The catch, per CNN: no indication Russia has budged on its maximalist positions — full Donbas, a shrunken Ukrainian military, no NATO. Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev separately said the US–Russia diesel deal goes ahead despite European criticism. CNN · NY Post
